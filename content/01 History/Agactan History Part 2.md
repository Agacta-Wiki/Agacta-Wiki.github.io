# Agactan History

Agacta's political history includes its unification around 1200, its incorporation into Sensin's Kingdom of Kingdoms in 1608, and its emergence as an independent republic in 1811. The struggle over independence involved three distinct positions: continued union with Sensin, an independent republic, and an independent Agactan monarchy. The republican settlement prevailed, although rival monarchist movements survived through the nineteenth century before becoming marginal by the early twentieth century.

## Unification and incorporation into Sensin

Agacta was unified by approximately 1200. Its neighbours included Sensin to the south, Alaram to the southwest, and Luum to the north and northwest.

Following the famine of 1606, Lefelio bent the knee in Lojburg in 1608. Agacta consequently became the thirteenth constituent kingdom of Sensin's Kingdom of Kingdoms. The arrangement was autonomous and federal, allowing Agacta a degree of self-government within the larger monarchy.

A settlement in 1692 restored most borders but did not restore Agacta's independence. Agacta consequently regarded itself as the principal loser of the settlement.

## The three-way struggle

The dispute over Agacta's future concerned both its relationship with Sensin and the form of government it should have. Opposition to Sensin did not necessarily imply support for a republic, while support for monarchy did not necessarily imply loyalty to Sensin.

| Faction | Position on independence | Preferred government |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Republicans | Independence from Sensin | An Agactan republic |
| Independent royalists, or Laltists | Independence from Sensin | A separate Agactan monarchy |
| Pro-Sensin royalists, or unionists | Continued membership of Sensin; restoration of the union after 1811 | The pre-independence monarchy within Sensin's Kingdom of Kingdoms |

### Republicans

The republicans sought independence from Sensin and the establishment of a republic. Following independence, they became the largest political camp and supported the preservation of the republic, with a president as head of state.

### Independent royalists

The independent royalists sought an Agactan monarchy separate from Sensin. Their movement was associated with Lalt and became known as Laltism. Lalt led the movement from exile in Luum and was subsequently killed there for treason. His descendants continued to advance royal claims and participate in royalist plots.

Lalt is referred to as both Prince Lalt and Duke Lalt. The working explanation is that he began life as a prince and later received a dukedom; the date and circumstances of that grant have not yet been established.

As Lalt's cause faltered, royalists also considered princes and dukes from other Alturian dynasties who possessed Agactan royal ancestry.

### Pro-Sensin royalists

The unionist royalists opposed independence and supported Agacta's place within Sensin's Kingdom of Kingdoms. After independence, they sought to restore the earlier monarchy and return Agacta to Sensin.

This became the smallest of the three political camps and received funding from Sensin. The former royal family served as Sensin's Wardens of Savia.

## Norbert's renunciation

Norbert, a direct descendant of Lalt, renounced his claim to the throne and joined the republicans, helping to unite the independence movement. His renunciation did not, however, bring all independent royalism to an end.

Across much of Altur, royal succession remained a subject of interest even in countries that had become republics. Royalists traced royal lines to identify potential sovereigns. If the first person in line declined the throne, supporters could move further down the succession until they found someone willing to accept the claim.

Norbert's decision therefore ended his own pursuit of the throne without preventing other claimants from being promoted. The continuing existence of Laltist pretenders was compatible with his acceptance of republicanism.

## Independence in 1811

Agacta became independent from Sensin in 1811 as a republic. Independence settled the country's formal relationship with Sensin, but competing visions of its government persisted.

Republicans defended the new political order. Laltists continued to seek an independent monarchy, while pro-Sensin monarchists advocated the restoration of the union. These were distinct causes: an independent royalist victory would not have fulfilled the pro-Sensin faction's objective.

## Royalist succession disputes

In 1867, Jasper, styled "King Jasper" by the Laltists, died without an heir. His death produced competing claims associated with Shadrak, Vinsent and Levio. Levio challenged Vinsent's legitimacy, alleging that he was illegitimate.

By the late 1880s, Shadrak, Vinsent's son Molto, and eventually Levio had accepted the republic. The pro-Sensin faction had become almost invisible.

Royalism nevertheless continued on an increasingly marginal basis. Supporters could seek further claimants through the royal lines whenever an existing claimant withdrew or accepted the republic. Successive so-called kings became less important as the movement's following dwindled.

By the early twentieth century, royalism was a niche interest with almost no adherents remaining in Agacta.

## Development of republican parties

The republican movement later divided into Constitution and Liberal factions, which continued as political parties. The Socialists subsequently emerged from a split within the Liberals.
