# 1981 Agactan general election

The **1981 Agactan general election** was held on **___ 1981** to elect the 240 members of the [[Parliament of Agacta]].

The election resulted in a change of government after the [[Constitution Party]] and [[Liberal Party]] agreed to form a coalition. Although the Constitution Party did not increase its parliamentary representation, losses suffered by the governing [[Socialist Party]] enabled the centre-right coalition to return to office.

---

# Background

The election followed four years of Socialist-Liberal coalition government. Despite relative political stability, the governing parties entered the campaign facing growing public concern over the economy and the future direction of the country.

The Constitution Party sought to return to government by presenting itself as a stable alternative, while the Liberal Party once again positioned itself as the moderate force capable of working with either of the two major parties.

# Government formation

Although the Socialist Party remained the largest party in Parliament, it was unable to secure enough support to remain in government.

Following coalition negotiations, the Constitution Party and Liberal Party agreed to form a government, commanding a combined majority of **124 seats**.

The President subsequently invited the leader of the Constitution Party to form the next government.

---

# Analysis

The election marked another significant step in the transformation of Agactan politics from one-party dominance to a competitive coalition system.

While the Constitution Party's representation remained unchanged, the Liberal Party achieved substantial gains and once again emerged as the decisive kingmaker. The Socialist Party continued its gradual electoral decline, losing fourteen seats and, for the second time in twelve years, surrendering government despite remaining the largest parliamentary party.

The Communist Party also recorded modest gains, continuing its gradual rise during the late 1970s and early 1980s.

---

# Legacy

The 1981 election reinforced the central role of coalition politics within Agacta's parliamentary system.

It further cemented the Liberal Party's reputation as the party that most often determined the composition of government, while demonstrating that the largest party was not always guaranteed to remain in office if an alternative parliamentary majority could be assembled.
