# 1993 Agactan general election

The **1993 Agactan general election** was held on **___ 1993** to elect the 240 members of the [[Parliament of Agacta]].

Although the [[Socialist Party]] remained the largest party in Parliament, it lost its parliamentary majority. Following coalition negotiations, the [[Constitution Party]] and [[Liberal Party]] agreed to form a government, returning the centre-right coalition to office.

The election was the first to take place following the collapse of the [[Communist Party]], whose absence fundamentally reshaped Agactan politics.

---

# Background

The election followed four years of Socialist majority government.

During the previous parliamentary term, the Communist Party collapsed amid the wider international decline of communism. Many of its former members joined other political movements or retired from politics, while its remaining support fragmented across the political spectrum.

The campaign focused on the economy, taxation, public services and the future direction of political reform during the post-Cold War era.

# Government formation

Although the Socialist Party remained the largest party, it no longer commanded a parliamentary majority.

Following coalition negotiations, the Constitution Party and Liberal Party agreed to form a government with a combined total of **122 seats**.

The President subsequently invited the leader of the Constitution Party to form the next government.

---

# Analysis

The election produced a more balanced Parliament than in 1989.

Both the Socialist Party and Constitution Party lost seats, while the Liberal Party recorded one of the strongest recoveries in its history, once again becoming the decisive parliamentary kingmaker.

The disappearance of the Communist Party also marked the end of the traditional four-party system that had characterised Agactan politics for much of the post-war period.

---

# Legacy

The 1993 election marked the beginning of a new era in Agactan politics following the end of the Cold War.

The collapse of the Communist Party permanently altered the party system, while the Liberal Party's resurgence reaffirmed its long-standing role as the country's principal coalition partner.

The election also paved the way for new political movements to emerge during the 1990s, most notably the [[Green Party]], which would contest its first general election four years later.
