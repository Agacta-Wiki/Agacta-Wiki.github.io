# 1985 Agactan general election

The **1985 Agactan general election** was held on **___ 1985** to elect the 240 members of the [[Parliament of Agacta]].

The election resulted in the [[Socialist Party]] remaining the largest party in Parliament. Although it fell short of an overall majority, the [[Liberal Party]] agreed to renew its coalition with the Socialists, allowing the government to remain in office for a further term.

---

# Background

The election followed four years of Constitution Party-Liberal coalition government. As the campaign progressed, debate centred on the economy, public services and taxation, with all four parliamentary parties presenting differing visions for Agacta's future.

The Socialist Party sought to return to government by highlighting its record in office and promising renewed investment in housing, infrastructure and public services. The Constitution Party argued that continued fiscal discipline and support for private enterprise remained the best path to sustained economic growth.

# Government formation

The Socialist Party remained the largest party but fell short of an overall majority.

Following coalition negotiations, the Socialist Party and Liberal Party agreed to form a government with a combined total of **146 seats**.

The President subsequently invited the leader of the Socialist Party to form the next government.

---

# Analysis

The election saw a modest recovery for the Socialist Party after its losses in 1981, allowing it to return to government with Liberal support.

The Constitution Party also improved its parliamentary representation but remained unable to assemble a governing majority.

Although the Liberal Party lost seats, it once again held the balance of power and continued its long-established role as Agacta's principal coalition partner. The Communist Party suffered a slight decline but retained a parliamentary presence.

---

# Legacy

The 1985 election further demonstrated the stability of Agacta's coalition politics. By the mid-1980s it had become widely accepted that governments would often depend on Liberal support, reinforcing the party's reputation as the country's political kingmaker.

The election would also prove to be the last in which the Communist Party maintained a significant parliamentary presence before the dramatic political changes that followed the collapse of communism in Europe later in the decade.
