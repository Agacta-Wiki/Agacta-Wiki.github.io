# 2009 Agactan general election

The **2009 Agactan general election** was held on **___ 2009** to elect the 240 members of the [[Parliament of Agacta]].

The election resulted in the [[Socialist Party]] losing its parliamentary majority after twelve years in government. Although it remained the largest party, it fell well short of an outright majority and subsequently formed a coalition government with the [[Green Party]].

The election also marked the parliamentary debut of the [[Agacta Left Bloc]] (AMB), which entered Parliament with nine seats.

---

# Background

The election followed four years of Socialist majority government.

The governing Socialists entered the campaign after a prolonged period in office, facing increased pressure from both the Constitution Party and newer political movements. Economic uncertainty and growing debate over public spending, environmental policy and social inequality contributed to a more fragmented political landscape.

The election also saw the newly established Agacta Left Bloc contest its first general election, seeking to occupy political space left vacant by the collapse of the Communist Party two decades earlier.

# Government formation

Despite suffering significant losses, the Socialist Party remained the largest party in Parliament.

Following coalition negotiations, the Socialist Party and Green Party agreed to form a government with a combined total of **127 seats**, giving the coalition a narrow parliamentary majority.

The President subsequently invited the leader of the Socialist Party to form the next government.

---

# Analysis

The election marked the end of the Socialist Party's era of majority government.

Although it remained the largest party, the loss of twenty-one seats forced it into coalition with the Green Party for the first time.

The Constitution Party continued its recovery, gaining eighteen seats and moving closer to government, while the Liberal Party suffered another disappointing election after losing seven seats.

The principal story of the election, however, was the debut of the Agacta Left Bloc. Winning nine seats in its first contest, the party quickly established itself as a significant force on the left of Agactan politics, attracting voters seeking a more radical alternative to the Socialists.

---

# Legacy

The 2009 election marked the beginning of a new five-party era in Agactan politics.

It was the first election contested by the Agacta Left Bloc and the first to produce a Socialist-Green coalition government. The result also demonstrated the increasing fragmentation of the party system, with support becoming more evenly distributed among a growing number of parliamentary parties.
