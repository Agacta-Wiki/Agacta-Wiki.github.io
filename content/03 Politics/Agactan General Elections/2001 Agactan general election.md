# 2001 Agactan general election

The **2001 Agactan general election** was held on **___ 2001** to elect the 240 members of the [[Parliament of Agacta]].

The election resulted in a landslide victory for the [[Socialist Party]], which secured one of the largest parliamentary majorities in the history of the modern republic. The [[Constitution Party]] suffered a historic defeat, while both the [[Liberal Party]] and [[Green Party]] increased their parliamentary representation.

---

# Background

The election followed four years of Socialist majority government.

During the late 1990s, Agacta experienced continued economic growth and political stability. The governing Socialist Party entered the campaign seeking a renewed mandate, while the Constitution Party attempted to recover from its defeat in 1997.

The Green Party also sought to build upon its parliamentary breakthrough four years earlier.

# Government formation

The Socialist Party secured an overall majority of **148 seats**, allowing it to form a government without coalition partners.

The President subsequently invited the leader of the Socialist Party to form the next government.

---

# Analysis

The election produced one of the most decisive victories in modern Agactan political history.

The Socialist Party substantially increased its parliamentary majority, benefiting from strong public confidence in the government's management of the economy and public services.

The Constitution Party suffered a historic defeat, losing thirty-five seats and recording its weakest electoral performance since the establishment of the modern republic.

The Liberal Party recovered from its poor performance in 1997, while the Green Party more than doubled its parliamentary representation, confirming its emergence as a permanent feature of Agactan politics.

---

# Legacy

The 2001 election represented the high-water mark of Socialist support in the early twenty-first century.

It also confirmed the Green Party's continued growth, while the Constitution Party began a period of internal reflection and organisational reform following its worst electoral defeat in decades.
