# Politics of Agacta

## Overview

Agacta is a parliamentary republic governed under the Constitution of 1952. Executive authority is exercised by the Prime Minister and Cabinet, who are responsible to the 240-member Parliament of Agacta. The President serves as head of state in a largely ceremonial capacity, while retaining important constitutional responsibilities relating to government formation and parliamentary stability.

Agacta operates a multiparty political system historically dominated by the centre-left Socialist Party and centre-right Constitution Party. The centrist Liberal Party has frequently held the balance of power and participated in coalition governments with both major parties. More recently, the Green Party and Agacta Left Bloc have emerged as significant parliamentary forces.

Parliamentary elections are held every four years using a distinctive mixed-member proportional electoral system which prioritises directly elected representatives while accepting some deviation from full proportionality.

Presidential elections are also held every four years, midway between parliamentary elections.

Local government operates through county, town and city councils, while Agacta's four overseas territories enjoy substantial autonomy through their own elected parliaments.

The country's electoral calendar is deliberately staggered, with different counties and overseas territories holding elections at different points in a four-year cycle.

# Constitutional System

## The Republic

Agacta is a parliamentary republic in which the President is head of state and the Prime Minister is head of government.

The President performs ceremonial and constitutional functions, while day-to-day executive authority rests with the Prime Minister and Cabinet.

Although the presidency is largely ceremonial, the office plays an important role in ensuring continuity of government.

The President appoints the individual considered most likely to form a viable government following a parliamentary election or the collapse of an existing administration.

Agactan political tradition strongly favours the formation of functioning governments within the existing Parliament rather than calling early elections.

## Parliament of Agacta

The Parliament of Agacta is a unicameral legislature consisting of 240 Members of Parliament (MPs).

Parliament is responsible for:

- Passing national legislation.
- Approving the national budget.
- Scrutinising the Prime Minister and Cabinet.
- Holding the Government accountable.
- Expressing confidence or no confidence in the Government.

Members are elected every four years.

The parliamentary electoral calendar has remained regular since the first elections under the 1952 Constitution, held in 1953.

Since 1998, Parliament has met in the Ana Nlustro Agactan Parliament Complex in Treslio.

# Parliamentary Electoral System

## Mixed-Member Proportional Representation

Agacta operates a county-based mixed-member proportional (MMP) electoral system.

The country is divided into 19 parliamentary constituencies, corresponding to its counties, including the four overseas territories.

Parliamentary seats are distributed between counties according to population.

Each county elects half its parliamentary representatives through single-member sub-constituencies using first-past-the-post voting.

The remaining seats are initially allocated through compensatory county party lists using the D'Hondt method.

An electoral threshold of 5% applies.

The system differs from more strictly proportional versions of MMP because directly elected MPs are guaranteed to retain their seats, even when their party wins more sub-constituencies than its proportional entitlement would otherwise permit.

## Constituency Primacy

Agacta's electoral system is designed around three principles:

1. Protecting directly elected parliamentary mandates.
2. Maintaining a fixed Parliament of 240 members.
3. Achieving proportional representation as far as the first
   two requirements allow.

Unlike systems which create overhang and balancing seats, Agacta adjusts the proportional allocation to accommodate directly elected MPs.

Where a party wins more sub-constituency seats than its proportional entitlement, the number of seats used in the D'Hondt calculation is reduced.

The calculation is repeated as necessary until the party-list allocation and additional protected direct mandates together equal the county's prescribed number of parliamentary seats.

For example, a county entitled to 12 parliamentary seats would ordinarily elect six MPs directly and allocate the remaining six seats through the compensatory list system.

If a party wins five sub-constituencies despite having a proportional entitlement of only four seats, its additional directly elected MP is protected.

The D'Hondt calculation is reduced to accommodate that mandate without increasing the county's overall representation.

Further reductions can be made if necessary, including where more than one party has won additional direct mandates.

This can produce results which are less proportional than the original D'Hondt distribution.

Such disproportionality is an intentional feature of the Agactan system rather than an error requiring additional parliamentary seats.

## County-Wide Representation

A distinctive principle of Agactan parliamentary representation is that every MP represents their entire county, regardless of whether they were elected directly or through a party list.

An MP elected in Treslio Sub-Constituency 5 therefore represents all of Treslio, rather than exclusively representing the fifth sub-constituency.

The principal purpose of sub-constituencies is to ensure that every geographical part of a county elects at least one direct parliamentary representative.

Following an election, political parties distribute constituency responsibilities among their elected MPs.

These arrangements generally follow geography as closely as possible, with different MPs responsible for clinics, constituency enquiries and other local parliamentary work.

A directly elected MP retains responsibility for the sub-constituency in which they won election.

Other areas may be assigned to the party's remaining MPs, including those elected through the county list.

This means that voters whose preferred party loses their particular sub-constituency may nevertheless receive representation from that party's other county MPs.


## Party Lists

Candidates contesting individual sub-constituencies may also appear on their party's county list.

Consequently, losing a direct election does not necessarily prevent a candidate from entering Parliament.

A candidate may still be elected through their party's list allocation.

However, party lists do not guarantee parliamentary seats for unsuccessful direct candidates.

Where a party's entire parliamentary entitlement is occupied by directly elected MPs, it receives no list seats.

This can occasionally result in prominent politicians losing their seats despite occupying high positions on their party lists.

### Semi-Open Party Lists

Agacta uses a semi-open party-list system, allowing voters to influence which candidates receive list seats while retaining an initial list order established by each party.

Voters may cast their list vote for a political party as a whole or express a preference for an individual candidate appearing on that party's list.

Votes cast for individual candidates also count towards their party's total vote for the proportional allocation.

A candidate receiving more than 12% of their party's total vote within the county can move ahead of candidates placed above them on the original party list.

Candidates who do not reach this threshold remain subject to the party's established list order.

This arrangement allows voters to influence the selection of individual MPs without completely replacing the party's original ordering of candidates.

## Independent Candidates

Independent candidates may contest individual sub-constituencies or submit their own electoral lists.

An independent may also submit a list containing only their own name.

Independent parliamentary representation is uncommon in Agacta.

Candidates without established party organisations generally face the challenge of either winning a first-past-the-post sub-constituency or securing enough list votes to obtain representation under the proportional allocation.

## Parliamentary Vacancies

Vacancies arising from the departure of directly elected MPs are filled through by-elections.

Vacancies involving list-elected MPs are filled by the next eligible candidate on the relevant party list.

## Electoral Philosophy

Agacta openly accepts that its parliamentary electoral system does not produce perfect proportionality.

The system reflects a preference for strong local representation and fixed parliamentary membership over complete mathematical proportionality.

Agactan political culture places considerable importance on the principle that a candidate directly elected by local voters should not subsequently lose that mandate through proportional calculations.

The system is commonly understood as a compromise between the disproportionality of pure first-past-the-post systems and the greater mathematical proportionality pursued by other forms of proportional representation.

# The Presidency

## Role of the President

The President of Agacta is the country's head of state.

The office is largely ceremonial, with executive authority exercised by the Prime Minister and Cabinet.

However, the President has important constitutional responsibilities relating to the appointment of governments and the resolution of parliamentary crises.

Following parliamentary elections, the President invites the person considered most likely to form a viable government to do so.

When a government loses parliamentary confidence, the President seeks to facilitate negotiations leading to an alternative administration.

The President is also head of state of Agacta's overseas autonomous territories and represents them internationally.

The President's official residence is Treslio Palace.

## Presidential Elections

The President is directly elected by the people of Agacta every four years.

Presidential elections take place two years after parliamentary general elections.

A President may serve a maximum of three terms, each lasting four years.

Agacta uses a conventional two-round presidential electoral system.

If a candidate receives an absolute majority of valid votes in the first round, that candidate is elected President.

If no candidate obtains an absolute majority, the two highest-placed candidates advance to a second-round runoff.

The candidate receiving a majority of valid votes in the second round is elected.

The presidential electoral cycle is therefore separate from the parliamentary electoral cycle.

For example, parliamentary elections take place in 2025 and 2029, while presidential elections take place in 2027 and 2031.

## Presidential Nominations

Presidential candidates may qualify for the ballot through either a political party nomination or a popular signature petition.

### Political Party Nominations

A political party may nominate a presidential candidate if it meets at least one of the following conditions:

- It holds at least one seat in the Parliament of Agacta.

- It holds elected local council representation in at least five different Agactan counties.

The second condition refers to geographical counties rather than individual local councils.

For example, representation on Treslio County Council, Treslio City Council, Yojo Town Council and Zogyo Town Council counts as representation in one county: Treslio.

A party must have representation in five different counties to qualify through the local-government route.

A candidate must win the qualifying party's presidential nomination process.

An uncontested nomination is considered valid.

The major political parties select their presidential candidates through postal ballots of their membership.

### Popular Nominations

A candidate may alternatively qualify by collecting 20,000 signatures from eligible voters.

Signatures may be collected anywhere in Agacta, without a requirement for geographical distribution among counties.

This provides a route onto the presidential ballot for independent candidates and others without a qualifying party nomination.

## Multiple Nominations and Endorsements

A presidential candidate may seek and win the nominations of more than one political party.

However, they must satisfy each party's own nomination procedures.

A candidate receiving multiple nominations appears only once on the presidential ballot.

In practice, formal multiple-party nominations are uncommon.

Political parties may instead choose to endorse another party's candidate or an independent candidate without formally nominating that person as their own.

For example, before a presidential election, members of the Liberal Party vote on whether the party should field its own candidate.

If members vote against contesting the election, the party may instead endorse another candidate, potentially the Constitution Party's nominee.

Such an endorsement does not make the individual the Liberal Party's presidential candidate.

Independent or national-unity candidates may also receive endorsements from several parties while continuing to stand as independents.

# Government Formation and Parliamentary Confidence

## Appointment of the Prime Minister

Following a parliamentary election, the President invites the individual considered most likely to form a viable administration to do so.

This does not necessarily mean appointing the leader of the party which won the most seats.

A party finishing second may form a government if it can obtain sufficient parliamentary support through agreements with other parties.

This has occurred several times in Agactan political history.

## Coalition Governments

Coalition governments are a longstanding feature of Agactan parliamentary politics.

The Liberal Party has frequently occupied a pivotal position between the Socialist Party and Constitution Party.

It has participated in governments led by both parties.

The Constitution Party and Socialist Party, despite their relatively moderate positions within Agactan politics, have never formed a coalition government together.

Their longstanding rivalry remains an important political tradition.

## Minority Governments

Minority governments are permitted under Agacta's parliamentary system.

A government does not necessarily need to consist of parties holding an outright majority of parliamentary seats.

It may govern with sufficient support or parliamentary tolerance to remain in office.

## Votes of No Confidence

A government may be dismissed by a majority vote of no confidence in Parliament.

However, the loss of parliamentary confidence does not automatically trigger a general election.

Instead, the President seeks to establish whether another administration can be formed from the existing Parliament.

The President may bring political representatives together to negotiate a compromise government.

Technocrats frequently play an important role in attempts to establish such compromise administrations.

## Early Parliamentary Elections

Early parliamentary elections are constitutionally possible but have never occurred in modern Agactan history.

The established preference is to find an alternative government capable of continuing until the next scheduled parliamentary election.

This tradition has helped maintain the regular four-year parliamentary electoral cycle.

# Local Government

## Structure

Agacta operates a predominantly single-tier system of local government.

Local administration is carried out by county councils and independent town or city councils.

These authorities exercise equivalent local-government responsibilities within their respective jurisdictions.

Unlike a conventional two-tier system, residents are not simultaneously governed by a county council and a separate municipal council.

Instead, each area falls under one ordinary local authority.

## County Councils

County councils administer rural areas and smaller towns which do not fall within an independent town or city council's territory.

For example, Treslio County Council administers rural Treslio and smaller settlements without their own independent municipal authorities.

However, larger settlements such as Treslio Capital, Yojo and Zogyo have their own councils.

Treslio County Council does not administer these independent municipal territories.

## Town and City Councils

Larger towns and cities generally have their own elected councils.

A population of approximately 20,000 is the usual guideline for independent town or city councils.

Town and city councils exercise the same basic responsibilities as county councils.

The distinction is geographical rather than hierarchical.

Their administrative territories are not necessarily limited to the built-up town or city.

A town council may also administer surrounding countryside and smaller settlements within its boundaries.

This resembles the geographical structure of municipalities in countries such as Spain, where municipal territories can extend considerably beyond the principal settlement.

## Creation of New Councils

New independent town or city councils can only be established through an Act of the Agactan Parliament.

The affected population will often be consulted through a local vote on whether the area should separate from county council administration and establish its own local authority.

Passing the population guideline does not, by itself, automatically create a new council.

## Local Elections

Local councillors are elected every four years.

Agacta uses the same basic mixed-member proportional system for local elections as for national parliamentary elections.

Each local authority has its own party-list electorate covering the territory it administers.

Directly elected councillors are chosen from smaller sub-constituencies.

These commonly follow the boundaries of national parliamentary sub-constituencies, although they are normally divided further.

For example, Treslio parliamentary Sub-Constituency 2 might be divided into local electoral areas 2A, 2B and 2C.

Local elections retain the combination of direct representation and compensatory party-list seats used nationally.

All councils within a particular county hold their elections simultaneously.

Residents vote for the council responsible for their area, rather than electing both county and municipal councillors.

## Staggered Local Elections

Local elections are staggered geographically across a four-year cycle.

Each year, counties from the South, East, West, North and Overseas groupings hold elections according to a fixed schedule.

Because the Northern grouping contains only three counties, there is no Northern county election in the first year of the cycle.

The electoral schedule is:

| Region | 2025 | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| South | Bantro | Nleto | Stototrip | Tarbo |
| East | Merino | Mlaxa | Orientoport | Treslio |
| West | Abeldrip | Nlastromund | Riala | Setroburg |
| North | — | Kalisto | Montanomund | Tleve |
| Overseas | Aquatrip | Savtrio Islands | Tlelmund | Ulos |

The cycle repeats every four years.

Consequently, Agacta holds local elections somewhere in the country every year.

All local authorities within the county scheduled to vote hold their elections during that county's assigned election year.

# Overseas Autonomous Territories

## Constitutional Status

Agacta has four overseas autonomous territories:

- Aquatrip
- Savtrio Islands
- Tlelmund
- Ulos

Unlike mainland counties, the overseas territories possess an additional level of government through their own autonomous parliaments.

These institutions exercise substantial devolved legislative and executive powers.

The degree of autonomy is broadly comparable to arrangements found in Navarre in Spain, the Azores in Portugal, or South Tyrol in Italy.

The overseas territories remain part of Agacta, with representation in the national Parliament.

The President of Agacta serves as their head of state and represents them internationally.

## Powers of the Autonomous Governments

The autonomous governments exercise responsibilities which are generally retained by the national government in mainland Agacta.

Examples include substantial powers in areas such as:

- Education
- Healthcare
- Policing
- Taxation

These are examples rather than an exhaustive list of devolved responsibilities.

Finance and border security are partially devolved.

Defence remains the responsibility of the national government.

The Agactan government retains constitutional and governmental authority in areas not transferred to the autonomous territories.

## Autonomous Parliaments

Each overseas territory has its own elected autonomous parliament.

The autonomous parliaments exercise legislative responsibilities within the powers granted under their statutes of autonomy.

Their governments are headed by an office broadly equivalent to a First Minister.

The autonomous parliaments use the same general mixed-member proportional electoral system as the national Parliament.

Aquatrip, Tlelmund and Ulos use territory-wide party lists.

The Savtrio Islands use island-wide lists.

This reflects the distinctive geography of the Savtrio archipelago.

## Autonomous Parliamentary Elections

Autonomous parliamentary elections are held every four years.

As with local elections, the elections are deliberately staggered rather than taking place simultaneously in all four territories.

The established electoral cycle is:

| Year | Autonomous Parliamentary Elections |
|---|---|
| 2025 | None |
| 2026 | Tlelmund |
| 2027 | Savtrio Islands and Ulos |
| 2028 | Aquatrip |

This cycle repeats every four years.

Autonomous parliamentary elections are never held during an Agactan national parliamentary election year.

They also never coincide with ordinary local council elections within the same overseas territory.

These arrangements were agreed with the Agactan government when the territories' statutes of autonomy were established.

The intention was to distinguish autonomous parliamentary institutions from ordinary local government and from the national Parliament of Agacta.

The election calendar also seeks to avoid requiring overseas residents to make four separate trips to the ballot box during a four-year electoral cycle.

Presidential elections may coincide with autonomous parliamentary elections.

## Local Government in the Overseas Territories

The overseas territories also maintain ordinary local-government institutions.

These operate separately from their autonomous parliaments.

Local government follows the same broad principles as mainland Agacta.

The Savtrio Islands have a particularly distinctive arrangement.

Individual islands may have their own local councils, but the Savtrio Islands collectively constitute one of Agacta's 19 counties.

This distinction is important for national parliamentary representation and the geographical qualification rules applying to presidential party nominations.

Representation on several Savtrio island councils still counts as representation within a single county.

## The West Lothian Question

Agacta has its own version of the constitutional issue commonly known as the West Lothian question.

MPs elected from the overseas territories participate fully in the national Parliament of Agacta.

They can therefore vote on national legislation concerning matters such as mainland education and healthcare, even though these subjects are substantially devolved within their own territories.

This creates an asymmetry between the legislative responsibilities affecting mainland constituents and those affecting constituents in the autonomous territories.

# National Electoral Calendar

Agacta's electoral arrangements combine regular parliamentary, presidential, local and autonomous elections.

National parliamentary and presidential elections alternate at two-year intervals.

Local elections are staggered by county, while autonomous parliamentary elections follow a separate schedule.

The four-year cycle can be summarised as follows:

| Year | National Election | Local Elections | Autonomous Elections |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | Parliamentary | Bantro, Merino, Abeldrip, Aquatrip | None |
| 2026 | None | Nleto, Mlaxa, Nlastromund, Kalisto, Savtrio Islands | Tlelmund |
| 2027 | Presidential | Stototrip, Orientoport, Riala, Montanomund, Tlelmund | Savtrio Islands, Ulos |
| 2028 | None | Tarbo, Treslio, Setroburg, Tleve, Ulos | Aquatrip |

The cycle repeats in subsequent years.

The staggered arrangements ensure that elections to different levels of government remain distinct while limiting the number of separate voting occasions required of residents.

# Political Parties

## Constitution Party

The Constitution Party is a centre-right political party founded during Agacta's nineteenth-century independence era.

Historically the country's second-largest political party, it has frequently formed coalition governments with the Liberal Party.

The Constitution Party formed governments following the elections of 1969, 1981 and 1993 despite the Socialist Party remaining the largest individual parliamentary party.

It became the largest party in Parliament following the 2013 general election.

Since then, the party has led four consecutive governments in coalition with the Liberals.

Although generally classified as centre-right, its policies are often comparatively centrist by international standards.

## Socialist Party

The Socialist Party is a centre-left political party founded during the 1920s.

For much of modern Agactan history it was the country's dominant political force and supplied most Prime Ministers.

The party dominated the early decades of the modern republic.

Although officially centre-left, it has frequently governed from the political centre.

The Socialists returned to power in 1997 and won outright parliamentary majorities in 1997, 2001 and 2005.

Following the 2009 election, they formed a coalition government with the Green Party.

The party lost power in 2013 and subsequently experienced a period of declining support.

It recovered strongly in the 2025 general election, regaining its position as Agacta's principal parliamentary opposition.

## Liberal Party

The Liberal Party occupies the political centre and has frequently held the balance of power between the Constitution and Socialist parties.

It has participated in coalition governments led by both parties.

Its preferred coalition partner has traditionally been the Constitution Party, although it has also governed with the Socialists.

Because of its frequent role as a junior coalition partner, the party is jokingly known as the "Party of the Deputy Prime Minister."

The Liberals have sometimes faced criticism for keeping their coalition options open.

During the 2001 general election, as a Constitution–Liberal government became increasingly unlikely, the party emphasised the importance of preventing an unchecked Socialist parliamentary majority.

This approach allowed the Liberals to appeal to voters who were broadly satisfied with the government but wanted an additional parliamentary counterweight.

Critics have accused the party of wanting to "hunt with the hounds and run with the hare."

## Green Party

The Green Party emerged as Agacta's principal environmentalist political party during the 1990s.

It entered Parliament for the first time in 1997 and expanded its representation over the following two decades.

The Greens entered government in coalition with the Socialist Party following the 2009 general election.

In 2021, the party overtook the Socialists and Liberals to become the Official Opposition.

However, it subsequently faced criticism over its opposition performance and increasing competition from other parties.

The Greens suffered significant losses in the 2025 election.

## Agacta Left Bloc

The Agacta Left Bloc (AMB) is a democratic socialist political alliance founded in 2009.

It emerged as a political force to the left of the traditional Socialist Party.

The party expanded its parliamentary representation following the COVID-19 pandemic and the subsequent inflation crisis.

It made substantial gains in the 2025 general election, consolidating its position among Agacta's principal parliamentary parties.

# Historical Development

## The Early Republic

The Constitution of 1952 established the modern framework of Agacta's parliamentary republic.

The first parliamentary election under the new constitution was held in 1953.

The Socialist Party dominated the early republic, winning four consecutive parliamentary majorities between 1953 and 1965.

The Constitution Party became its principal parliamentary rival, while the Liberal Party occupied the political centre.

## The Four-Party Era

For much of the twentieth century, Agactan parliamentary politics was dominated by four parties:

- Socialist Party
- Constitution Party
- Liberal Party
- Communist Party

The Socialists were generally the largest parliamentary party, while the Constitution Party was the principal alternative governing party.

The Liberals frequently held the balance of power.

The Communist Party maintained a smaller but established parliamentary presence.

## Coalition Politics

The 1969 election marked an important change in government.

Although the Socialist Party remained the largest individual party, the Constitution Party and Liberals formed a majority coalition.

The Socialists returned to power in 1973 and subsequently governed with the Liberals from 1977.

Further Constitution–Liberal coalitions were formed following the elections of 1981 and 1993.

The Liberal Party's repeated participation in governments led by either major party established its reputation as Agacta's principal parliamentary kingmaker.

## Decline of the Communist Party

Following the political changes of 1989, the Communist Party experienced a rapid decline and eventually disappeared from parliamentary politics.

The Green Party subsequently emerged as a significant political force, entering Parliament in 1997.

## Evolution of Party Lists

Agacta's party-list electoral system has undergone several reforms since the establishment of the modern republic.

Before 1989, parliamentary elections used closed party lists, with political parties determining the order in which their candidates would receive list seats.

Fully open party lists were introduced for the 1989 and 1993 general elections.

However, these elections produced numerous unexpected defeats for established politicians, as voters elevated candidates who had originally been placed lower on party lists.

These results became associated with the political phenomenon known as a "Portillo moment", in which a prominent politician unexpectedly loses their parliamentary seat.

Following these experiences, Agacta introduced party primaries in 1997 to give party members a greater role in establishing candidate lists.

Initially, political parties were permitted to choose their own methods of conducting these primaries.

In 2005, the Single Transferable Vote (STV) became the standard method for internal party primaries.

The current semi-open system retains party-established lists while allowing candidates receiving substantial personal support to move ahead of their original list positions.

## Socialist Government, 1997–2013

The Socialist Party returned to government following the 1997 general election.

It won three consecutive outright parliamentary majorities in 1997, 2001 and 2005.

The 2001 election took place during a period of strong economic performance, with the Socialists winning one of their strongest electoral victories.

By 2009, the political system had become more fragmented.

The Socialists lost their majority and formed a coalition with the Greens.

The newly founded Agacta Left Bloc also entered Parliament for the first time.

## Constitution-Led Governments, 2013–Present

The 2013 general election ended sixteen years of Socialist-led government.

For the first time, the Constitution Party became the largest individual parliamentary party.

It formed a coalition government with the Liberal Party.

The coalition retained office following the 2017 and 2021 general elections.

In 2021, the Green Party became the Official Opposition after overtaking the Socialist Party.

The 2025 election produced another significant political realignment.

The Socialists recovered strongly, while the Greens suffered substantial losses.

The Constitution Party remained the largest parliamentary party and renewed its coalition with the Liberals for a fourth consecutive term.

# Political Institutions and Residences

## Ana Nlustro Agactan Parliament Complex

The Ana Nlustro Agactan Parliament Complex has served as the seat of Parliament since 1998.

The decision to construct a new parliamentary complex was reached during the late 1980s, following longstanding concerns about the suitability of the former Capitol Building.

The new complex was intended to provide modern facilities for Parliament and its associated administration as Agacta approached the new millennium.

It was named in honour of Ana Nlustro, an Agactan suffragette who contributed to the development of voting rights.

The decision to name Parliament after Nlustro recognised her role in the democratic rights enjoyed by Agactan citizens.

## Agactan Capitol Building

Before 1998, Parliament met in the Agactan Capitol Building near the centre of Treslio.

The building was controversial for several reasons.

It had become inadequate for the needs of Parliament, with politicians and civil servants working in cramped offices.

Some parliamentary and government staff were accommodated in separate buildings away from the Capitol itself.

The building's central location also contributed to serious traffic difficulties.

More controversially, the Capitol had been constructed by the Sensinese and was viewed by some Agactans as a colonial relic.

Following Parliament's relocation in 1998, the Capitol was renovated and converted into the Agactan Politics Museum.

Some government offices remained in the building and were also refurbished.

## Treslio Palace

Treslio Palace is the official residence of the President of Agacta.

The building previously served as the residence of the Agactan royal family, which had adopted the name De Maro.

During the monarchy, the palace was colloquially known as "De Sensin Palace."

This reflected the royal family's enthusiasm for and pride in Agacta's association with Sensin's Kingdom of Kingdoms.

Following the end of the monarchy, the building became the presidential residence.

Today, it is known exclusively as Treslio Palace.

## House of Four Colours

The House of Four Colours is the official residence of the Prime Minister of Agacta.

The building contains four distinctively coloured areas or wings:

- White
- Yellow
- Sky blue
- Lavender

Originally, the entire building was painted lavender.

It was known as the Agactan Lavender House, sharing its name and original colour with the Sensinese Prime Minister's residence, Lavender House.

During the 1950s, sections of the Agactan building were repainted.

The resulting appearance led to the popular nickname "House of Four Colours."

The nickname subsequently became the building's official name.

# Contemporary Politics

Following the 2025 general election, the Constitution Party and Liberal Party remain in government.

The coalition has governed continuously since 2013.

The Socialist Party is the principal parliamentary opposition, following its recovery in the 2025 election.

The Green Party remains represented in Parliament despite the substantial losses it experienced in 2025.

The Agacta Left Bloc has increased its parliamentary presence and remains a significant political force to the left of the Socialists.

Agacta's political system continues to be characterised by coalition politics, county-based electoral representation, a largely ceremonial but constitutionally important presidency, and a preference for parliamentary continuity over early elections.

The country's overseas autonomous governments and staggered electoral calendar also form important parts of its constitutional and political structure.
