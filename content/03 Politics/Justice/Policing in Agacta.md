# Policing in Agacta

Policing in [[Agacta]] follows a model influenced by several European policing traditions. The mainland is served by a single unified national police force, while policing in the four overseas territories is devolved to separate territorial forces.

## Agactan National Police Force

The **Agactan National Police Force** is the principal police service of Agacta and is responsible for policing throughout the country's fifteen mainland provinces.

Although it is a unified national organisation, the force is divided into provincial subdivisions corresponding to each of the fifteen mainland provinces. These provide local administration and command while remaining components of the same national force rather than separate provincial police services.

The National Police also retains responsibility for **border security throughout Agacta**, including in the overseas territories. Border policing is therefore not among the policing powers devolved to the territorial governments.

The force has a reputation for being **efficient, precise and serious**. Officers are generally somewhat less informal or immediately approachable than members of [[An Garda Síochána]], for example, and the force can appear relatively militaristic to outsiders. However, there is a strong professional culture centred on taking the job seriously and assisting members of the public. Corruption is low and Agactans are generally satisfied with the police.

### Police culture

The National Police is an entirely professional civilian police service. Nevertheless, its institutional culture has been significantly influenced by Agacta's system of [[Conscription]].

Traditionally, almost every police officer would have completed conscription before joining the force. Many people who intended to pursue policing as a career also chose to extend their military service voluntarily to two or three years before entering police training.

As a result, military experience became commonplace among police recruits despite the police themselves being a civilian organisation. Elements of this background remain visible in the force's emphasis on discipline, precision and relatively formal relations between ranks.

## Uniform

The traditional uniform of the Agactan National Police Force consists of a **sky-blue shirt, navy trousers and navy cap, with a red tie and red trimmings**.

As with many European police services, the everyday uniform has become increasingly **practical and less formal** in recent decades. More functional clothing and equipment are now common during ordinary police duties, while the traditional uniform remains particularly prominent for formal and ceremonial occasions.

## Ranks

The standard rank structure of the Agactan National Police Force is:

1. **Cadet**
2. **Officer**
3. **Sergeant**
4. **Lieutenant**
5. **Captain**
6. **Deputy Commander**
7. **Commander**
8. **Deputy General**
9. **General**

The **General of Police** is the professional head of the Agactan National Police Force.

Because the National Police has responsibilities extending into border security and national security, the General holds one of the highest levels of security clearance in Agacta. The level of access is comparable to that held by the [[Prime Minister of Agacta]], members of the [[Cabinet of Agacta]] and other senior national-security officials.

The General is consequently often present at high-level national-security meetings rather than functioning solely as an administrative head of the police.

## Detectives

Detective policing is a **specialist career pathway within the police**, rather than a fundamentally separate non-uniformed branch.

Every prospective detective must first qualify and serve through the ordinary ranks of:

**Cadet → Officer**

After reaching Officer, a member of the force may apply to enter the detective pathway. Applicants must pass a **state examination** and successfully complete an **interview**. Successful candidates receive the rank of **Detective**.

The detective ranks are:

| Agactan rank             | Approximate UK equivalent |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| **Detective**            | Detective Constable       |
| **Detective Sergeant**   | Detective Sergeant        |
| **Detective Lieutenant** | Detective Inspector       |
| **Detective Captain**    | Detective Chief Inspector |

Above Detective Captain, the distinction disappears and officers progress into the ordinary senior ranks of **Deputy Commander, Commander, Deputy General and General**.

### Detective uniforms

Detectives normally wear civilian clothing while carrying out investigative duties. However, this is understood somewhat differently from the British concept of detectives belonging to a non-uniformed branch.

An Agactan detective technically wears the same police uniform appropriate to their equivalent standard rank. Their normal use of civilian clothing is regarded as a consequence of their **assignment**, rather than detectives inherently being non-uniformed police.

Detectives therefore wear their appropriate police uniform when circumstances require formal dress. This includes occasions such as [[Flag Day]] parades, official commemorations and ceremonies at which an officer receives an honour from a mayor, the [[President of Agacta]] or another public authority.

Culturally, an Agactan detective is therefore regarded as **a police officer whose normal assignment does not require uniform**, rather than as a member of a separate non-uniform branch.

## Overseas Territorial Police Forces

Each of Agacta's four [[Overseas Territories of Agacta|overseas territories]] maintains its own devolved police service:

- **[[Ulos Police Force]]**
- **[[Tlelmund Police Force]]**
- **[[Aquatrip Police Force]]**
- **[[Savtrio Islands Police Force]]**

Almost all ordinary policing responsibilities in the territories are devolved. The principal exception is **border security**, which remains the responsibility of the Agactan National Police Force.

### Ranks and authority

The territorial forces generally use the same rank structure as the National Police, including the senior titles of **Deputy Commander, Commander, Deputy General and General**.

However, identical rank titles do **not** imply equivalent national authority or security clearance.

The General of one of the territorial police forces is the senior professional police officer within that territory but does not possess the national-security status of the General of the Agactan National Police Force.

In terms of security access and institutional position, the head of a territorial force is more comparable to the Chief Constable of a regional British police service, such as Lincolnshire Police. Territorial police chiefs may receive sensitive national information relevant to their jurisdictions but are not routinely participants in Agacta's highest-level national-security discussions.

### Territorial uniforms

Each overseas territory has developed a distinctive police uniform. The different colours provide a visible expression of the autonomy of the territorial forces.

Despite these differences, the forces use broadly comparable styles of police clothing, equipment, insignia and protective equipment. Their uniforms represent different service identities within the broader Agactan policing tradition rather than completely separate approaches to police dress.

#### Tlelmund Police Force

The [[Tlelmund Police Force]] wears a **white shirt with navy trousers**, accompanied by a **sky-blue tie and sky-blue trimmings**.

#### Aquatrip Police Force

The [[Aquatrip Police Force]] wears a **light-green shirt with dark-green trousers**. Its colour combination bears some visual similarity to that used by the Police Service of Northern Ireland.

[[Aquatrip]] is a continental tropical territory rather than an island, geographically more comparable in character to French Guiana, although considerably smaller.

#### Savtrio Islands Police Force

The [[Savtrio Islands Police Force]] wears a **light-brown shirt with brown trousers**. The colour combination has some resemblance to the tan-and-brown uniforms associated with some American sheriff's departments, although the design itself follows Agactan police conventions.

#### Ulos Police Force

The [[Ulos Police Force]] wears a **blue shirt with navy-blue trousers**. Its colour palette is broadly comparable to that used by standard police uniforms in South Korea.

As with the National Police, all four territorial forces have increasingly adopted more practical operational clothing in recent decades while retaining their established colours as important elements of their individual identities.

## Agactan Gendarmerie

The modern policing system developed partly through the gradual abolition of the **Agactan Gendarmerie**, formerly a militarised national policing organisation.

The [[Constitution of Agacta|1954 Constitution]] called for the dissolution of the Gendarmerie and its replacement by fully professional civilian policing. The original expectation was that the organisation would have been completely decommissioned by approximately **1970**.

Implementation proved considerably slower.

Bureaucratic delays and prolonged negotiations concerning salaries, pensions and the transfer of serving officers meant that the Gendarmerie survived well beyond the original timetable.

By **1978**, its role had largely been reduced to **border control and policing the overseas territories**.

Around **1980**, the newly established devolved territorial police forces had almost completely replaced the Gendarmerie in ordinary overseas policing. Gendarmes were subsequently phased out of airport duties by the mid-1980s.

A much smaller residual organisation survived for another decade, principally performing duties at important ports.

The **Agactan Gendarmerie was finally decommissioned in 1994**. Its final personnel were stationed at the ports of **Orientoport, Treslio, Aquatrip and Ulos**.

Its abolition completed the transition envisaged by the 1954 Constitution and left Agacta with its modern system of a fully professional civilian National Police alongside the four autonomous territorial police forces.
