# Education in Agacta

Education in Agacta is divided into four main stages:

1. **Pre-Primary Education** — approximately ages 3–5
2. **Primary Education** — approximately ages 6–12
3. **Secondary 1** — approximately ages 13–15
4. **Secondary 2** — approximately ages 16–18

The education system is designed around gradual independence, broad education before specialisation, continuous assessment, multiple respected educational pathways, and accessible progression into higher education, vocational training, employment, or professional sport.

---

# Pre-Primary Education

Pre-primary education covers approximately ages 3–5 and becomes progressively more structured as children approach primary-school age.

## Pre-Primary Year 1

**Typical age:** 3  
**Schedule:** 3 days per week  
**Status:** Optional  
**Typical hours:** 08:00–11:00

The first year is predominantly play-based.

Activities commonly include:

- Games
- Nursery rhymes
- Songs
- Storytelling
- Basic motor skills
- Socialisation
- Early language development

## Pre-Primary Year 2

**Typical age:** 4  
**Schedule:** 4 days per week  
**Status:** Obligatory  
**Typical hours:** 08:00–11:00

The programme remains strongly play-based while gradually introducing more structured routines and learning activities.

## Pre-Primary Year 3

**Typical age:** 5  
**Schedule:** 5 days per week  
**Status:** Obligatory  
**Typical hours:** 08:00–12:00

The final pre-primary year serves as a transition between early childhood education and formal primary school.

Children continue to learn through play, songs and activities, but there is an increased emphasis on:

- Basic literacy preparation
- Basic numeracy
- Classroom routines
- Following instructions
- Independent work
- Preparation for Primary Year 1

---

# Primary Education

**Typical ages:** 6–12  
**Duration:** 7 years

Primary education provides broad general education in literacy, numeracy, languages, science, social studies, physical education, arts and other foundational areas.

The school day gradually becomes longer as children progress through primary school.

## Primary Years 1 and 2

**Typical hours:** 08:00–12:30

## Primary Years 3 and 4

**Typical hours:** 08:00–13:00

## Primary Years 5–7

**Typical hours:** 08:00–14:00

From Primary Year 2 onward, there is normally a **30-minute morning break at approximately 11:00**.

Large schools may stagger break times between different age groups.

---

# Typical Full School Day

A common timetable for older primary and secondary students is:

| Time | Period |
|---|---|
| 08:00–09:00 | Class |
| 09:00–10:00 | Class |
| 10:00–11:00 | Class |
| 11:00–11:30 | Break |
| 11:30–12:30 | Class |
| 12:30–13:15 | Class |
| 13:15–14:00 | Class |

The final two periods are normally shorter than the earlier morning periods.

---

# Breakfast and Morning Provision

Many schools provide breakfast before the formal school day.

A typical breakfast period is **07:00–08:00**.

Morning snacks may also be provided.

Breakfast provision is particularly useful for:

- Children arriving early
- Families with early work schedules
- Students travelling longer distances
- Low-income families

Most students nevertheless continue to eat breakfast at home.

---

# School Lunches

The traditional Agactan pattern is for students to eat their main lunchtime meal at home after classes.

However, many schools provide an **optional hot lunch after the formal school day**.

Families may pay for school meals:

- Through a single annual payment
- In instalments
- Individually on days when the student uses the service

Meals are subsidised and normally cost the equivalent of approximately **€2–€5 per meal**, depending on location, facilities and operating costs.

Children from low-income households may receive school lunches **free of charge through means-tested support**.

---

# Afternoon Activities

The end of formal classes does not necessarily mean the end of a child's organised day.

Agacta has extensive afternoon educational, sporting and childcare infrastructure.

Common activities include:

- ASC sports training
- School sports
- Band practice
- Choir
- Individual instrument lessons
- Homework clubs
- Chess clubs
- Debating societies
- Drama
- Hobby clubs
- Language academies
- Supervised study
- Community activities

This allows the relatively short formal school day to coexist with modern working patterns, including households in which both parents work full-time.

---

# School Transport

Agacta has an extensive school transport system.

Buses operate not only between schools and residential areas but also between:

- Schools
- ASC clubs
- Sports facilities
- Community centres
- Other educational facilities

Connections between schools and ASC clubs are particularly common because many children attend sports practice during the afternoon.

A typical weekday might therefore involve:

> 08:00 school  
> 14:00 classes finish  
> 14:00 school lunch  
> 14:45 bus to ASC club  
> 15:15 training  
> 17:00 home

On another day, the same child might instead return home for lunch before attending a language academy, music lesson or local activity later in the afternoon.

---

# Secondary 1

**Typical ages:** 13–15  
**Duration:** 3 years  
**Qualification:** **Secondary 1 Diploma**, commonly called the **Diplomita**

Secondary 1 is the compulsory lower-secondary stage.

Students continue to receive a broad general education while beginning to make limited subject choices.

## Compulsory Subjects

Students normally study:

- Ido
- English
- Mathematics
- Physical Education
- Religion or Ethics
- General Science
- Geography
- History

Students additionally choose **3 elective subjects**.

---

# Secondary 1 Electives

Common elective subjects include:

- Art
- Music
- Drama
- Sociology
- Politics
- Engineering
- Technology
- Computer Science
- Philosophy
- Economics

Every school must also offer at least one language from:

- Esperanto
- French
- German
- Rickan
- Luumian

Not every school offers every possible elective.

## Esperanto

Esperanto has a special status within the education system.

For students educated bilingually in Ido and Esperanto, Esperanto is treated as an **L1 subject** rather than a conventional foreign language.

Its position is broadly comparable to Irish in an Irish-medium school in Ireland.

Esperanto is generally not taught as an ordinary L2 to Ido-speaking students because the two languages are considered too closely related for this to provide a meaningful conventional foreign-language course.

---

# Elective Organisation

Schools have considerable freedom in how they organise elective subjects.

## Subject Tracks

Many schools group electives into informal tracks.

### Arts

- Art
- Music
- Drama

### Humanities

- Economics
- Politics
- Sociology

### Technical

- Computer Science
- Engineering
- Technology

A common combination is for a student to choose **two subjects from one group plus a language**.

Other schools actively encourage students to mix subjects across different areas.

## Timetable Blocks

Smaller schools often organise choices through timetable blocks rather than offering completely unrestricted combinations.

For example:

### Elective Slot 1
- Music
- Computer Science

### Elective Slot 2
- French
- Politics

### Elective Slot 3
- Engineering
- Art

Students choose one subject from each slot.

Consequently, the exact combinations available can vary substantially between schools.

---

# Secondary 1 Assessment

The Secondary 1 Diploma is accumulated across all three years.

There is no single high-stakes examination comparable to the Junior Certificate, GCSEs or similar systems.

## Continuous Assessment — 60%

Continuous assessment varies by subject and may include:

- Chapter or unit tests
- Written assignments
- Essays
- Practical assignments
- Laboratory work
- Presentations
- Individual projects
- Group projects
- Performances
- Technical work

## Formal Examinations — 40%

The formal examination component is progressively weighted:

- **5%** — end of Year 1
- **15%** — end of Year 2
- **20%** — end of Year 3

Later performance therefore carries greater weight without making the entire diploma dependent on one final examination period.

---

# External Marking and Moderation

Routine classwork and smaller continuous assessments are normally marked internally by the student's teachers.

More significant assessments may be externally marked or moderated.

These commonly include:

- End-of-year examinations
- Major essays
- Significant projects
- Major practical assessments
- Portfolios
- Major performances

Work may be sent to another school or assigned to an external examiner.

Samples may also be reviewed by adjudicators to ensure that marking standards remain reasonably consistent between schools and that individual examiners are not excessively strict or lenient.

Agacta has increasingly experimented with **digital external marking**, allowing more significant assignments and examination scripts to be submitted electronically.

---

# Progression from Secondary 1

Students normally require an **overall average of 40%** to receive the Diplomita and progress directly to Secondary 2.

Students who fall below this threshold may sit a **Secondary 2 entrance examination**.

Passing this examination allows progression despite the original Secondary 1 result.

Students who do not qualify through either route cannot immediately enter Secondary 2.

However, they remain entitled to **three years of publicly funded education or training**.

Possible routes include:

- Technical colleges
- Adult education colleges
- Funded apprenticeships
- Other recognised vocational training programmes

Failure to enter Secondary 2 therefore does not mean the end of publicly funded education.

---

# Secondary 2

**Typical ages:** 16–18  
**Duration:** 3 years  
**Qualification:** **Secondary 2 Diploma**

Secondary 2 is elective rather than compulsory.

The stage is deliberately designed as a transition between conventional school and adult life.

Students are increasingly expected to manage their own education, attendance, deadlines and behaviour.

---

# Secondary 2 Pathways

The principal pathways are:

1. **Academic**
2. **Vocational**
3. **Arts**
4. **ASC**

These are regarded as different forms of upper-secondary education rather than as a simple hierarchy of academic success.

---

# Academic Secondary 2

Academic Secondary 2 is the most common route for students intending to attend university.

Students progressively narrow their studies over the three years.

## Subject Load

- **Year 1:** 6 subjects
- **Year 2:** 5 subjects
- **Year 3:** 4 subjects

There are **no nationally compulsory subjects** at Secondary 2 level.

Completion of the Diplomita is considered sufficient proof that students have already received a broad general education.

Students can therefore specialise according to their interests and intended future studies.

---

# Secondary 2 Subject Selection

Students normally submit a ranked list containing more subjects than they ultimately require.

For example, a student may rank **7 or 8 preferred subjects**.

Schools normally guarantee the student's highest-ranked choices where possible.

Remaining choices depend on:

- Timetable compatibility
- Teacher availability
- Class size
- Facilities
- Overall demand

Large schools may permit highly flexible combinations.

Smaller schools may instead organise approximate pathways such as:

- Science
- Social Sciences
- Languages
- Mixed
- Technical

---

# Vocational Secondary 2

Vocational Secondary 2 combines general education with occupational and practical training.

Possible areas include:

- Tourism
- Hospitality
- Hair and Beauty
- Business Administration
- Technical Trades
- Media Production
- Retail
- Culinary Studies
- Applied Technology
- Other locally relevant vocational programmes

Vocational Secondary 2 is a deliberate educational choice and is not restricted to students with poor academic results.

---

# Arts Secondary 2

Arts education may take place in conventional secondary schools or specialist arts schools.

Areas can include:

- Painting
- Sculpture
- Photography
- Music
- Theatre
- Drama
- Dance
- Design
- Other performing and visual arts

Some arts programmes follow the same **6–5–4 subject structure** as Academic Secondary 2.

Specialist programmes may require:

- An interview
- An audition
- A portfolio
- An aptitude test
- A practical demonstration

These requirements establish whether a student has reached a sufficient standard for serious advanced or potentially professional artistic training.

---

# ASC Secondary 2

The **ASC pathway** is intended for students who have entered elite sporting development.

It provides an Agactan alternative to both the American college-sports system and the traditional European route in which promising athletes may effectively leave formal education during their mid-teens.

Students may enter the ASC pathway only after securing a place in the academy system of one of the **19 ASC franchises**.

For example, a student selected for the **Nleto Knights academy** may combine formal education with intensive athletic training.

## ASC Education

ASC students continue studying essential academic and practical subjects such as:

- Ido
- English
- Business Studies
- Mathematics
- Health
- Other subjects considered useful for their future needs

A substantial proportion of the week is devoted to:

- Team training
- Strength and conditioning
- Tactical instruction
- Recovery
- Sports science
- Competition preparation

The ASC pathway allows elite teenage athletes to pursue professional sporting development without abandoning formal education.

---

# Secondary 2 Culture

Secondary 2 is culturally distinct from Secondary 1.

Students are regarded as **adults in training**.

They are expected to assume increasing responsibility for their own choices.

## Attendance

Attendance is generally optional.

Students nevertheless remain fully responsible for:

- Missing lessons
- Completing assignments
- Attending assessments
- Meeting deadlines
- Catching up on missed material

Missing an ordinary lesson is not necessarily a disciplinary matter.

Missing an assessment without an accepted reason may result in a zero or another academic consequence.

The broad philosophy is:

> Secondary 1 is responsible for ensuring that students are educated.  
> Secondary 2 provides education, but students are increasingly responsible for using it.

## Discipline

Disciplinary matters in Secondary 2 are generally handled more like workplace conduct issues than conventional school discipline.

Meetings often resemble an HR or professional-conduct discussion rather than a traditional summons to the principal's office.

Parents are rarely involved.

Parental involvement is generally limited to serious situations involving matters such as:

- Safeguarding
- Serious misconduct
- Violence
- Criminal behaviour
- Significant welfare concerns

---

# Secondary 2 Assessment

The Secondary 2 Diploma follows the same broad assessment model as Secondary 1.

## Continuous Assessment — 60%

This may include:

- Written assignments
- Essays
- Practical work
- Presentations
- Individual projects
- Group projects
- Laboratory work
- Performances
- Technical assessments
- Smaller tests

## Formal Examinations — 40%

- **5%** — end of Year 1
- **15%** — end of Year 2
- **20%** — end of Year 3

Students require an **overall average of 40%** to pass the Secondary 2 Diploma.

Major work and formal examinations may be externally marked or moderated.

---

# Secondary 2 Results

Final Secondary 2 results are issued once major assessments, examinations and moderation procedures have been completed.

Historically, results were delivered by post.

Today they are normally released through an online national portal.

Results may begin appearing during July, but virtually all students have their complete final results by **mid-August**.

The later diploma ceremony is therefore ceremonial rather than the point at which students discover their results.

---

# Secondary 2 Conferring Ceremony

Students completing Secondary 2 are normally invited to an optional formal **conferring ceremony** at their secondary school in September or October.

Students wear academic dress, including:

- Cap
- Gown

The ceremony is an important Agactan rite of passage.

Students formally receive their Secondary 2 Diploma in front of teachers, families and classmates.

Because their academic results have already been known for several weeks, the ceremony represents the symbolic conclusion of school life.

It is commonly associated with:

- Family photographs
- Speeches
- Reunions with classmates
- Formal presentation of diplomas
- Celebration before students disperse into university, employment, training, sport or other paths

---

# University Access

Successful completion of the **Secondary 2 Diploma** gives students the right to access state-owned universities in Agacta.

Most university programmes accept students on the basis of the diploma.

Certain courses have additional subject prerequisites.

Examples include:

- Medicine
- Law
- Engineering
- Certain sciences
- Other highly specialised programmes

A programme may require students to achieve a specified percentage in particular Secondary 2 subjects.

Where the number of qualified applicants exceeds available places, **grade point average** may be used to allocate places.

In general, a student who satisfies the relevant prerequisites can normally expect to receive an offer from **some state university**, even when they do not obtain a place at their preferred institution.

---

# Educational Philosophy

The Agactan education system is built around several broad principles:

- Broad general education before specialisation
- Limited reliance on high-stakes examinations
- Continuous assessment across several years
- Multiple respected academic, vocational, artistic and sporting pathways
- Strong public guarantees of continued education or training
- Increasing student autonomy between ages 16 and 18
- Close integration between schools, ASC clubs, community organisations and transport
- Accessible state higher education
- Recognition that university is only one of several legitimate outcomes

The overall transition is gradual:

**Pre-Primary**  
→ play, social development and preparation for formal education

**Primary School**  
→ broad structured childhood education

**Secondary 1**  
→ increasing independence and limited subject choice

**Secondary 2**  
→ specialisation and adult responsibility

**University, employment, apprenticeship, professional sport or advanced training**  
→ independent adult life
