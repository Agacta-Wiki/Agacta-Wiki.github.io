# Christmas in Agacta

**Christmas in Agacta** is a 52-day festive season beginning on **12 December** and ending with **[[Candlemas]] on 2 February**. Christmas Day, St Stephen's Day, New Year's Day and Candlemas are national holidays, while many Agactans take much or all of the period from 23 December to 1 January as holiday time.

The long duration of the season is one of the most distinctive features of an Agactan Christmas. Decorations remain up throughout January, Christmas music and films continue to be broadcast, and Christmas greetings remain normal until the beginning of February.

Christmas overlaps with **[[Cultural and Ancestral Week]]**, which is held from 23 to 29 December. Despite their overlap, the two are completely separate observances. Agactan households may celebrate Christmas, Cultural and Ancestral Week, both, or neither, although the majority celebrate both.

## The Christmas season

### 12–22 December

The Christmas season begins on **12 December**, when public decorations and town-square illuminations are traditionally switched on.

From this point, *Felican Kristnasin!* ("Merry Christmas!") becomes a normal seasonal greeting.

Christmas markets, decorations and seasonal events become increasingly prominent during the following days.

### Cultural and Ancestral Week

**[[Cultural and Ancestral Week]]** takes place from **23–29 December**.

Although it occurs during the Christmas season, it is not a Christmas observance. It is a non-denominational celebration of Agactan heritage inspired in its structure by Kwanzaa.

Families participating in Cultural and Ancestral Week traditionally eat dinner together on each of its seven nights and spend time telling family stories alongside Agactan history, legends and fairy tales.

Seven candles are progressively lit during the week:

1. Dark blue
2. Red
3. Light blue
4. Dark blue
5. Red
6. Light blue
7. White

The final day, **29 December**, is a national holiday and commonly concludes with a large family meal followed by storytelling, music or dancing.

### Christmas Day

**Christmas Day, 25 December**, is a national holiday centred principally on family meals and gift-giving.

Religious Agactans may also attend Christmas services. Within the country's majority Unitarian tradition, services tend to emphasise compassion, generosity and goodwill rather than theological doctrine.

Children traditionally receive presents from **[[Grandfather Talmar]]**, Agacta's Christmas gift-bringer.

### St Stephen's Day

**St Stephen's Day, 26 December**, is also a national holiday.

The day tends to be more outwardly social than Christmas Day. Visiting friends, charity and community activities are common.

Sport forms a major part of St Stephen's Day in a manner comparable to Boxing Day and St Stephen's Day sporting traditions in Britain and Ireland.

In the [[Savtrio Islands]], the day is also associated with the arrival of [[Grandfather Talmar]] for his traditional post-Christmas holiday.

### 30 December–1 January

The period between Cultural and Ancestral Week and New Year's Day is generally relaxed.

**New Year's Eve** is considerably more formal. Fireworks are common in towns and cities, and dressing up for the evening is traditional. Families commonly eat the final meal of the year together before going to bars, house parties or celebrations at home. Champagne and other sparkling wines are strongly associated with the occasion.

A more recent custom is deliberately choosing fast food or unusually indulgent food for the final meal, based on the joking idea that because it is the last day of the year, **"it doesn't count."**

**New Year's Day** is considerably quieter. A large late brunch is traditional, particularly after the previous night's celebrations. Eating a substantial first meal of the year is associated with prosperity for the year ahead. Small concerts are also commonly held in town and community halls.

## The Long Christmas

Christmas does not end with New Year in Agacta.

The period from **2 January until 1 February** is commonly regarded as the **Long Christmas**. Christmas trees and decorations remain in homes and public spaces throughout January, while Christmas films, music, advertising and other seasonal media remain commonplace.

Consequently, wishing somebody *Felican Kristnasin!* even in the final week of January is entirely normal.

Schools, workplaces and ordinary life nevertheless resume during this period. The continuation of Christmas is therefore part of everyday January life rather than an uninterrupted period of holiday.

## Candlemas

**[[Candlemas]]**, celebrated on **2 February**, is both a national holiday and the traditional conclusion of Christmas.

Religious services are held by several Christian denominations. Candles are traditionally carried to services to be blessed and may subsequently be used in the home during the year.

Candlemas is also the occasion for the **second great festive dinner** of the Agactan Christmas season. Its importance within the household is comparable to Christmas dinner itself.

Most families deliberately serve a different main course at Candlemas from the one they ate at Christmas. A family that has roast duck for Christmas, for example, might choose trout, pork or beef for Candlemas.

Christmas decorations traditionally come down on **3 February**, bringing the 52-day season to an end.

## Food

Food is central to Christmas and Candlemas celebrations, but there is **no prescribed Agactan Christmas dinner**.

Families vary their menus considerably. Some maintain family favourites while others change the meal from year to year. Christmas dinner and Candlemas dinner are generally different meals rather than repetitions of one another.

Popular festive main courses include:

- roast duck with orange and honey glaze
- venison tenderloin
- slow-roasted pork shoulder
- lamb with rosemary and honey
- trout baked with cream and herbs
- roast chicken with white wine sauce
- braised beef with juniper and red wine
- beef stew with bread dumplings
- pork and apple stew
- duck with chestnut and onion stuffing
- spicy sausage or chicken stews
- swordfish with pineapple and chilli
- curried goat
- sea bass with tamarind glaze


Common accompaniments include potatoes, **paskuli**, bread, **pano-frito**, vegetables, pâtés, cured meats and olives.

Turkey has become more common, particularly in cities, through overseas influence, but has not displaced the wider variety traditionally found on Agactan festive tables.

### Dueta Spicpano

The signature Agactan Christmas cake is **Dueta Spicpano**, a two-layer ginger sponge cake filled with vanilla cream and covered with thick white icing.

Sugared stars or candied fruit are common decorations.

Numerous variations exist such as:

- Lemon-zest cream with light almond icing
- Rum-cream filling and caramel icing
- White chocolate glaze with berry jam between layers.
- Honey-cream centre and cinnamon icing
- Hazelnut cream with a rustic sugar icing.
- Chestnut filling and orange icing
- Rich milk chocolate cream, white chocolate icing, dark chocolate glaze
- Papaya jam layer with pineapple icing.
- Mango cream and cardamom icing topped with edible gold leaf.


## Grandfather Talmar

**[[Grandfather Talmar]]**, or *Patro Talmar*, is the traditional Agactan Christmas gift-bringer.

He is portrayed as a bearded old sailor from [[Tlemund]]. According to folklore, Talmar was originally a sailor who became renowned for giving food and toys to poor families in the coastal communities of Agacta's northern territories. The story eventually developed into the modern tradition of Talmar delivering presents to children throughout Agacta.

Talmar is a secular folkloric character rather than a saint or religious authority and is consequently celebrated by Agactans from a variety of religious backgrounds as well as non-religious families.

### The Lightship

Talmar travels aboard the **Lumoŝipo**, or **Lightship**, a magical amphibious vessel based at his workshop in [[Tlemund]].

On Christmas Eve it travels by sea from Tlemund to the mainland and Agacta's other territories. Upon reaching land, the Lightship simply continues travelling across the ground as though it were still sailing on water.

Talmar does not enter houses through chimneys. He is instead said to possess the ability to walk through walls.

### Tlemund and the Christmas Eve departure

Talmar's association with [[Tlemund]] has produced a substantial Christmas tourism industry. Families from mainland Agacta travel north during December to visit his traditional home and workshop.

A large parade is held on Christmas Eve to mark Talmar's departure aboard the Lightship.

After completing his deliveries, Talmar is traditionally said to travel to the [[Savtrio Islands]], where he remains on holiday from St Stephen's Day until **1 June**.

### The June return

Talmar's return to [[Tlemund]] on **1 June** is marked by another major parade and is broadcast nationally on television.

Children are jokingly reminded that Talmar has returned to work and is watching their behaviour again, conveniently shortly before the summer holidays.

Talmar ceremonially reopens his workshop for the coming Christmas. Later that day, a small number of selected children, particularly children who have experienced serious illness or have been recognised for bravery, receive the **"first toys of the year."**

These can be substantial presents such as bicycles, scooters and games consoles, or even family holidays.

### Talmar in modern Agactan culture

On the final day of school before the Christmas holidays, Talmar traditionally appears for a humorous national radio interview which is played in classrooms around the country.

He is portrayed as friendly, patient, slightly eccentric and somewhat old-fashioned. Running jokes frequently involve his attempts to understand new technology and changes in children's lives.

Talmar is also known to appear at the **[[Nleto]] Christmas market**, sometimes unexpectedly helping vendors at their stalls.

Children traditionally contacted Talmar by post. Letters remain popular, particularly among younger children, although email has become increasingly accepted.

### Appearance

Talmar traditionally wears a **long navy-blue coat with white fur trim, red scarf and captain's hat bearing a silver star**.

His sailor appearance distinguishes him visually from the conventional red-suited Santa Claus.

A **robin** traditionally accompanies him on his journeys.

Talmar is usually portrayed as patient, humorous and gentle rather than threatening. Children are expected to behave well if they want a present, but there is no Agactan equivalent of receiving coal or another deliberately unpleasant punishment. A child who has behaved badly simply receives nothing from Talmar.

### The Hearth-Shoe Tradition

One of the oldest Talmar traditions is the **hearth-shoe**.

On Christmas Eve, children clean and polish a pair of shoes and leave them near the hearth or against a wall before going to bed.

During the night, Talmar fills the shoes with **sweets, nuts and small toys**, with larger presents left nearby.

## Regional traditions

### Setroburg

In [[Setroburg]], fishermen traditionally decorate their boats with Christmas lights and Christmas trees.

Setroburg also differs from most of Agacta in when decorations are removed. Historically, decorations remained up until the **first warm day of the year**, sometimes weeks after Candlemas.

Today they are generally removed a week or two after Candlemas. However, an unusually warm day in early February is still traditionally taken as a sign that the decorations should come down immediately.

### Stototrip

In [[Stototrip]], street bonfires on 12 December traditionally mark the beginning of Christmas.

The city's proximity to [[Sensin]] and its Esperanto-speaking population are also reflected in Christmas celebrations. Carols can be heard in both Ido and Esperanto, while bilingual Christmas cards are common.

## Overseas territories

### Ulos

Christmas and [[Cultural and Ancestral Week]] in [[Ulos]] reflect the territory's mixture of Agactan and Inuit cultures.

Storytelling is particularly important during Cultural and Ancestral Week, incorporating both Agactan traditions and Inuit and Avarkan oral traditions. The week has developed into an important celebration of the territory's mixed cultural heritage.

### Tlemund

[[Cultural and Ancestral Week]] is similarly important in [[Tlemund]], where Inuit traditions play a particularly prominent role.

Tlemund is also the centre of **Grandfather Talmar culture**, attracting large numbers of Christmas visitors from mainland Agacta. His Christmas Eve departure and 1 June return are among the territory's largest annual public events.

### Aquatrip

The tropical climate of [[Aquatrip]] gives Christmas a markedly different appearance from mainland celebrations.

Christmas begins on 12 December with the **Lighting of the Palms**, when palm trees are covered in fairy lights. Decorations commonly incorporate tropical flowers and marine imagery.

A distinctive feature is the tradition of attending **two Christmas services**, one around midnight on Christmas Eve and another on Christmas morning.

The Christmas Eve service is the more traditional of the two. Christmas morning celebrates the birth of Jesus with a characteristically Amarcan welcome involving extensive singing and brightly coloured summer clothing. Girls and young women aged 20 and under traditionally wear bright dresses and attend barefoot.

Afterwards, communities hold large **Christmas meals on the beach**. Families bring dishes to communal tables and deliberately provide more food than they require themselves so that people unable to provide their own meal can participate freely.

Candlemas has a similar pattern of church services, colourful clothing and communal beach dining.

### Savtrio Islands

The [[Savtrio Islands]] share several Christmas traditions with [[Aquatrip]], including the two Christmas services, colourful summer clothing and communal Christmas meals on the beach.

Savtrio is also a major Christmas tourism destination for mainland Agactans seeking a tropical Christmas. Visitors frequently participate in the local services and beach celebrations, while restaurants in tourist areas accommodate visitors who do not have cooking facilities by preparing dishes for them to contribute to communal meals.

Local festive foods include spiced mango cakes, honey bites and cardamom milk drinks, while [[Cultural and Ancestral Week]] prominently features drumming, dance and ancestral art.

Savtrio also occupies an important place in Talmar folklore. He is said to arrive there on St Stephen's Day for his annual holiday, prompting a large welcome parade.

His traditional summer residence is said to have been built for him by St Stephen as a reward for his work.

Talmar remains in Savtrio until his return to [[Tlemund]] on 1 June.
