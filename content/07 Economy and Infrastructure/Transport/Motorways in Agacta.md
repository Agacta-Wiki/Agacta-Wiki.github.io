# Motorways in Agacta

The motorway network of [[Agacta]] consists of a system of high-capacity roads known as **Autostrade**, designated by the prefix **A** followed by a route number.

Agacta was an early adopter of motorway construction, opening its first motorway section in **1957**. The network initially developed around two principal corridors: the **A1**, popularly known as *Agacta's Main Street*, and the **A2**, connecting the capital, [[Treslio]], with [[Setroburg]].

Unlike countries where motorway construction primarily occurred during the late 20th and early 21st centuries, Agacta had already established an extensive intercity motorway network by 1980. By that year, the capital had continuous motorway connections with [[Nleto]], [[Merino]], [[Stototrip]] and Setroburg, meaning that the country's five largest cities were connected by motorway.

The opening of the A5 to [[Orientoport]] in 1988 and the completion of the A1 extension to [[Kalisto]] in 1990 further strengthened this network.

Subsequent motorway construction concentrated increasingly on providing alternative routes, improving regional connectivity and linking areas not directly served by the original A1 and A2 corridors.

By 2026, Agacta's motorway network is considered largely complete, although selected road improvements and motorway upgrades continue.

## Motorway network

Agacta's motorway network consists of nine principal A-series routes, eight of which were constructed as motorways, while the A9 has been developed through the selective upgrading of an existing road corridor.

The A1 and A2 form the original backbone of the network, while subsequent routes provide connections between additional cities and regions.

One distinctive feature of the motorway system is the difference between the official extent of a numbered motorway and the intercity corridor it serves.

This is particularly noticeable around [[Setroburg]], where the city's geographical position beside [[Lake Setro]] has influenced the development of the motorway network.

### A1: Kalisto–Stototrip

The **A1**, popularly known as *Agacta's Main Street*, is the country's principal north–south motorway.

It runs from [[Kalisto]] in the north to [[Stototrip]] in the south, serving:

- [[Kalisto]]
- [[Montanomund]]
- [[Treslio]]
- [[Merino]]
- [[Nleto]]
- [[Stototrip]]

The motorway provides a continuous connection between several of Agacta's largest cities.

The earliest sections opened in 1957, while the final extension to Kalisto was completed in 1990.

The northern extension was particularly expensive because of the extensive tunnelling required through mountainous terrain.

### A2: Setroburg–Treslio

The **A2** connects [[Setroburg]] with the capital, [[Treslio]], passing through the [[Abeldrip]] area.

It follows broadly the same corridor as the N2, which was retained as a separate national road.

The A2 was completed in 1975 following lengthy disputes over its alignment around [[Lake Setro]].

### A3: Setroburg–Treslio

The **A3** provides a more southerly motorway connection between Setroburg and Treslio.

It is also of particular importance because several other major motorway corridors share its alignment when travelling to or from Setroburg.

The A4, A6 and A7 diverge from the A3 at different locations, allowing traffic from Setroburg to reach Nleto, Stototrip and Merino.

The motorway was constructed between 1995 and 2003.

### A4: Setroburg–Nleto corridor

The **A4** forms part of the principal motorway connection between Setroburg and [[Nleto]].

Although the route serves the Setroburg–Nleto corridor, the A4 does not begin in Setroburg itself.

Traffic initially follows the A3 before joining the A4 at [[Mliuntra]], a small lakeside town a few kilometres west of [[Salialako]].

The A4 was constructed between 1992 and 1998.

### A5: Treslio–Orientoport

The **A5** connects Treslio with [[Orientoport]].

Constructed between 1984 and 1988, it provided Orientoport with a direct motorway connection to the capital.

### A6: Setroburg–Stototrip corridor

The **A6** forms part of the motorway connection between Setroburg and Stototrip, continuing towards the border with [[Sensin]].

Its official numbered route begins south of Lake Setro, at the point where it separates from the A3.

Motorists travelling from Setroburg towards Stototrip therefore initially use the A3 before continuing along the A6.

The A6 was constructed between 2003 and 2010.

### A7: Setroburg–Merino corridor

The **A7** provides the motorway connection between Setroburg and [[Merino]].

Like the A4 and A6, it does not begin in Setroburg itself.

Traffic follows the A3 before joining the A7 near [[Salia Abel]].

The earliest sections of the A7 outside Merino were constructed in 1995.

A short section beyond its junction with the A3 opened in 2003, with the remaining motorway completed between 2004 and 2008.

### A8: Setroburg–Montanomund

The **A8** is the principal motorway connecting western and northern Agacta.

Its official route begins at [[Glintrotrip]], north of Lake Setro, and continues towards [[Montanomund]], passing directly through [[Riala]].

For motorists travelling from Setroburg, the A8 forms the principal motorway route towards northern Agacta.

At Riala, motorists can continue towards [[Tleve]], while at Montanomund the A8 connects with the A1 towards Kalisto.

The route is equally important for traffic travelling in the opposite direction, providing northern Agacta with direct motorway access to Riala and the western region.

The A8 was constructed between 2000 and 2006.

### A9: Treslio–Tleve

The **A9** forms part of the road corridor between Treslio and Tleve.

The route also serves [[Yafo]], one of Agacta's principal skiing destinations, and provides access towards the ski towns of [[Amio]] and [[Mlat]].

Unlike the other eight principal motorways, the A9 was not constructed as an entirely new motorway.

Instead, sections of the existing corridor have been upgraded to motorway standard, while much of the remaining route operates as a dual carriageway.

Motorway upgrades began in 2013.

As of 2026, there are no plans to convert the entire corridor into a continuous motorway.

## Road classifications

Agacta operates a hierarchical road classification system.

| Classification | Prefix | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Autostrade | A | Motorways |
| National roads | N | Principal roads connecting major settlements |
| Regional roads | Varies by region | Roads serving regional and interurban traffic |
| County roads | County-specific | Roads connecting smaller settlements and rural communities |

Motorways and national roads form the principal national road network.

Regional and county roads provide more localised connections, including routes serving towns and villages that are not directly connected to the motorway network.

In northern Agacta, regional roads use the prefix **X**, as the letter N is already reserved for national roads. This convention is comparable to the use of X for Wednesday in Spanish calendars, where M is already used for Tuesday.

Examples of northern regional road numbers include X17, X18 and X31.

### Relationship between motorways and national roads

An important feature of Agacta's road system is the coexistence of motorways and older national roads serving broadly similar corridors.

The A1–A8 motorway corridors each have a corresponding N-series national road following the same general route.

These national roads provide alternative connections and continue to serve settlements that are bypassed by the motorways.

In 1965, the government decided that the A1 and A2 should be constructed as separate motorways rather than replacing the existing N1 and N2.

Consequently, national roads continued to serve local and regional traffic, including settlements bypassed by the new motorways.

The motorway network was designed primarily for faster long-distance travel, while the national road system retained its wider role in connecting communities.

The A9 differs from this pattern because it was developed through the partial upgrading of an existing road corridor rather than the construction of an entirely separate motorway.

## Motorway construction standards

Agacta drives on the **right-hand side of the road**, with overtaking normally taking place on the left.

The general motorway speed limit is **130 km/h**.

Agactan motorways are fully controlled-access roads, with grade-separated junctions and physical separation between opposing traffic flows.

The standard motorway configuration consists of:

- Two running lanes in each direction.
- A central reservation with safety barriers.
- Hard shoulders.
- Grade-separated intersections.
- Dedicated entry and exit slip roads.

Busier sections, particularly those serving major metropolitan areas, may have additional lanes.

Hard shoulders are standard on almost all Agactan motorways.

Older motorway sections may have narrower shoulders, shorter slip roads or less generous junction layouts than modern roads. Many have undergone reconstruction or modernisation since their original opening.

The national 130 km/h limit does not prevent lower speed restrictions on particular sections, especially around tunnels, difficult terrain, major junctions and urban approaches.

### National and regional roads

National roads vary considerably in their engineering standards.

Some are modern dual carriageways, while others remain conventional single-carriageway roads.

Regional and county roads also vary in quality and capacity. Roads connecting substantial towns may be relatively wide and well engineered, while older rural roads can retain narrower alignments and sharp bends.

Road maintenance standards and road design are not necessarily the same. A narrow rural road may have a well-maintained surface despite retaining its original alignment.

Roundabouts are moderately common in Agacta, particularly around towns and on bypasses, although traffic lights and conventional priority junctions are also widely used.

## History

### Early development: 1957–1965

Agacta's first motorway section opened in **1957**, extending southwards from Treslio.

In **1958**, a second motorway section opened east of Setroburg, followed by another section extending westwards from Treslio.

The early motorway projects were not initially part of a fully continuous intercity network. They were particularly important for relieving congestion around major urban areas.

In **1960**, the northern suburbs of Nleto were bypassed by a new motorway section.

By **1964**, motorists travelling between Treslio and either Nleto or Setroburg could use motorway sections to avoid much of the suburban road network at both ends of their journeys.

However, the intervening routes had not yet been developed into continuous motorways.

### The 1965 motorway decision

In 1965, the Agactan government formally adopted a policy of constructing separate A1 and A2 motorways, broadly following the corridors of the existing N1 and N2.

Rather than converting the national roads themselves into motorways, the government chose to preserve them for local and regional traffic.

This decision established the foundations of Agacta's modern motorway network.

Construction subsequently concentrated on completing continuous routes between the country's principal cities.

### Completion of the A1 between Treslio and Nleto: 1968–1973

Between 1968 and 1973, construction progressed along the A1 corridor between Treslio and Nleto.

By **1973**, the two cities were connected by continuous motorway.

The new route considerably improved long-distance road travel along one of Agacta's most important transport corridors.

### Construction of the A2: 1968–1975

The A2, connecting Treslio with Setroburg, presented greater planning difficulties.

Construction continued between 1968 and 1975, with particularly contentious debates surrounding the motorway's route near [[Lake Setro]].

Several alternative alignments were considered, including routes passing to the north or south of the lake.

The planning disputes concerned:

- Whether the motorway should pass north or south of Lake Setro.
- The distance between the road and the lakeshore.
- Environmental concerns surrounding the lake.
- Opposition from environmental organisations.
- The acquisition of prairie land from farmers.
- Compensation and land-purchase arrangements.

These disagreements contributed to the A2's prolonged development.

The continuous motorway connection between Treslio and Setroburg was completed in **1975**.

### Southern extension of the A1: 1975–1980

Following completion of the original Treslio–Nleto corridor, construction continued southwards towards Stototrip.

The extension was completed in **1980**.

By this time, the A1 and A2 provided continuous motorway connections between Treslio and the country's second, third, fourth and fifth largest cities: Nleto, Merino, Stototrip and Setroburg.

This represented a major achievement in Agacta's transport development.

Although substantial motorway construction would continue over the following decades, the country already possessed an extensive network connecting its principal population centres.

### Northern extension to Kalisto: 1980–1990

Between 1980 and 1990, the A1 was extended northwards towards Kalisto.

This was among the most expensive road construction projects undertaken in Agacta.

The difficult mountainous terrain required extensive tunnelling and major engineering works.

The completed extension provided a continuous motorway connection between Kalisto and the existing A1 network.

The A1 consequently became an important route across much of the country's north–south extent.

### The A5: 1984–1988

The **A5**, connecting Treslio and Orientoport, was constructed between 1984 and 1988.

Its completion provided another major city with direct motorway access to the capital.

By 1990, Treslio had continuous motorway connections with six other major Agactan cities: Nleto, Merino, Stototrip, Setroburg, Orientoport and Kalisto.

The A1, A2 and A5 formed the backbone of the country's intercity motorway system.

## Later motorway expansion

From the 1990s onwards, motorway construction increasingly concentrated on consolidating the existing network.

Rather than primarily connecting the largest cities to Treslio for the first time, new motorways improved cross-country connections, provided alternative routes and extended motorway access to additional areas.

### A4: 1992–1998

Construction of the A4 was completed between 1992 and 1998.

The motorway provided an important connection between the Setroburg and Nleto corridors, branching from the A3 at Mliuntra.

### A3: 1995–2003

The A3 was constructed between 1995 and 2003.

Its completion was particularly important for the developing motorway network south of Lake Setro, providing a shared approach to Setroburg for traffic using the A4, A6 and A7.

### A7: 1995–2008

The A7 was developed in several stages.

The earliest motorway sections outside [[Merino]] were constructed in **1995**.

In **2003**, a short motorway section opened beyond the point where the A7 separates from the A3.

The remaining sections were constructed between **2004 and 2008**, completing the motorway.

### A8: 2000–2006

The A8 was constructed between 2000 and 2006.

The motorway strengthened connections between western and northern Agacta, particularly between Setroburg, Riala and Montanomund.

### A6: 2003–2010

The A6 was constructed between 2003 and 2010.

Its completion formed part of the final major period of national motorway expansion, providing an important connection towards Stototrip and the Sensinese border.

### A9: 2013–present

Since 2013, individual sections of the A9 corridor have been constructed or upgraded to motorway standard.

Unlike most other A-series routes, the A9 has not been developed as a continuous motorway.

Much of the remaining route consists of dual carriageway rather than full motorway.

As of 2026, there are no plans to upgrade the entire route to motorway standard.

The A9 reflects a more selective approach to motorway investment, in which individual sections receive improvements without requiring the reconstruction of an entire corridor.

## Motorway tolls

Most motorways in Agacta are **toll-free**.

The motorway network is predominantly funded through public infrastructure expenditure.

Tolling is not a general requirement for using the country's A-series roads.

## Motorway services

Agacta has an extensive network of motorway service areas, providing fuel, food, rest facilities and other amenities.

The A1 and A2 generally have full service areas approximately every **35–50 km**.

On other motorway routes, full services are generally spaced approximately **45–60 km** apart.

One distinctive feature of the system is the prevalence of large shared service complexes accessible to traffic travelling in both directions.

Smaller facilities are more commonly provided separately on each carriageway.

The motorway service-area land is publicly owned, while private companies develop and operate the facilities under long-term ground leases, generally lasting **75–99 years**.

The major Agactan motorway service operators include AgOleo, Ulben, Pauzo Inn, Auto Halto and Grilerio de Veturisto.

See [[Motorway Services in Agacta]] for further information.

## The motorway network in 2026

By 2026, Agacta's motorway network is largely complete.

The A1 remains the country's best-known motorway and is widely referred to as **Agacta's Main Street**.

The A1 and A2 retain particular historical importance because they provided extensive intercity motorway connections decades before the construction of much of the remaining network.

Later motorway development has primarily strengthened connections between existing corridors and improved access to other regions.

The national motorway system therefore combines infrastructure originating in the 1950s and 1960s with roads constructed during the major expansion programmes of the 1990s and 2000s.

While motorway construction has slowed considerably since the completion of the A6 in 2010, selective improvements continue, particularly along the partially upgraded A9 corridor.
