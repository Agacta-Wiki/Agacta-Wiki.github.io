# Rail Transport in Agacta

Rail transport in [[Agacta]] consists of an extensive high-speed, long-distance, regional and suburban network. The mainland network combines publicly owned infrastructure with open-access competition on high-speed and major long-distance routes, competitive tendering on several major regional networks, and direct operation of other regional services by the state-owned [[Agacta Naciona Fervojo]] (ANF).

Agacta occupies an important position in the continental rail network. The **Continental East high-speed corridor** crosses the country between [[Sensin]] and [[Luum]], while high-speed lines through [[Setroburg]] connect the eastern network with [[Lonin]] and [[Alaram]]. Since the opening of the Setroburg–Nleto high-speed line in May 2026, Agacta also forms part of the fastest rail route between the Sensinese capital [[Lojburg]] and the Luumian capital Lonin.

High-speed lines in Agacta permit speeds of up to **320 km/h**.

## Infrastructure and operating model

Mainland high-speed infrastructure within Agacta is owned by the Agactan state but is operated under an **open-access system**.

Any licensed operator meeting the necessary technical, signalling and safety requirements may apply for train paths and operate services upon payment of the applicable infrastructure charges. No operator has an exclusive right to operate a high-speed route.

Foreign operators are permitted to carry passengers on journeys wholly within Agacta. For example, a Sensinese or Luumian train travelling through Agacta may sell tickets between two Agactan stations.

In practice, foreign state-owned railways generally avoid establishing services specifically intended to compete for purely domestic Agactan traffic. Such operations, while legal, could be politically sensitive because they could be interpreted as a foreign government using its state railway to compete directly with Agactan operators.

This convention does not generally apply to private foreign operators.

## High-speed network

### Continental East Corridor

The **Continental East Corridor** is the principal north–south high-speed railway along the eastern side of the continent.

From south to north, the corridor begins at [[An Goma]] in southern [[Sensin]] and runs through Sensin's eastern regions, including:

- [[Casto]]
    
- [[Lojburg]]
    
- [[Signaburg]]
    
- [[Aulburg]]
    
- [[Sandmund]]
    

It crosses the Sensin–Agacta border at [[Stototrip]] before continuing through:

- [[Nleto]]
    
- [[Merino]]
    
- [[Treslio]]
    
- [[Kalisto]]
    

A branch from Treslio serves [[Orientoport]].

North of Kalisto, the railway crosses the Agacta–Luum border near [[Tlavend]], traverses the Luumian panhandle and continues to [[Uvulp]] in northeastern [[Luum]].

The corridor forms the principal high-speed backbone of the eastern side of the continent and carries Agactan, Sensinese, Luumian and private services.

### Development

Agacta's first high-speed railway opened in **1988**, connecting:

**Treslio – Merino – Nleto**

The line was extended north from Nleto to **Kalisto in 1994**.

Construction subsequently continued towards Sensin. In **June 1997**, Agactan and Sensinese construction teams met on the Stototrip–Sandmund section. The meeting became an iconic moment in the history of the railway and was frequently compared with the meeting of the British and French Channel Tunnel construction teams earlier in the decade.

The connection between Stototrip and the Sensinese network opened in **1998**.

The **Treslio–Orientoport branch** opened in **2002**.

In **2008**, the high-speed railway between Kalisto and Uvulp was completed, creating a continuous high-speed route along the eastern side of the continent from An Goma to Uvulp.

The Prime Ministers of Agacta, Sensin and Luum travelled together aboard the inaugural through journey between Uvulp and An Goma.

## Treslio–Setroburg High-Speed Line

A high-speed railway between [[Treslio]] and [[Setroburg]] was first proposed during the late 1980s but experienced a prolonged development process.

Disputes and delays included the precise alignment, construction contracts, compensation for landowners, reconstruction of stations and a lengthy debate over whether the railway should pass north or south of [[Lake Setro]].

The line eventually opened in **2015**.

Like the Continental East Corridor, it is publicly owned and open to competing licensed operators.

## Western high-speed network

### Setroburg–Lonin

The high-speed line west of Setroburg connects Agacta with the southeastern Luumian network and the Luumian capital [[Lonin]].

The complete **Setroburg–Lonin high-speed connection opened in 2019**.

Together with the Treslio–Setroburg line, this permits through high-speed services between Treslio and Lonin.

### Setroburg–Alaram

The western railway also diverges southwest towards [[Alaram]].

The **Setroburg–Alaram connection opened in 2023**. The Agactan portion operates to high-speed standards, although sections of the connecting Alaramian network have not yet been upgraded to full high-speed operation.

### Setroburg–Nleto

The **Setroburg–Nleto high-speed line opened in May 2026**, following a delay from its originally planned February opening.

The project was partly funded by the governments of **Sensin and Luum** because of its international significance. Connecting Setroburg directly with Nleto completed a high-speed route between the Luumian and Sensinese capitals:

**Lonin – Setroburg – Nleto – Stototrip – Sandmund – Aulburg – Lojburg**

This provides the fastest rail connection between Lonin and Lojburg and allows trains between the two capitals to bypass Treslio.

## Conventional long-distance rail

Open-access competition is not restricted exclusively to high-speed lines.

The **Setroburg–Kalisto** conventional long-distance route is open to competing operators, although it is currently served only by ANF. [[Agacta Net]] has announced plans to enter the route.

A future high-speed upgrade has been proposed for the corridor but has not entered formal planning.

## Regional and suburban rail

Several **major regional and suburban networks** are competitively tendered by the central government. These include networks serving the areas around:

- Treslio
    
- Setroburg
    
- Nleto
    
- Stototrip
    
- Merino
    
- Orientoport
    
- Kalisto
    

ANF is permitted to compete for these contracts alongside private operators.

Regional services outside the tendered networks are generally operated directly by ANF.

ANF remains the **largest regional rail operator in Agacta**, followed by Agacta Net and [[Destinia]].

## Overseas territories

Rail and light-rail systems in Agacta's overseas territories operate under a different model from the mainland.

The principal systems are:

- [[Aquatrip Light Rail]]
    
- [[Ulos Light Rail]]
    
- [[Tlelmund Light Rail]]
    
- [[Savtrio Islands Tramways]]
    

These networks are public monopolies controlled by their respective devolved territorial governments.

The process of transferring responsibility for the networks began with reforms to the overseas territories' statutes of autonomy agreed in **1992**. Ownership was fully transferred from ANF to the respective devolved regional railway operators in **1997**.

The resulting operators are broadly comparable in institutional structure to FGC in Catalonia, FGV in the Valencian Community and Euskotren in the Basque Country.

# Passenger operators

## Agacta Naciona Fervojo

**[[Agacta Naciona Fervojo]] (ANF)** is Agacta's state-owned national railway company and largest passenger rail operator. It has operated rail services in Agacta for more than a century.

ANF operates high-speed, conventional long-distance, regional and suburban services. It also competes for regional networks offered through competitive tender.

Its livery uses **dark blue, red and light blue**, mirroring the colours of the Agactan flag.

### Granda Veloceso

ANF markets its high-speed services as **[[Granda Veloceso]]**.

Granda Veloceso is a full-service high-speed product and includes:

- 2+2 seating in Standard Class
    
- 2+1 seating in First Class
    
- Free Wi-Fi
    
- Relatively flexible fares
    
- Dining carriage
    
- Extensive onboard food options
    

ANF's onboard sandwiches have developed a particularly good reputation among passengers.

## Sensinese Rail

The Sensinese state railway operates high-speed services under the **[[Sonik]]** brand.

Sonik offers a full-service product broadly comparable with ANF's Granda Veloceso. Sonik high-speed rolling stock is frequently double-deck.

Although Sensinese Rail is legally permitted to operate purely domestic services within Agacta, Sonik services operating in Agacta generally originate or terminate in Sensin.

### Sonik routes serving Agacta

#### Lojburg–Treslio

**Lojburg – Signaburg – Aulburg – Sandmund – Stototrip – Nleto – Merino – Treslio**

This is one of the principal Sensin–Agacta high-speed services.

#### Neutra–Treslio

**Neutra – Daulburg – Sandmund – Stototrip – Nleto – Merino – Treslio**

Two services operate daily.

[[Neutra]], Sensin's second-largest city, provides interchange with Sensin's West Coast railway, allowing passengers from Agacta to transfer for destinations in western Sensin.

#### An Goma–Uvulp

Sonik operates one daily **An Goma–Uvulp** service covering the Continental East Corridor through Sensin, Agacta and Luum.

#### Lojburg–Lonin

Following the opening of the Setroburg–Nleto HSR in May 2026, Sonik introduced **two daily Lojburg–Lonin services** via Nleto and Setroburg.

## Luum Train

The Luumian state railway operates its high-speed services under the **[[LT-HS]]** brand.

LT-HS provides a full-service product broadly comparable with ANF and Sonik.

Like Sensinese Rail, Luum Train generally uses its access to the Agactan network for services originating or terminating in its home country rather than establishing services solely within Agacta.

LT-HS routes serving Agacta include:

- **Uvulp – Treslio**
    
- **Uvulp – Lojburg**
    
- **Lonin – Setroburg – Treslio**
    
- **Lonin – Setroburg – Nleto – Lojburg**
    

The last of these became possible as a through high-speed service following the May 2026 opening of the Setroburg–Nleto line.

## Agacta Net

**[[Agacta Net]]** is Agacta's principal private general-purpose railway company and the main private competitor to ANF.

Unlike the dedicated low-cost HSR operators, Agacta Net operates across several markets:

- High-speed rail
    
- Conventional long-distance rail
    
- Regional rail
    
- Suburban rail
    

It is the **second-largest high-speed and long-distance operator** in Agacta and the **second-largest regional operator**.

Agacta Net operates on the major Agactan high-speed corridors and is currently the only competitor to ANF on the **Treslio–Orientoport** and **Setroburg–Alaram** high-speed routes. It also plans to compete with ANF on the conventional Setroburg–Kalisto long-distance route.

### High-speed service

Agacta Net's high-speed product combines a conventional full-service onboard offering with some pricing practices associated with low-cost operators.

High-speed trains provide:

- 2+2 seating in Second Class
    
- 2+1 seating in First Class
    
- Dining carriage
    
- Free Wi-Fi of generally good quality
    
- Multiple levels of ticket flexibility
    
- Dynamic pricing
    

Manual seat selection costs **4 Valuto**, slightly more than €1. This compares with approximately 20–30 Valuto on dedicated low-cost carriers, while ANF provides seat selection without an additional charge.

### Regional and suburban services

Agacta Net competes for government tenders to operate regional and suburban networks.

Onboard facilities and rolling stock vary according to the nature and length of the service rather than following the company's high-speed configuration.

## Low-cost high-speed operators

Three dedicated low-cost high-speed operators have a significant presence in Agacta:

- [[National Express (Sensin)|National Express]]
    
- [[RapiGo]]
    
- [[Aro-Rail]]
    

None operates regional services in Agacta.

Common characteristics of the low-cost sector include restrictive ticket conditions, baggage limits, extensive ancillary charges, the absence of conventional dining carriages and little or no genuine First Class provision.

### National Express

**National Express** is a Sensinese low-cost bus and rail company unrelated to the present-day British company of the same name.

#### Origins

The company originated with an unsuccessful attempt by the British National Express company to enter the Sensinese bus market in **1998**. The venture failed within months and the Sensinese operation and rights to the name in Sensin were acquired by businessman **[[Mateo Geltro]]**.

Geltro is a son of Sensinese property tycoon [[Haroldo Geltro]] and the younger brother of [[Hugo Geltro]], owner of the low-cost airline [[Totaljet]].

Hugo had acquired the fledgling Totaljet in **1996**. The airline, whose name predates Geltro's ownership, had previously concentrated on Sensinese domestic and nearby international flights. Geltro subsequently transformed it into a major transcontinental low-cost airline, drawing heavily upon the business models of Southwest Airlines, Ryanair and easyJet.

Mateo initially worked with his brother at Totaljet before acquiring National Express Sensin and applying a similar low-cost model to bus travel.

Following the liberalisation of the Sensinese passenger railway market in **2007**, National Express entered rail operations. It expanded its high-speed rail operations into Agacta in **2012**.

The Geltro brothers are frequently parodied in Sensinese popular culture for their extreme cost-consciousness and the customer-service reputation of their respective companies. A recurring joke portrays the brothers as admirers of Ryanair chief executive Michael O'Leary.

#### Advertising

National Express became particularly well known in Sensin for inexpensive television advertising using part of **"National Express" by The Divine Comedy**.

The advertisements repeatedly use the section in which "National Express" is sung while low fares appear as graphics on screen.

The song had previously been little known in Sensin, with the result that many Sensinese people incorrectly believe the extract was created as the company's advertising jingle.

The advertising style was subsequently introduced in Agacta.

#### Agactan branding

The unqualified **National Express** name presented a potential political problem when the Sensinese company entered Agacta.

Because the company originated in Sensin, describing its Agactan operations simply as "National Express" could be interpreted as suggesting that Sensin and Agacta formed part of the same nation.

The company therefore uses different branding in the Agactan market:

- **Agactan National Express** for services operating wholly within Agacta
    
- **International Express** for international services
    

#### Onboard product

National Express pursues an especially high-density, highly unbundled low-cost model.

Its high-speed trains are **single-deck** and use **3+2 seating**.

Characteristics include:

- Restricted baggage allowance
    
- Additional charges for excess baggage
    
- Paid manual seat selection
    
- Paid Wi-Fi
    
- Very limited ticket flexibility on basic fares
    
- Higher charges for flexible tickets
    
- Minimal onboard staffing
    
- No conventional staffed station help desks
    
- Customer support concentrated online
    
- No dining carriage
    
- Vending machines for food and drink
    
- High vending-machine prices
    
- No quiet carriage
    
- Bright onboard lighting
    
- Frequent and relatively loud announcements
    
- Rapid train turnarounds
    

The rapid turnaround and limited-service model has contributed to frequent passenger complaints about rubbish remaining around seats and the cleanliness of toilets.

National Express does not provide a conventional premium First Class. Its nominal First Class primarily provides **seat selection, increased baggage allowance and greater ticket flexibility**, rather than substantially different onboard accommodation.

Passengers sometimes joke that the bright lighting and frequent announcements are deliberately intended to prevent passengers sleeping so that they purchase more products from the vending machines.

## RapiGo

**[[RapiGo]]** is an Agactan low-cost high-speed operator identifiable by its **green livery**.

It follows a low-cost model but attempts to provide a less austere onboard experience than National Express.

Its high-speed product includes:

- 2+2 seating
    
- Double-deck rolling stock on some/all services
    
- Free Wi-Fi, although speeds can be relatively slow
    
- One suitcase and one backpack included
    
- Restricted ticket flexibility
    
- No conventional First Class
    
- No dining carriage
    
- Vending machines for food and drink
    

RapiGo concentrates overwhelmingly on the Agactan domestic market and does not operate extensively elsewhere on the continent. Some services extend beyond Agacta to nearby cross-border destinations such as **Sandmund** in Sensin.

## Aro-Rail

**[[Aro-Rail]]** is a Flymese low-cost high-speed operator and one of National Express's principal competitors in the continental low-cost rail market.

Its product includes:

- 2+2 seating
    
- Single or double-deck trains depending upon the route and service
    
- One suitcase and one backpack included
    
- Restricted ticket flexibility
    
- No conventional First Class
    
- No dining carriage
    
- Paid Wi-Fi
    
- Onboard trolley service for food and drink
    

Unlike the primarily domestic RapiGo, Aro-Rail operates extensively in the continental international low-cost market.

## Destinia

**[[Destinia]]** is an Agactan regional passenger railway operator.

It is the **third-largest regional operator in Agacta**, behind ANF and Agacta Net, and operates services awarded through competitive regional tenders.

Destinia uses a **white and sky-blue livery** and does not operate Agactan high-speed services.
