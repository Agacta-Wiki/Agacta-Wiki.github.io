# Vehicle Registration Plates in Agacta

Vehicle registration plates in [[Agacta]] use a nationally unified system in which registrations are **randomly allocated and contain no geographical or chronological information**. The current system was introduced in **2005**, replacing an older province-based format.

## Current system

The standard registration format is:

**`123 BC BC`**

For example:

**`753 TN ZP`**

The registration consists of:

- a three-digit numerical block;
- two consonants;
- a second pair of consonants.

Registrations are **completely randomly allocated**. Motorists cannot request a particular registration or choose from available combinations. Consequently, the registration itself reveals neither where a vehicle was registered nor the identity or location of its owner.

Only **consonants** are used in the letter positions.

## Prohibited combinations

Because the two letter blocks effectively create a sequence of four consonants, the system has the potential to generate words, abbreviations and suggestive combinations by accident.

The registration authority therefore maintains an extensive list of **prohibited combinations**. Potential registrations are automatically screened before allocation.

The filtering system considers offensive, obscene, extremist, politically sensitive and otherwise controversial combinations in a range of languages. These include:

- [[Ido]];
- [[Esperanto]];
- Luumian;
- English;
- French;
- other languages considered relevant by the registration authority.

The process is considerably more extensive than simply maintaining a list of individual prohibited two-letter codes. The four consonants are also considered together.

For example, a registration such as:

**`123 FV CK`**

would be prohibited because of the obvious reading created by the combined letter sequence.

The list is periodically revised as new slang, abbreviations and potentially problematic meanings are identified.

## Plate appearance

Standard Agactan plates have **black characters on a white background**.

Unlike systems in which different colours are used at the front and rear, Agactan vehicles use white plates at **both ends**.

A thin **blue outline** runs around the edge of the plate. This is a characteristic feature of Agactan registrations and is comparable to the coloured outlines found on some Austrian and Danish registration plates.

### National sidebar

A **yellow national sidebar** may optionally be fitted on the left side of the plate.

The sidebar contains the [[Flag of Agacta]], represented by its horizontal:

- dark blue;
- red;
- sky blue

tricolour.

Below the flag are the national vehicle identification letters:

**AG**

The letters are printed in black.

The sidebar is optional rather than an integral part of the registration itself. Vehicles may therefore be encountered with either the yellow **AG** strip or a plain white plate without it.

## Capacity and future series

The use of three digits combined with four consonants gives the current system a very large number of possible registrations. Even after prohibited and reserved combinations are removed, the system has considerably more capacity than the total number of vehicles registered in Agacta.

The 2005 format was also designed so that exhaustion of the initial series would **not require an entirely new registration system**.

When the original arrangement eventually approaches exhaustion, the registration authority can begin a new series by changing the positions of the existing numerical and letter blocks, in a manner comparable to the successive registration series used in the Netherlands and Portugal.

A possible progression is:

**First series**  
`123 BC BC`

**Second series**  
`BC 123 BC`

**Third series**  
`BC BC 123`

Further arrangements could be introduced if eventually required.

This allows the basic visual identity of Agactan registration plates to remain unchanged while providing several generations of additional combinations. Because the Agactan system contains a three-digit block, individual series are expected to remain usable for particularly long periods.

## Pre-2005 system

Before 2005, Agacta used the format:

**`AB BC 123`**

The first two letters represented the **province in which the vehicle was originally registered**.

Examples included:

| Code | Province / city |
| --- | --- |
| **TR** | [[Treslio]] |
| **TS** | [[Treslio]], after the TR series was exhausted |
| **NL** | [[Nleto]] |
| **SE** | [[Setroburg]] |
| **ME** | [[Merino]] |
| **AB** | [[Abeldrip]] |

Additional codes existed for the other Agactan provinces.

Once the available registrations under a provincial prefix became insufficient, an additional prefix could be assigned, as occurred when **TS** supplemented the original **TR** designation for Treslio.

The old system therefore allowed anyone familiar with Agactan registration codes to determine approximately where a vehicle had originally been registered.

## 2005 reform

The provincial system was abolished in **2005**.

Several considerations influenced the reform.

### Privacy

Removing the provincial prefix meant that a registration no longer publicly identified the vehicle's place of registration.

This became increasingly desirable as vehicle ownership, commuting and internal migration made the relationship between a vehicle and its original province less meaningful.

### Vehicle resale

Provincial registrations could affect the perception and resale of vehicles outside their original area.

A nationally neutral registration removed this distinction. A vehicle registered in Treslio, Nleto or a small provincial town would receive a registration drawn from exactly the same national pool.

### Registration capacity

Moving from numerous provincial sequences to a single national allocation system also created a much larger and more efficiently usable pool of registrations.

### Visual break with the old system

The order of the registration was deliberately changed from:

**`AB BC 123`**

to:

**`123 BC BC`**

This ensured that new registrations were immediately distinguishable from those issued under the provincial system.

Rather than merely removing the geographical meaning from an otherwise identical plate, the reform gave post-2005 registrations a visibly new identity.

## Older registrations

Vehicles registered before the 2005 reform may retain their original provincial registrations, making the old format an increasingly recognisable feature of older Agactan vehicles.

Consequently, the two formats represent distinct eras of Agactan motoring:

**Pre-2005:**  
`AB BC 123`  
Provincial registration system.

**2005–present:**  
`123 BC BC`  
Random national registration system.

Over time, future rearrangements of the national format may similarly allow people familiar with the system to identify the approximate **registration era** of a vehicle, while still revealing no province or other personal information about its owner.
