# SLC Match Types and Rules

## Overview

**Super League Combat (SLC)** presents professional wrestling as a worked form of competitive combat in which the aim is to make matches feel believable, coherent, and consequential.

SLC does not restrict wrestlers to one house style. Technical wrestling, strong style, high flying, heavyweight wrestling, hardcore wrestling, and hybrid styles are all encouraged.

What matters is that the match tells a logical story and that the rules, damage, and finish feel credible within the context of the bout.

SLC also avoids building its major-event calendar around mandatory gimmick matches. Stipulations are introduced when a feud or competitive situation naturally calls for them.

---

## Standard Singles Matches

A standard singles match is contested one-on-one.

Victory can normally be achieved by:

- pinfall;
- submission;
- count-out;
- disqualification;
- referee stoppage.

SLC does not require matches to end with a recognised finishing move.

A match may end through:

- a roll-up;
- a suplex;
- a clothesline;
- a strike;
- a submission;
- a high-impact move;
- another credible match-ending sequence.

Finishers are treated as high-percentage moves rather than automatic match-ending devices.

---

## Tag Team Matches

Standard tag-team matches are normally contested two-on-two.

Legal tagging rules are generally enforced, with only the legal wrestler permitted to score a fall.

SLC treats established teams as specialist units rather than simply pairing singles wrestlers together.

Tag-team chemistry, experience, and coordination are therefore considered important competitive advantages.

---

## Triple Threat Matches

SLC generally uses **elimination rules** for triple threat matches.

The match continues until two wrestlers have been eliminated by pinfall or submission.

This prevents a wrestler from winning without directly defeating the remaining competition.

In championship matches, elimination rules also ensure that a champion cannot normally lose a title without being pinned or submitted.

---

## Multi-Person Matches

Multi-person matches are relatively uncommon in SLC.

The promotion generally prefers simpler competitive structures such as:

- singles matches;
- tag-team matches;
- elimination triple threats;
- tournaments.

Four-way and larger multi-person matches can occur, but they are treated as special attractions rather than routine booking tools.

Traditional battle royals are also uncommon.

This reflects both SLC's sporting philosophy and the preferences of many traditional Agactan wrestling fans.

---

## Match Length

Most SLC matches are intended to remain within a realistic competitive length.

Matches lasting more than **30 minutes** are extremely rare.

SLC treats fatigue and accumulated damage seriously. A wrestler who has spent 20 or 25 minutes absorbing sustained offence should visibly show the effects.

Very long matches are therefore reserved for exceptional circumstances and are expected to reflect genuine physical deterioration rather than allowing wrestlers to continue indefinitely without consequence.

---

## False Finishes

SLC avoids excessive false finishes.

Major matches may contain convincing near-falls, but repeated kick-outs from major moves are used sparingly.

A match is not expected to escalate into a sequence of numerous finishers followed by repeated kick-outs.

The aim is to preserve the credibility of both finishing moves and ordinary offence.

---

# Special Match Types

## Steel Siege

**Steel Siege** is SLC's major team cage match.

It is broadly comparable to traditional multi-team or faction cage warfare matches, but in SLC it is treated as a rare feud-driven stipulation.

Steel Siege is not tied to a particular annual event.

It is generally used when a rivalry between teams or factions has escalated to the point where normal tag-team matches are no longer sufficient.

The match is intended to feel dangerous, chaotic, and decisive.

---

## Hardcore Ascent

**Hardcore Ascent** is SLC's major ladder-based hardcore match.

Tables, ladders, chairs, and other legal weapons can be used during the match.

Victory normally requires climbing a ladder to retrieve a suspended object or championship.

Hardcore Ascent is not an annual obligation and is only used when the rivalry or championship situation justifies the increased danger.

---

## No Escape

**No Escape** is SLC's most restrictive enclosed-cage match.

The structure is designed to prevent outside interference and keep the participants physically contained.

The match is normally reserved for particularly intense rivalries where escape from the conflict itself has become part of the story.

No Escape is considered one of SLC's most serious stipulations.

---

## Hardcore Match

A **Hardcore Match** removes most standard restrictions on weapons and outside-the-ring combat.

Falls can generally be decided through pinfall or submission under relaxed rules.

Hardcore matches are part of SLC but do not define the promotion.

They are normally used when a rivalry, wrestler, or storyline naturally calls for a more violent environment.

---

## Steel Cage Match

Traditional steel cage matches are used as feud-ending or containment matches.

Unlike Steel Siege, a standard steel cage match usually involves one-on-one competition.

The cage is intended to:

- keep outside interference away;
- force rivals to settle the issue directly;
- create a decisive environment.

SLC does not treat the cage as a routine spectacle.

---

## Two-out-of-Three Falls

Two-out-of-three falls matches are highly respected within SLC.

The first wrestler to win two falls wins the match.

Falls can normally occur by pinfall or submission.

The stipulation is particularly suited to:

- technical rivalries;
- evenly matched opponents;
- championship rematches;
- feuds where one match has not provided a decisive answer.

The format fits SLC's preference for sporting escalation over arbitrary gimmicks.

---

## Submission Match

A Submission Match can only be won by forcing the opponent to submit.

The format is generally used for technical wrestlers, specialists, or rivalries centred around proving superior wrestling ability.

---

## Ladder Match

Traditional ladder matches can be used independently of Hardcore Ascent.

A ladder match is generally more focused on the climb and the championship or object suspended above the ring, while Hardcore Ascent allows a broader and more violent collection of weapons.

---

## Iron Man and Round-Based Matches

Iron Man or round-based matches can occasionally be used when the story calls for a prolonged competitive test.

These matches are rare because of SLC's preference for shorter, more physically realistic bouts.

When used, the emphasis is on pacing, endurance, strategy, and cumulative damage rather than constant high-impact action.

---

# Championship Rules

## Champion Must Be Defeated

In normal championship matches, the champion must be pinned or submitted to lose the title.

This principle is especially important in multi-person matches, where elimination rules are normally used.

SLC avoids situations where a champion loses a championship simply because another challenger was pinned.

---

## Second Challenge Bet

SLC uses a rule known as the **Second Challenge Bet**.

If a wrestler challenges a champion and loses, they cannot challenge that same champion for the same championship again during the current reign unless they put something meaningful of their own on the line.

This effectively turns the second challenge into a wager.

Possible stakes include:

- hair;
- mask;
- career;
- stable membership;
- leadership of a stable;
- being forced to join a stable;
- being forced to leave a stable;
- another personally meaningful condition.

The rule is intended to prevent repetitive rematches and make second challenges feel more significant.

---

## Contender Zero

The winner of the **Contender Zero Tournament** becomes **Contender Zero**.

Contender Zero receives one championship opportunity that can be used for any SLC championship.

The holder must formally declare:

- which championship they are challenging for;
- which Fire, Boom, or major event will host the match.

The opportunity must be invoked at least **24 hours in advance**.

This prevents immediate post-match cash-ins while still allowing strategic timing.

A heel may wait until a champion appears vulnerable before declaring the opportunity.

A babyface will more commonly announce the challenge openly in advance.

Once the opportunity has been used, Contender Zero status ends.

---

# Disqualifications and Cheating

SLC does not assume that heel wrestlers must cheat to win.

A heel winning cleanly is considered completely normal.

Cheating is used when it fits:

- the wrestler;
- the storyline;
- the stakes;
- the competitive situation.

This allows different heels to feel distinct.

Some may rely on:

- technical superiority;
- power;
- speed;
- experience;
- intimidation.

Others may use:

- distraction;
- illegal weapons;
- manager interference;
- hidden rule-breaking.

Because cheating is not automatic, significant cheating incidents carry greater narrative weight.

---

# Interference

Outside interference is generally treated seriously.

If interference occurs, there should normally be a credible reason why the referee:

- did not see it;
- could not prevent it;
- was distracted;
- was incapacitated.

SLC avoids routine interference that makes referees appear incompetent.

---

# Referee Stoppages

Referees may stop a match when a wrestler is unable to intelligently defend themselves or when continuing would present an unreasonable risk.

This can occur without a formal submission.

Referee stoppages are particularly relevant in:

- submission-heavy matches;
- strong-style matches;
- matches involving significant accumulated damage.

---

# Match Philosophy

SLC's guiding principle is that professional wrestling should feel believable even when the audience understands that it is worked.

The promotion therefore aims to avoid:

- excessive false finishes;
- constant finisher kick-outs;
- unnecessarily long matches;
- meaningless multi-person matches;
- stipulations used only because of the calendar;
- automatic heel cheating;
- overcomplicated finishes.

The goal is not to imitate real combat exactly.

The goal is to make the audience believe, for the duration of the match, that what they are watching has logic, consequence, and competitive meaning.
