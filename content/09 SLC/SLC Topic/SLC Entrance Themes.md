# SLC Entrance Themes

SLC uses a deliberately eclectic entrance-music style, balancing British and American alternative music with hip-hop, classic rock, pop-punk, international music and occasional comedy themes. The overall approach is intended to feel curated rather than genre-locked, with songs chosen to suit individual characters rather than following a standard wrestling-rock formula.

The soundtrack has a strong transatlantic identity while generally avoiding nu metal and overly generic wrestling music. Some themes are deliberately unusual or ironic, helping give SLC a slightly offbeat, John Peel-style musical identity.

## Custom SLC Recordings

SLC has booked studio time for Lemonbeck to record two comedy entrance themes specifically for the promotion.

### The Bannons in Pyjamas

The Bannons use a custom parody of the *Bananas in Pyjamas* theme. The central lyric is changed to:

**"The Bannons in Pyjamas..."**

Lemonbeck performs the recording rather than SLC simply using the original children's television theme.

### Prince Barry

Prince Barry uses a custom *Prince Ali*-style parody built around the lyric:

**"Prince Barry, fabulous he..."**

The song is again performed by Lemonbeck, giving Prince Barry a deliberately excessive mock-royal entrance.

The joke works best if Lemonbeck treats both recordings as serious studio performances rather than intentionally singing them badly.

## Musical Direction

SLC's entrance music is intended to sound like a playlist assembled by someone with broad alternative-music tastes rather than a conventional wrestling music department.

The current mix includes British and Irish alternative music, American grunge and alternative rock, hip-hop, pop-punk, classic rock, international music and deliberately comic selections. Artists represented include The Prodigy, Black Grape, Foo Fighters, Coolio, U2, Ween, Pearl Jam, Fall Out Boy, OK Go, Beady Eye, Franz Ferdinand, Muse, The White Stripes, Juanes and Wes.

Not every wrestler requires a licensed song immediately. Generic music can be used temporarily until a more distinctive theme naturally emerges for the character.
