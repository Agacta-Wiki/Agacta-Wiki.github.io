# SLC Dojo

## Overview

The **SLC Dojo** is the training and developmental facility of **Super League Combat (SLC)**.

It is located inside one of the former hangars of the SLC Arena complex in **Treslio, Agacta**.

The Dojo exists to prepare wrestlers for SLC competition, improve the skills of developing talent, and provide established wrestlers with a permanent training base.

It is not a separate promotion, television brand, or developmental show.

Its purpose is simple:

**train wrestlers until they are ready for SLC.**

---

## Location

The Dojo occupies a converted airport hangar within the wider SLC Arena complex.

The large open space made the hangar suitable for conversion into a permanent wrestling and conditioning facility.

Its location also allows trainees to work close to:

- the main SLC Arena;
- company offices;
- production staff;
- creative staff;
- active roster members.

This proximity is considered an important part of the developmental system.

Prospects are not training in an isolated facility several hours away from the promotion.

They are training inside the same building where SLC operates.

---

## Facilities

The SLC Dojo includes:

- two wrestling rings;
- strength-training equipment;
- cardio equipment;
- conditioning areas;
- mat-training space;
- film-study facilities;
- recovery and treatment areas;
- basic promo and interview practice space.

The two-ring layout allows different groups to train simultaneously.

One ring may be used for technical instruction while the other is used for match practice, drills, or conditioning.

---

## Purpose

The SLC Dojo is primarily intended for:

- inexperienced wrestlers;
- local independent talent;
- wrestlers signed to developmental agreements;
- performers transitioning into professional wrestling from another sport;
- wrestlers who need additional training before appearing regularly on television.

Established roster members may also use the facility.

This can include wrestlers:

- returning from injury;
- adapting to a new wrestling style;
- working on conditioning;
- practising matches;
- helping train younger talent.

---

## Developmental Philosophy

SLC does not expect every new signing to appear on television immediately.

A wrestler may spend weeks or months in the Dojo before making a regular appearance on Fire or Boom.

The length of time depends on the individual.

Some wrestlers may already have significant independent experience and require only limited preparation.

Others may need substantial work on:

- ring safety;
- timing;
- selling;
- conditioning;
- match structure;
- television awareness;
- promo delivery;
- character presentation.

There is no fixed graduation date.

A wrestler moves forward when the training staff and SLC management believe they are ready.

---

## Preparing Wrestlers for Television

Training at the Dojo goes beyond learning wrestling moves.

Prospects are also taught how to operate within a televised wrestling promotion.

This includes:

- working within time limits;
- communicating with referees;
- responding to production cues;
- understanding camera positioning;
- adapting matches for television;
- conducting interviews;
- improvising promos;
- presenting a consistent character.

SLC places particular importance on wrestlers feeling comfortable speaking naturally.

Promos are generally not intended to be memorised word-for-word.

The Dojo therefore encourages performers to understand their character and motivation rather than simply reciting dialogue.

---

## Match Structure

SLC's in-ring philosophy is introduced during training.

Prospects are encouraged to make matches feel competitive and believable.

Training emphasises:

- selling accumulated damage;
- logical transitions;
- credible finishes;
- avoiding unnecessary false finishes;
- protecting major moves;
- pacing matches appropriately;
- making different wrestling styles feel distinct.

A trainee may specialise in technical wrestling, high flying, strong style, heavyweight wrestling, hardcore wrestling, or another style.

The Dojo does not attempt to make every wrestler perform the same type of match.

---

## Character Development

The SLC Dojo is also used to help developing wrestlers establish their identity.

This can involve experimenting with:

- ring names;
- entrance presentation;
- gear;
- promo style;
- personality;
- character motivation;
- wrestling style.

SLC generally prefers to develop a promising character rather than completely replace it after one unsuccessful appearance.

If a performer is not connecting, the first response is normally to determine what needs improving.

This may involve changing:

- presentation;
- motivation;
- entrance;
- promo style;
- alignment;
- relationships with other wrestlers.

A complete gimmick change is generally considered a last resort.

---

## Creative Involvement

The SLC creative team may observe Dojo sessions or review training footage when considering future television plans.

Different members of the creative team naturally focus on different areas.

**Lemonbeck** may look at:

- character;
- presentation;
- promo potential;
- star quality.

**Limebeck** may focus on:

- technical ability;
- realism;
- match structure;
- sporting credibility.

**Kiana Martins** may focus on:

- originality;
- crowd connection;
- unusual wrestling styles;
- younger or unconventional performers.

**Tropicbeck** may focus on:

- personality;
- emotional presence;
- character consistency;
- overall atmosphere.

**Jim Barry** generally takes the broader view of whether a wrestler is ready to contribute to SLC as a whole.

---

## Assessments

Dojo prospects are assessed periodically.

These assessments may consider:

- ring safety;
- conditioning;
- match quality;
- character development;
- promo ability;
- reliability;
- ability to work with different opponents.

There is no single scoring system that automatically determines promotion.

Training staff provide recommendations to SLC management about whether a wrestler is ready for:

- further training;
- non-televised matches;
- Boom appearances;
- regular SLC television.

---

## Relationship with Boom

**Boom** can provide an early opportunity for developing wrestlers to appear before a broader audience.

However, Boom is **not** SLC's developmental show.

It remains the promotion's wrestling-focused secondary programme.

A Dojo prospect may appear on Boom because the programme provides:

- more ring time;
- shorter stories;
- showcase matches;
- opportunities to work with established wrestlers.

An appearance on Boom does not necessarily mean a wrestler has permanently joined the main roster.

Similarly, established wrestlers regularly compete on Boom.

---

## Debuts

When SLC decides that a Dojo prospect is ready for television, the objective is to give them the best possible chance to succeed.

New wrestlers are not deliberately placed in situations designed to embarrass them or test whether their character will fail.

Their first appearances are normally structured to establish:

- who they are;
- how they wrestle;
- what makes them different;
- why the audience should care.

If the character does not immediately connect, the promotion may adjust the presentation rather than abandon the wrestler after one appearance.

---

## Training Culture

The Dojo encourages interaction between developing talent and members of the active SLC roster.

Experienced wrestlers may:

- give advice;
- watch training matches;
- help with particular styles;
- practise with younger wrestlers;
- offer feedback after sessions.

This allows younger talent to learn from wrestlers with very different backgrounds and philosophies.

It also helps prevent the Dojo from becoming disconnected from the promotion it serves.

---

## Style Diversity

SLC actively encourages different wrestling traditions within the Dojo.

Training may draw from:

- technical wrestling;
- strong style;
- lucha libre;
- British wrestling;
- North American professional wrestling;
- hardcore wrestling;
- high flying;
- heavyweight wrestling.

The aim is not to create a single "SLC style".

Instead, wrestlers are expected to develop a style that fits their strengths while still understanding SLC's broader emphasis on believable competition and coherent storytelling.

---

## Use by the Main Roster

The Dojo remains available to wrestlers after they reach the main roster.

Regular SLC performers may use it for:

- conditioning;
- rehabilitation;
- match preparation;
- learning unfamiliar techniques;
- working with future opponents;
- helping train developing talent.

This makes the Dojo part of SLC's everyday operation rather than simply a school for beginners.

---

## Importance to SLC

The SLC Dojo is central to the promotion's long-term plan to develop its own wrestlers.

SLC uses established stars and industry veterans, but does not want to depend entirely on talent made famous elsewhere.

The Dojo provides a pathway for new wrestlers to move from:

**training → development → opportunity → regular SLC competition**

without requiring the creation of a separate developmental promotion.

It reflects one of SLC's core ambitions:

**to build new stars rather than simply collect existing ones.**
