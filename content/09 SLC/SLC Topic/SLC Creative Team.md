# SLC Creative Team

## Overview

The **Super League Combat (SLC)** creative team is responsible for the promotion's long-term storylines, match planning, character development, roster direction, and overall television presentation.

The core creative team consists of:

- **Lemonbeck** — Head Booker
- **Jim Barry** — SLC President and senior creative adviser
- **Limebeck** — Creative Team
- **Kiana Martins** — Creative Team
- **Tropicbeck** — Creative Team

The group deliberately contains several different wrestling philosophies.

Rather than attempting to make everyone agree on what professional wrestling should be, SLC uses those differences to create a broader product.

The central creative principle is that SLC should combine:

- long-term storytelling;
- believable wrestling;
- strong characters;
- unpredictability;
- stylistic variety;
- an underground atmosphere;
- enough structure that stories remain coherent.

The creative team plans major storylines well in advance but allows wrestlers considerable freedom to improvise dialogue, develop mannerisms, and contribute ideas to their own characters.

# Lemonbeck

**Role:** Head Booker

Lemonbeck is responsible for the day-to-day creative direction of SLC.

He took over primary booking duties after Jim Barry became increasingly occupied with his responsibilities as SLC president and his wider work with **Invictus Sport**.

His appointment also coincided with SLC becoming slightly more chaotic, colourful, and unpredictable.

## Background

Lemonbeck grew up as a major WCW fan during the Monday Night Wars.

The end of WCW had a lasting effect on his view of professional wrestling and helped create his fascination with:

- wrestling promotions as distinct worlds;
- ambitious presentation;
- factions;
- major characters;
- long-form television storytelling.

**Eric Bischoff** is one of his strongest wrestling influences.

However, Lemonbeck is equally interested in narrative television.

Series built around long-term mysteries, evolving relationships, major twists, and interconnected character arcs strongly influence the way he approaches wrestling storytelling.

## Booking Philosophy

Lemonbeck believes wrestling should be entertaining before it becomes anything else.

He is particularly interested in:

- long-term story arcs;
- memorable characters;
- strong promos;
- factions and alliances;
- managers;
- major reveals;
- dramatic visual moments;
- unusual character combinations;
- wrestlers who can tell stories both in and outside the ring.

He likes twists, but strongly dislikes twists that exist purely for shock value.

A surprise should make the audience rethink what they have already seen rather than make previous episodes meaningless.

## Presentation

Lemonbeck enjoys strong visual presentation and wants wrestlers to look distinctive.

He frequently works with developing performers on:

- entrances;
- clothing;
- presentation;
- character voice;
- promo delivery;
- identifying what makes them different from everybody else.

He believes SLC should retain its rough-edged underground atmosphere even as the company grows.

Unlike some of his earlier creative instincts, he no longer believes SLC should become polished enough to resemble a mainstream corporate wrestling product.

The abandoned-airport atmosphere is now considered one of the company's most valuable assets.

## Wrestling Style

Lemonbeck does not have a strong preference between:

- technical wrestling;
- high flying;
- heavyweight wrestling;
- hardcore wrestling;
- strong style.

His main requirement is that the wrestler can tell a story and connect with the audience.

He is particularly fond of opening major shows with fast-paced matches that immediately energise the crowd.

## Celebrity Involvement

Lemonbeck is open to celebrity involvement when there is a natural connection to wrestling's audience.

He is more interested in:

- alternative rock musicians;
- rappers;
- athletes;
- skateboarders;
- combat sports personalities;
- other figures with obvious crossover appeal.

He is much less interested in celebrities appearing solely because they are famous.

## Other Promotions

Lemonbeck previously had a stronger instinct toward wrestling promotional rivalry.

Under the current SLC philosophy, that instinct has been deliberately moderated.

SLC does not consider itself "at war" with WWE, AEW, TNA, or any other promotion.

The company's view is:

> Other promotions do their thing. SLC does its thing.

Lemonbeck now channels his competitive instincts into making SLC more entertaining rather than taking shots at rival companies.

## Strengths

Lemonbeck is particularly strong at:

- identifying major storyline hooks;
- creating memorable television moments;
- developing factions;
- recognising star potential;
- improving character presentation;
- building layered stories over several months.

## Weaknesses

Left completely unchecked, Lemonbeck can:

- become too interested in spectacle;
- add one twist too many;
- prioritise a memorable moment over competitive logic;
- attempt to fit too many stories onto one show.

The rest of the creative team frequently acts as a filter for these instincts.

# Jim Barry

**Role:** SLC President / Senior Creative Adviser

Jim Barry originally played the largest role in SLC's creative direction.

As the promotion and **Invictus Sport** expanded, his administrative workload increased and he gradually handed day-to-day booking responsibility to Lemonbeck.

Barry remains involved in major creative decisions and serves as the group's principal mediator.

## Background

Barry has one of the broadest wrestling backgrounds on the creative team.

He originally became a fan during the WWF New Generation era, with wrestlers such as:

- Bret Hart;
- Shawn Michaels;
- The Undertaker

among his early favourites.

During the Monday Night Wars he followed both WWF and WCW.

During the early 2000s his interests increasingly shifted toward:

- TNA;
- ROH;
- independent wrestling;
- ECW;
- older territorial wrestling.

He also developed a particular interest in Southern wrestling traditions.

Barry later trained as a professional wrestler and worked intermittently on the independent scene while balancing wrestling with other employment and personal responsibilities.

Although he never became a major wrestling star, the experience gave him an understanding of wrestling from both the performer and promoter perspective.

## Philosophy

Barry's key word is **balance**.

He believes a strong wrestling promotion should contain:

- technical wrestling;
- high flying;
- heavyweight wrestling;
- hardcore wrestling;
- strong characters;
- serious promos;
- occasional comedy;
- long-term stories.

He dislikes the idea that every match on a card must attempt to be an epic.

An intense match followed by another intense match followed by another intense match can exhaust an audience.

Barry therefore believes strongly in **card flow**.

Lighter matches, unusual personalities, and lower-card comedy can serve as palate cleansers between major story beats.

## Long-Term Storytelling

Barry particularly enjoys long babyface journeys.

He would rather see a future champion:

- fail;
- learn;
- recover;
- develop;
- eventually reach the title

than simply become champion immediately because the audience likes them.

This philosophy strongly influences wrestlers such as **Dante Navarro**.

## Match Structure

Barry generally prefers:

- singles matches;
- standard tag-team matches;
- simple competitive structures.

He believes multi-person and heavily gimmicked matches become more effective when they are relatively rare.

A cage, Hardcore Ascent, Steel Siege, or other unusual match should feel like an escalation.

## Leadership Style

Barry is democratic.

He expects creative meetings to involve disagreement and generally encourages everyone to defend their ideas.

He does not expect his creative team to share the same wrestling tastes.

His main concern is whether the final product feels like SLC.

Although he no longer books every episode himself, Barry can intervene when:

- a major long-term storyline is being decided;
- significant money is involved;
- a major signing is being discussed;
- an idea could fundamentally change SLC's identity.

## On Show Days

When Barry is not required on camera, he can often be found around **gorilla position**, production, or other parts of the SLC Arena overseeing the operation of the show.

# Limebeck

**Role:** Creative Team / Sporting Structure

Limebeck is the strongest advocate for treating professional wrestling as a legitimate sporting contest.

## Background

Limebeck is an obsessive sports fan.

His interests extend well beyond wrestling and include major international sports leagues and competitions.

He frequently looks at how organisations such as:

- football leagues;
- basketball leagues;
- combat sports;
- American sports leagues

present statistics, standings, competition, and major events.

His wrestling influences include traditional wrestling realism and the sport-oriented philosophies associated with figures such as **Jim Cornette** and **Antonio Inoki**.

## Philosophy

Limebeck's key word is **sport**.

He believes wrestling should be presented in a manner similar to a major sporting broadcast.

This includes:

- rankings;
- statistics;
- tournament tables;
- contender standings;
- win-loss records;
- injury updates;
- tactical analysis;
- press conferences;
- serious interviews.

He believes championships should normally be earned rather than granted because two wrestlers happened to argue backstage.

## Match Philosophy

Limebeck particularly enjoys:

- technical wrestling;
- strong style;
- submission wrestling;
- logical high flying;
- international wrestling traditions.

He has no objection to spectacular moves when they make sense.

He strongly dislikes situations in which wrestlers visibly cooperate in order to perform an elaborate sequence.

Examples of things he dislikes include:

- wrestlers waiting unnaturally for an opponent to jump onto them;
- someone standing on a ladder for an implausibly long period instead of winning;
- repeated sequences that appear rehearsed rather than competitive.

## Storytelling

Limebeck prefers rivalries to develop through things such as:

- interviews;
- press conferences;
- competitive results;
- championship rankings;
- rivals joining commentary during matches;
- analysis of previous encounters.

He believes a storyline does not need to become unrealistic simply because wrestling is predetermined.

## Tournaments

Limebeck is one of the strongest advocates for:

- leagues;
- tournaments;
- rankings;
- the Grand Super League;
- Contender Zero;
- clearly defined routes to championship opportunities.

He also strongly favours saving major first-time matches for important events rather than giving them away casually.

## International Wrestling

Limebeck regularly advocates incorporating ideas from wrestling cultures outside North America.

He is interested in traditions from:

- Japan;
- Mexico;
- Britain;
- continental Europe;
- other international wrestling scenes.

The goal is not to imitate another promotion but to make SLC's wrestling vocabulary broader.

## Dislikes

Limebeck strongly dislikes:

- cinematic wrestling;
- worked-shoot storylines;
- excessive fourth-wall breaking;
- unexplained title shots;
- meaningless multi-person matches;
- finishes that require referees to behave irrationally.

## Strengths

Limebeck provides:

- logic;
- continuity;
- competitive structure;
- statistical consistency;
- protection against excessive hot-shotting.

## Weaknesses

If left completely unchecked, Limebeck's version of SLC could become:

- overly rigid;
- too serious;
- too dependent on rankings;
- reluctant to embrace creative chaos.

Lemonbeck and Kiana frequently push him to accept that occasionally the most entertaining answer is not the most perfectly sporting one.

# Kiana Martins

**Role:** Creative Team / Innovation and Indie Wrestling

Kiana Martins represents the strongest modern independent-wrestling influence on the creative team.

## Background

Kiana belongs to a younger wrestling generation than the other core members.

While several of the others developed their wrestling tastes during the Monday Night Wars, Kiana became a fan during a period in which WWE had considerably less mainstream competition.

She quickly became interested in wrestling outside the mainstream.

Her early wrestling interests expanded into:

- TNA;
- ROH;
- PWG;
- ECW;
- classic WWF;
- classic WCW;
- independent wrestling.

Over time she developed an unusually broad knowledge of professional wrestling history.

Outside wrestling, Kiana is strongly interested in:

- science fiction;
- comic books;
- video games;
- fantasy.

These interests occasionally influence characters, presentation ideas, and storyline concepts.

## Philosophy

Kiana's key word is **exciting**.

She believes every match should have some consequence.

That consequence does not always need to be enormous.

A match can matter because:

- the winner moves closer to a title;
- the loser falls down the rankings;
- somebody proves they can compete at a higher level;
- a wrestler's confidence changes;
- a rivalry evolves;
- a tag team discovers whether it can function together.

She strongly dislikes completely disposable matches.

## Wrestling Styles

Kiana particularly enjoys:

- high flying;
- technical wrestling;
- hardcore wrestling;
- cruiserweight wrestling;
- unusual hybrid styles.

The X Division and independent wrestling strongly influence her taste.

She is especially attracted to:

- underdogs;
- mavericks;
- eccentric wrestlers;
- performers with obvious individuality.

## Character Philosophy

Kiana believes wrestlers should be encouraged to amplify their own personalities rather than being forced into generic roles.

She frequently advocates for wrestlers who seem:

- unusual;
- awkward;
- unconventional;
- culturally specific;
- difficult to categorise.

Rather than smoothing away those traits, she usually wants to make them part of the attraction.

## Wins and Losses

Kiana agrees strongly with Limebeck that victories and defeats should matter.

However, she is more willing to temporarily bend strict sporting logic if doing so creates an exceptional moment or advances an important story.

This is one of their regular creative disagreements.

## Innovation

Kiana regularly pushes SLC to:

- try new presentation ideas;
- experiment with character concepts;
- incorporate subcultures;
- avoid becoming trapped by wrestling tradition.

At the same time, her strong knowledge of wrestling history means she frequently builds new ideas from older wrestling concepts.

## Strengths

Kiana is particularly strong at:

- identifying underdogs;
- developing younger wrestlers;
- connecting match results to character development;
- recognising indie talent;
- finding unusual character hooks;
- keeping SLC contemporary.

## Weaknesses

If completely unchecked, Kiana can:

- become too enthusiastic about high-risk ideas;
- favour exciting moments over pacing;
- attempt to give too many matches significant consequences;
- push experimental concepts before they are fully developed.

# Tropicbeck

**Role:** Creative Team / Character and Atmosphere

Tropicbeck focuses primarily on character, atmosphere, and long-term emotional development.

## Background

Unlike several other members of the creative team, Tropicbeck does not strongly identify with one wrestling promotion.

His philosophy is simply that every promotion has things worth liking and things worth disliking.

His wrestling influences include:

- Mick Foley;
- Sting;
- Vader;
- The Undertaker;
- Kane;
- Bray Wyatt;
- Vampiro;
- Abyss;
- Paul Bearer.

His wider cultural interests include darker film, television, comic-book, and horror influences.

## Philosophy

Tropicbeck believes every important wrestler should have a strong identifiable personality.

A wrestler should not simply be:

> "the technical one"

or:

> "the high-flyer."

Their wrestling style should be part of a broader character.

He strongly favours characters that can develop gradually over months or years.

## Character Consistency

Tropicbeck dislikes unnecessary gimmick changes.

If a wrestler is not immediately successful, his preference is usually to:

- deepen the character;
- reveal another side of the personality;
- adjust presentation;
- add relationships;
- change motivation.

Completely replacing the character should normally be a last resort.

## Darker Influences

Tropicbeck enjoys:

- psychologically intense characters;
- brooding babyfaces;
- monster heels;
- horror-influenced aesthetics;
- unsettling presentation.

However, SLC's current philosophy places boundaries around these ideas.

Atmosphere can be strange.

Characters can be frightening.

Promos can become psychologically unusual.

The wrestling itself should still feel like wrestling.

SLC therefore avoids cinematic matches or situations where supernatural events fundamentally override the promotion's physical reality.

## Managers

Tropicbeck strongly supports the use of managers.

He believes a manager should not simply speak for someone who cannot talk.

A good manager can become:

- an extension of the wrestler's character;
- a strategist;
- a manipulator;
- a source of conflict;
- a character in their own right.

## Heel Philosophy

Tropicbeck was an early advocate for SLC's current approach to heel victories.

He dislikes the assumption that every villain must cheat.

A dangerous heel becomes more threatening when the audience knows they are capable of simply beating a babyface.

He therefore supports a mixture of:

- clean heel victories;
- cheating;
- interference;
- tactical manipulation

depending on the character and story.

## Wrestling Styles

Tropicbeck particularly enjoys:

- technical wrestling;
- hardcore wrestling;
- powerful heavyweights;
- monster characters.

He is more willing than Limebeck to push violent or unusual match types when the story has reached the appropriate point.

## Strengths

Tropicbeck is especially useful for:

- character consistency;
- atmosphere;
- emotional tone;
- managers;
- monster presentation;
- long-term character arcs.

## Weaknesses

If left completely unchecked, Tropicbeck could:

- make the promotion too dark;
- overuse horror aesthetics;
- lean too heavily toward monster characters;
- push hardcore conclusions too frequently.

He is generally relaxed in creative meetings and does not expect every idea he pitches to be used.

# Creative Meetings

SLC's creative meetings are collaborative.

Lemonbeck runs the meeting as head booker, but major ideas are openly debated.

A typical discussion may involve:

- Lemonbeck asking what creates the best television story;
- Limebeck asking whether the competitive logic makes sense;
- Kiana asking what the result changes for each wrestler;
- Tropicbeck asking whether the characters remain consistent;
- Jim Barry asking whether the entire show flows properly.

The goal is not consensus on every detail.

The different perspectives are considered one of SLC's strengths.

---

# Creative Tensions

The members frequently disagree.

## Lemonbeck and Limebeck

This is perhaps the most obvious creative tension.

Lemonbeck may want:

- a surprise;
- a major angle;
- an unexpected alliance;
- a dramatic reveal.

Limebeck will immediately ask:

> "Why would they do that?"

Their arguments often produce stronger versions of both ideas.

Lemonbeck provides energy.

Limebeck provides logic.

## Limebeck and Kiana

Both believe wins and losses matter.

Their disagreement is usually over how strictly that principle should be applied.

Limebeck favours sporting consistency.

Kiana is more willing to occasionally break the pattern for an exceptional story.

## Lemonbeck and Kiana

Both enjoy unconventional wrestling but approach it differently.

Lemonbeck tends to think in terms of television drama and presentation.

Kiana tends to think in terms of wrestling culture, underdogs, and crowd excitement.

## Tropicbeck and Limebeck

Tropicbeck's more atmospheric ideas can collide with Limebeck's desire for realism.

Their compromise often defines SLC's approach:

**the presentation can become strange, but the match should remain believable.**

## Jim Barry

Barry generally acts as the final filter.

He is particularly likely to intervene when an idea:

- overloads a card;
- burns through a major match too early;
- undermines a wrestler's long-term direction;
- makes SLC feel too much like another promotion.

---

# Controlled Chaos

The current SLC product deliberately contains more unpredictability than the earliest version of the promotion.

This reflects Lemonbeck's influence as head booker and the gradual incorporation of ideas from the company's earlier creative development.

However, the aim is **controlled chaos**.

SLC can feature:

- unexpected partnerships;
- eccentric backstage incidents;
- surprise debuts;
- unusual characters;
- sudden opportunities;
- volatile interviews.

But major developments should still make sense when viewed retrospectively.

The audience should frequently wonder:

> "What is going to happen next?"

They should rarely wonder:

> "Why did that happen?"

---

# SLC Creative Identity

No single member of the creative team completely represents SLC.

If Lemonbeck controlled everything, the promotion could become too chaotic.

If Limebeck controlled everything, it could become too dry.

If Kiana controlled everything, it could become too relentlessly experimental.

If Tropicbeck controlled everything, it could become too dark and specialised.

If Jim Barry controlled everything personally, the promotion could become overly cautious.

SLC's identity comes from the tension between those approaches.

The result is intended to be:

**an underground, character-driven wrestling promotion with believable matches, long-term storytelling, stylistic variety, and just enough chaos that the audience never becomes completely comfortable predicting what will happen next.**
