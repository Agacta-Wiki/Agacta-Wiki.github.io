## James Metcalfe

The trophy is named after [[James Metcalfe]], a British mercenary originally from [[York]], who served with forces employed by [[Luum]] during border skirmishes between Agacta and [[Alaram]] in the 1910s.

Luum, a longstanding ally of Agacta, was involved in supporting Agacta during the period, and Metcalfe was stationed west of [[Setroburg]] as part of this wider military presence.

While in the region, Metcalfe began playing rugby league informally with local Agactans. With conventional rugby league equipment not readily available, early games were played using oval balls normally used for [[Agactan Rules Football]].

The sport first caught on in border towns west of Setroburg before spreading into Setroburg itself. From there, rugby league gradually expanded across Agacta and eventually developed into a major national sport.

Metcalfe later married an Agactan woman and settled permanently in Agacta after his period of mercenary service came to an end.

Following the establishment of the [[Agactan Sports Commission]] in 1928, Metcalfe was offered a position within the new organisation. He subsequently became one of the most influential figures in the development and organisation of rugby league in Agacta and is widely regarded as the **godfather of Agactan rugby league**.

The national championship trophy, the [[Kopa de Metcalfe]], was later named in his honour.
