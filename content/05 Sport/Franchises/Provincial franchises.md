# Provincial franchises

The **provincial franchises** are the highest level of professional sport in Agacta. Each of the country's **19 provinces** (15 mainland provinces and 4 overseas territories) is represented by a single franchise, which competes across multiple sports administered by the [[Agactan Sports Commission]] (ASC).

Unlike most countries, where clubs are organised around individual towns or cities, Agacta's elite sporting system is based on provincial representation. Each franchise serves as the principal sporting representative of its province across all major ASC competitions, including [[Agactan Rules Football]], soccer, rugby league, basketball, baseball and ice hockey.

The franchise model was introduced to ensure every province had representation at the highest level of professional sport while encouraging provincial identity over local rivalries.

---

# Overview

Every provincial franchise has:

- A permanent identity used across all sports.
- Official colours.
- A home sports campus.
- One or more affiliated minor franchises.
- Responsibility for elite player development within its province.

The same franchise name and branding is used regardless of the sport being played.

# Identity

Each franchise possesses a unique name, colour scheme and visual identity intended to reflect the history, geography or culture of its province.

Unlike traditional sports clubs, franchise identities remain consistent across every sport governed by the [[Agactan Sports Commission]]. A supporter of the **Kalisto Falcons**, for example, supports the Falcons in rugby league, soccer, basketball, Agactan Rules Football, baseball and every other ASC competition.

# Cultural significance

The provincial franchises are among the most recognisable institutions in Agactan society.

Support for a franchise is usually inherited through family or place of residence and often forms an important part of provincial identity. Because each province has only one elite franchise, support tends to unite communities rather than divide them along local rivalries.

This model contrasts with the club-based systems found elsewhere in Europe and has frequently been cited as one of the defining characteristics of Agactan sport.
