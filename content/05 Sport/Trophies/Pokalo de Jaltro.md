# Pokalo de Jaltro

The **Pokalo de Jaltro**, commonly known simply as **the Jaltro**, is the championship trophy awarded to the winner of the [[Agactan Rules Football]] Grand Final.

Named after [[Teovaldo Jaltro]], one of the founders of the [[Agactan Sports Commission]] (ASC), the trophy is one of the most prestigious prizes in Agactan sport and a central symbol of the national game.

The Pokalo de Jaltro is a large handled cup, roughly comparable in size to the UEFA Cup and in general form to Ireland's Liam MacCarthy Cup. Within Agactan sporting culture, however, it is overwhelmingly referred to by its shortened name, **the Jaltro**.

Expressions such as **"we're going to win the Jaltro"**, **"the race for the Jaltro"** and **"bringing the Jaltro home"** are common among supporters, players and commentators.

---

# History

## Teovaldo Jaltro

The trophy is named after [[Teovaldo Jaltro]], one of the founders of the [[Agactan Sports Commission]].

Jaltro was an influential figure in the establishment of the ASC and the development of organised sport in Agacta.

The championship trophy of [[Agactan Rules Football]] was named in his honour.

---

# Design

The Pokalo de Jaltro is one of the most recognisable trophies in Agactan sport.

It is a large cup, approximately comparable in overall scale to the UEFA Cup, with prominent handles on either side. Its general silhouette has sometimes been compared to the Liam MacCarthy Cup awarded in Irish hurling.

The trophy's size and distinctive handled design have contributed to its status as one of the principal visual symbols of Agactan sport.

---

# Name and terminology

Although its formal name is the **Pokalo de Jaltro**, the trophy is almost universally known as **the Jaltro** in everyday speech.

The shortened form is used extensively in:

- Television commentary
    
- Newspaper headlines
    
- Supporter chants
    
- Advertising
    
- Season previews
    
- Post-match coverage
    

Common expressions include:

- "The race for the Jaltro"
    
- "Winning the Jaltro"
    
- "Bringing home the Jaltro"
    
- "One game from the Jaltro"
    

The term may also be used more broadly as shorthand for the Agactan Rules championship itself.

---

# Grand Final weekend

The [[Agactan Rules Football Grand Final]] weekend is one of the largest annual occasions in Agactan sport.

The Grand Final is traditionally played at **16:00 on Sunday**, with the wider weekend functioning as a national celebration of sport, volunteering and the [[Agactan Sports Commission]].

Events commonly include:

- Player meet-and-greets
    
- Appearances by former players and sporting legends
    
- Concerts and live performances
    
- Demonstrations of other ASC sports
    
- Gymnastics exhibitions
    
- Community sporting events
    
- Youth activities
    
- Public celebrations
    
- [[Agactan Sports Hall of Fame]] events
    

---

# Hall of Fame weekend

## Induction ceremony

The annual [[Agactan Sports Hall of Fame]] induction ceremony is held on the night before the Grand Final.

The ceremony honours figures from across Agactan sport rather than exclusively from Agactan Rules Football, reflecting the multi-sport character of the ASC.

---

## Presentation during the Grand Final

At the end of the **first quarter**, the newly inducted Hall of Fame members are formally presented to the crowd.

Each inductee is introduced individually by name and appears before the spectators, traditionally waving to and acknowledging the crowd.

The ceremony forms one of the established rituals of Grand Final day.

---

# Journey of the Jaltro

## Handover by the defending champions

On Sunday morning, the Pokalo de Jaltro begins its ceremonial journey to the stadium.

The trophy is traditionally handed over by the captain of the previous year's championship-winning team.

If the captain is unavailable, including in circumstances where the player has transferred to another franchise, another member of the previous year's winning team may perform the handover.

The player formally presents the Jaltro to the **President of the [[Agactan Sports Commission]]**.

---

## Transfer to the Agactan forces

The ASC President then hands the trophy to a member of the [[Agactan Armed Forces]] and formally tasks them with bringing the Jaltro to the stadium.

The handover marks the beginning of the public procession.

---

# Procession to the stadium

The Jaltro is carried towards the Grand Final venue in a major public procession.

The procession frequently includes:

- Marching bands
    
- Musicians
    
- Performers
    
- Community organisations
    
- ASC representatives
    
- Sporting groups
    

Large crowds commonly gather along sections of the route.

---

# Jaltro relay

As the procession approaches the stadium, the trophy passes through a ceremonial relay involving members of the Agactan sporting community.

The atmosphere is often compared to the final stages of an Olympic torch relay.

Participants are generally selected in recognition of exceptional service to sport, volunteering or their communities through the [[Agactan Sports Commission]].

Those selected may include:

- Long-serving youth coaches
    
- Amateur club volunteers
    
- Community fundraisers
    
- Organisers of local sporting programmes
    
- Volunteers recognised for exceptional service
    
- Members of disability and Special Olympics-style sporting teams
    
- Individuals who have made significant contributions to grassroots sport
    

The selected participants relay the Jaltro towards and ultimately into the stadium.

Participation in the relay is widely regarded as a major honour within Agactan sporting culture.

---

# Pre-match ceremonies

The [[President of Agacta]] traditionally attends the Grand Final.

Before the match, the President enters the field and shakes hands with the players of both competing franchises.

The President also traditionally performs the ceremonial toss before the beginning of the match.

---

# Local club recognition

A major part of the Grand Final tradition is the recognition of the amateur clubs that first developed the participating players.

Under the Agactan sporting system, professional players leave their local amateur clubs when they sign with a professional franchise. Nevertheless, a player's first club remains an important part of their sporting identity.

The Grand Final therefore places particular emphasis on the grassroots clubs from which the finalists emerged.

---

# Jaltro plaques

A local amateur club receives a commemorative plaque for every former player who becomes a member of a Jaltro-winning team.

The number of Jaltro winners produced by individual clubs is closely followed within Agactan sporting culture.

In the build-up to and aftermath of the Grand Final, considerable attention is given to questions such as:

- Which local clubs may gain their first Jaltro winner
    
- Which established clubs may add another winner to their historical total
    
- Which grassroots clubs have produced the largest number of champions
    
- Whether a small or previously little-known club will gain national recognition through a former player
    

A club's number of Jaltro-winning members can become a significant source of local pride.

The tradition reinforces the relationship between the professional provincial franchise system and the amateur clubs that form the grassroots foundation of Agactan sport.

---

# Half-time show

Since **2006**, the Agactan Rules Football Grand Final has featured a major half-time show.

The performance takes place during the main interval and has developed into one of the most prominent entertainment events in the Agactan sporting calendar.

---

# Cultural significance

The Pokalo de Jaltro is one of the most prestigious and recognisable prizes in Agactan sport.

The Grand Final weekend combines:

- Elite professional competition
    
- National sporting tradition
    
- ASC institutional ceremony
    
- Community volunteering
    
- Grassroots club identity
    
- Military participation
    
- Live entertainment
    
- Public celebration
    

The ceremonial journey of the trophy is particularly significant. The Jaltro passes from the previous champions to the President of the ASC, then to the Agactan forces, through a public procession, and finally into the hands of recognised members of the sporting community before reaching the stadium.

This journey is widely understood as symbolising the relationship between elite competition and the wider Agactan sporting community.

The plaque tradition similarly ensures that victory is not associated solely with the professional franchise. Every championship-winning player also adds to the history of the local amateur club where their sporting career began.
