# ASC Club Fundraising

The **Agacta Sport Commission (ASC)** views fundraising as an important part of community sport. While clubs receive funding from numerous sources, fundraising provides additional income, strengthens community ties, and allows individual sections to finance projects beyond their core budgets.

Fundraising is considered a normal part of club life throughout Agacta, particularly in smaller towns where residents regularly support local ASC activities.

## Philosophy

The ASC believes that community clubs should have diverse and sustainable funding sources rather than relying on a single income stream.

Fundraising is intended to:

- Support grassroots sport.
- Strengthen links between clubs and their communities.
- Allow individual sections to fund additional activities.
- Encourage volunteering.
- Build community pride and participation.

Most Agactans do not view donations as simply supporting a sports team. Instead, they see themselves as supporting an organisation that provides opportunities for young people, promotes healthy lifestyles, develops volunteers, and serves as a community hub.

## Sources of Club Funding

Most ASC clubs receive income from a combination of:

- ASC development funding.
- Professional franchise solidarity payments.
- National and local government grants.
- National lottery funding.
- Membership fees.
- Sponsorship.
- Ticket sales.
- Club events.
- Facility hire.
- Club lotteries.
- Donations.
- Community fundraising.

The mix varies depending on the size of the club.

## Club-Wide and Section Fundraising

ASC fundraising operates on two levels.

### Club Fundraising

Funds raised benefit the club as a whole and may be used for:

- Clubhouses.
- Playing facilities.
- Floodlights.
- Minibuses.
- Administration.
- Shared equipment.
- General operating costs.

Typical events include:

- Annual raffles.
- Sportsman's dinners.
- Gala evenings.
- Family sports days.
- Christmas draws.
- Community festivals.

### Section Fundraising

Individual sporting sections may also organise fundraisers for specific purposes.

Examples include:

- Tournament travel.
- National Championship participation.
- Equipment.
- Coaching costs.
- Training camps.
- Overseas competitions.

Funds raised are ring-fenced for that section.

Examples:

- "Support the Valaimund ASC Volleyball Team."
- "Help the Water Polo Section reach the National Finals."

## Annual Fundraising Planning

Each ASC club appoints a **Fundraising Committee** (or Community Events Committee) to coordinate fundraising throughout the year.

Towards the end of each season, every sporting section submits:

- Expected expenditure.
- Planned competitions.
- Likely funding requirements.
- Preferred fundraising activities.
- Approximate dates when funding will be required.

The committee then prepares an annual fundraising calendar designed to:

- Avoid clashes.
- Prevent donor fatigue.
- Make the best use of volunteers.
- Coordinate local businesses and sponsors.
- Ensure every section has fair fundraising opportunities.

Rather than simply approving or rejecting requests, the committee works with sections to identify the most suitable fundraising opportunities.

## Annual Fundraising Calendar

Most clubs operate an annual calendar where different sections receive fundraising windows throughout the year.

Example:

| Month | Activity |
|--------|----------|
| February | Swimming Bag Pack |
| March | Basketball Quiz Night |
| April | Club Spring Raffle |
| May | Athletics Fun Run |
| June | Rugby League Car Wash |
| July | Volleyball Barbecue |
| September | Water Polo Bake Sale |
| November | Christmas Draw |

This prevents multiple ASC teams competing for donations at the same time.

For example, a town would not normally have:

- Two rival ASC car washes on the same weekend.
- Two separate raffles within a fortnight.
- Two major "Evening With..." events competing for attendance.

Instead, the committee spaces fundraising events throughout the year.

## Joint Fundraisers

When two or more sections require funding at similar times, the committee may organise a joint fundraiser.

Examples include:

- Joint car washes.
- Combined raffles.
- Shared bag packing.
- Community barbecues.
- Quiz nights.
- Family fun days.

Funds are divided according to an agreed formula before the event.

This reduces volunteer workload while increasing the size and visibility of the fundraiser.

## Extraordinary Fundraisers

Sections may request an **Extraordinary Fundraising Permission** outside their allocated fundraising window.

Typical reasons include:

- Qualification for a National Final.
- Selection for an international tournament.
- Unexpected travel costs.
- Emergency equipment replacement.
- Storm damage to facilities.

These requests are considered individually by the club committee.

Because they celebrate exceptional achievements, communities are generally very supportive of these additional fundraisers.

## Common Fundraising Activities

Fundraising varies depending on the size of the club.

Smaller clubs commonly organise:

- Supermarket bag packing.
- Car washes.
- Bake sales.
- Sponsored walks.
- Quiz nights.
- Local raffles.
- Community barbecues.

Larger clubs are more likely to organise:

- Gala dinners.
- Corporate sponsorship events.
- Sportsman's lunches.
- Major raffles.
- Golf classics.
- Alumni events.
- Large community festivals.

Many clubs combine both approaches.

## Community Support

Throughout the year, residents of an Agactan town regularly encounter different ASC sections raising funds.

It is common to see:

- The swimming section packing bags in March.
- The rugby league under-16s holding a car wash in June.
- The volleyball section selling cakes in September.
- The basketball section running a Christmas raffle.

Supporting these events is considered a normal part of community life.

Most residents contribute small amounts whenever possible, recognising the positive role ASC clubs play in youth development, health, volunteering and community life.

## Professional Athlete Support

Professional athletes are expected to remain connected to their **Club of Origin**.

Many regularly assist fundraising through:

- Autograph sessions.
- "An Evening With..." events.
- Sportsman's dinners.
- Coaching clinics.
- Prize presentations.
- Charity matches.
- Acting as Master of Ceremonies.
- Speaking at awards nights.

This is regarded as giving back to the club that developed them.

For example, a Setroburg Lynx second-row forward who began at Valaimund ASC Klub would be expected to return periodically to assist with club fundraising and community events.

## Fundraising as Community Life

In Agacta, fundraising is not viewed as a sign of financial difficulty.

Instead, it is an important part of the relationship between ASC clubs and their communities.

Residents understand that by buying a raffle ticket, having their shopping packed, attending a barbecue or getting their car washed, they are helping sustain an organisation that provides sporting opportunities, develops young people, and strengthens community life throughout the country.
