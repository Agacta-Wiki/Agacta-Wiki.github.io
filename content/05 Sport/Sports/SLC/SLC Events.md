# SLC Events

## Overview

**Super League Combat (SLC)** uses a regular annual cycle of major events.

Each event has its own atmosphere, seasonal identity, or narrative purpose, but SLC deliberately avoids structuring its calendar around mandatory gimmick matches.

There is no annual "cage show", "ladder show", or "team match show".

Stipulations such as **Steel Siege**, **Hardcore Ascent**, **No Escape**, hardcore matches, ladder matches, or 2-out-of-3 falls matches are used when a feud naturally calls for them.

The regular event cycle is:

1. **April — Ignition**
2. **May — Mayhem**
3. **July — Trial by Combat**
4. **August — Birthday Bash**
5. **September — Dimensional Rift**
6. **November — Total Destruction**
7. **December — Grand Super League Finals**
8. **January — Champion's Way**
9. **March — Retribution**

---

# Ignition

**Month:** April

**Ignition** opens the new SLC season.

The event is intended to establish the direction of the promotion for the months ahead, with new rivalries, championship programmes, returning wrestlers, and emerging contenders often beginning to take shape.

Ignition is not tied to any particular match type.

Its identity comes primarily from being the point at which SLC begins moving forward again after the previous season's conclusion.

# Mayhem

**Month:** May

**Mayhem** begins SLC's annual **Summer of Violence**.

The event normally contains at least one and sometimes two more violent or hardcore matches, although these are not mandatory and must still arise naturally from existing feuds.

Mayhem is not a "hardcore PPV".

Instead, the beginning of the Summer of Violence gives the event a slightly more dangerous and unpredictable atmosphere.

## Summer of Violence

The **Summer of Violence** runs across:

- Mayhem
- Trial by Combat
- Birthday Bash

The concept affects atmosphere and presentation more than match structure.

Violent rivalries, unusual stipulations, and physical escalation are more common during this period, but standard wrestling matches remain the majority of the card.

# Trial by Combat

**Month:** July

**Trial by Combat** is traditionally regarded as SLC's **second-largest event of the year**.

It takes place during the Summer of Violence but is generally presented with a more serious competitive atmosphere than Mayhem or Birthday Bash.

The event often features major championship matches and important long-term rivalry developments.

Its name reflects the idea of wrestlers being tested rather than requiring any particular stipulation.

# Birthday Bash

**Month:** August

**Birthday Bash** is SLC's annual summer celebration and the final event of the **Summer of Violence**.

The show uses a deliberately playful beach-party presentation.

Typical visual elements include:

- Hawaiian shirts;
- beach balls;
- sand;
- summer decorations;
- bright seasonal graphics;
- relaxed backstage presentation.

The visual atmosphere can be light-hearted without making the wrestling itself unserious.

A major championship match at Birthday Bash is still treated with the same competitive importance as it would be at any other SLC event.

Birthday Bash closes the Summer of Violence before the promotion moves into the very different atmosphere of Dimensional Rift.

---

# Dimensional Rift

**Month:** September

**Dimensional Rift** is SLC's annual crossover month and supercard concept.

Rather than functioning simply as a one-night inter-promotional show, Dimensional Rift normally affects several weeks of SLC programming.

External wrestlers, independent performers, and wrestlers representing other promotions can enter the SLC environment with their own motivations, rivalries, and alliances.

The concept is closer to a television-universe crossover than a conventional wrestling supershow.

Stories may develop across several episodes of **Fire** and **Boom** before reaching the Dimensional Rift event.

The month can include:

- outside wrestlers challenging SLC talent;
- unexpected alliances;
- promotion-versus-promotion matches;
- wrestlers pursuing personal grudges rather than representing a company;
- consequences that continue after the crossover ends.

Dimensional Rift is intended to feel unusual and disruptive without making outside wrestlers automatically more important than the regular SLC roster.

## Year One

Dimensional Rift was omitted from SLC's first season and held back until the promotion had developed enough internal history for outside involvement to carry greater significance.

# Total Destruction

**Month:** November

**Total Destruction** is intentionally one of the least rigidly defined events on the SLC calendar.

Its purpose is to provide a major late-year event without forcing the promotion into a predetermined narrative structure.

The majority of the card consists of normal SLC business:

- championship matches;
- ongoing rivalries;
- contender matches;
- tag-team matches;
- personal feuds;
- special attractions where appropriate.

## Grand Super League Qualification

Total Destruction also plays a limited role in qualification for the **Grand Super League**.

The highest-ranked contenders qualify automatically.

Wrestlers around the qualification cutoff may be required to defend their position against wildcard challengers.

A typical structure may involve:

- contenders ranked 1–10 qualifying automatically;
- contenders ranked 11 and 12 entering qualification matches;
- wildcard wrestlers challenging for the final tournament places.

Only one or two matches on the card are normally devoted to Grand Super League qualification.

The rest of the event remains a standard SLC major show.

## Presentation

Total Destruction may use a subtle autumnal visual identity, including darker seasonal graphics, colder lighting, and late-year imagery.

This is an aesthetic choice rather than a booking requirement.

---

# Grand Super League Finals

**Month:** December

The **Grand Super League Finals** conclude SLC's major annual tournament.

The Grand Super League is one of the most important competitive structures in the promotion.

Its winner earns the right to compete in the main event of **Champion's Way** for the SLC World Championship.

The December event therefore serves as the bridge between the regular competitive season and SLC's biggest show of the year.

The exact tournament format can vary, but qualification is based primarily on competitive standing and performance rather than storyline convenience.

The event places a stronger-than-usual emphasis on sporting presentation, tournament consequences, and the pressure of reaching the Champion's Way main event.

---

# Champion's Way

**Month:** January

**Champion's Way** is the largest and most prestigious event in SLC.

It functions as the promotion's equivalent of a season-defining supercard.

Major rivalries, championship programmes, and long-term character arcs frequently reach their most important stage at Champion's Way.

The main event traditionally features the **SLC World Champion** defending against the winner of the **Grand Super League**.

Other major championships and important feuds are normally given prominent positions on the card.

Champion's Way is not tied to any specific match type.

Its importance comes from the stories reaching the event rather than from a required gimmick.

---

# Retribution

**Month:** March

**Retribution** is the final major event before the next season begins at Ignition.

The show deals primarily with the consequences of Champion's Way.

This can include:

- justified championship rematches;
- unresolved rivalries;
- new feuds created by Champion's Way;
- wrestlers reacting to major wins or losses;
- changing alliances;
- the beginning of new directions ahead of Ignition.

Retribution is not required to feature rematches from Champion's Way.

Its purpose is simply to explore the fallout from SLC's biggest event and bring the season toward a natural conclusion.

---

# Event Philosophy

SLC events are designed around **identity rather than obligation**.

An event can have a distinctive atmosphere without forcing the creative team to manufacture a specific storyline or stipulation simply because a particular month has arrived.

For example:

- Mayhem begins the Summer of Violence, but not every feud becomes hardcore.
- Birthday Bash has a beach-party presentation, but serious championship matches remain serious.
- Total Destruction can include Grand Super League qualifiers without becoming an all-qualifier event.
- Champion's Way is important because of the stories that reach it, not because it contains a mandatory match type.

This approach allows SLC's calendar to provide structure while still allowing storylines to develop naturally.
