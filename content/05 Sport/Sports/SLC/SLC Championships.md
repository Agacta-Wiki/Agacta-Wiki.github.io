# SLC Championships

## Overview

**Super League Combat (SLC)** maintains a relatively small championship structure designed to keep each title important and clearly differentiated.

The promotion currently recognises three primary championships:

- **SLC World Championship**
- **SLC Challenge Championship**
- **SLC Tag Team Championship**

SLC generally avoids creating large numbers of secondary or specialist championships. The aim is for each title to have a clear purpose, a recognisable place in the hierarchy, and enough television time for championship stories to remain important.

Championship matches are usually built through rankings, contender matches, tournaments, storyline developments, or special championship opportunities.

## SLC World Championship

The **SLC World Championship** is the promotion's highest-ranking championship.

It is intended to represent the best overall wrestler in SLC and is normally defended in the main event or upper portion of major events.

World Championship challengers are usually established through strong win-loss form, major contender matches, tournaments, or significant storyline developments.

The title is treated as a sporting achievement rather than simply as a storytelling prop, although long-term narratives frequently develop around the championship.

**Matt Riddle** became the first SLC World Champion by defeating **Rob Van Dam** at **Ignition**.

## SLC Challenge Championship

The **SLC Challenge Championship** is SLC's primary secondary singles championship.

Rather than functioning as a traditional "television title", the championship is built around the idea of regular competitive challenges from wrestlers across the roster.

The title can be defended against established contenders, rising wrestlers, specialists from different wrestling styles, or competitors attempting to move into the upper card.

This makes the Challenge Championship an important bridge between the mid-card and the World Championship scene.

**Velociraptor** became the first SLC Challenge Champion by defeating **Desmond Boyce** at **Ignition**.

## SLC Tag Team Championship

The **SLC Tag Team Championship** represents SLC's tag team division.

The promotion treats tag team wrestling as a distinct competitive style rather than simply as a collection of singles wrestlers placed together.

Regular teams and established partnerships are generally prioritised when determining championship contenders.

**Champagne Syndicate** became the first SLC Tag Team Champions by defeating **Pavement Cartel** at **Ignition**.

## Championship Rules

### Clean and Decisive Finishes

SLC does not require every championship match to end with a finishing move.

A championship match may end through:

- a recognised finishing move;
- a submission;
- a roll-up or flash pin;
- a high-impact suplex;
- a clothesline or strike;
- another credible match-ending move.

This reflects SLC's broader philosophy that any sufficiently effective move can end a match.

### Champions Must Be Defeated

A champion must normally be pinned or submitted to lose a championship.

In multi-person championship matches, SLC generally uses **elimination rules**, preventing a champion from losing a title without being directly defeated.

This means a champion cannot normally lose a championship simply because another challenger pins somebody else.

### Match Length

Championship matches do not automatically receive unusually long time limits.

Matches lasting more than 30 minutes are extremely rare in SLC.

The promotion treats physical damage and fatigue seriously, meaning long matches are expected to show visible deterioration rather than allowing wrestlers to continue indefinitely without consequence.

## Second Challenge Bet

SLC uses a rule known as the **Second Challenge Bet**.

If a wrestler challenges a champion for a title and loses, they cannot challenge that same champion for that same championship again during the current reign unless they put something meaningful of their own at stake.

This effectively turns the second challenge into a **Lucha de Apuesta-style wager**.

Possible stakes include:

- hair;
- mask;
- career;
- place in a stable;
- leadership of a stable;
- being forced to join a stable;
- leaving a stable;
- another personally meaningful condition.

The rule is designed to prevent repetitive championship rematches and ensure that second challenges carry greater stakes than the first.

## Contender Zero

SLC also recognises the **Contender Zero** system.

The winner of the **Contender Zero Tournament** earns one championship opportunity that may be used for any SLC championship.

Contender Zero is not tied to the normal ranking structure and can choose which championship to pursue.

The opportunity must be formally invoked at least **24 hours before** the Fire, Boom, or major event where the title match will take place.

This prevents surprise same-night cash-ins while still allowing strategic timing.

A babyface holder may announce their challenge well in advance, while a heel may deliberately wait until a champion appears vulnerable before declaring the opportunity for an upcoming show.

Once used, Contender Zero status is lost.

## Championship Philosophy

SLC championships are intended to reflect both sporting credibility and long-term storytelling.

The promotion avoids frequent title changes purely for shock value and generally expects championship reigns to contribute to a wrestler's development, character, or wider storyline.

At the same time, title changes are not treated as impossible events.

Unexpected champions, short reigns, and upset victories can occur when they make sense within the story and competitive structure.

The overall aim is to keep championships important without making them predictable.
