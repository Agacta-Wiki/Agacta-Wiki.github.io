# SLC Arena

## Overview

The **SLC Arena** is the permanent home of **Super League Combat (SLC)** in **Treslio, Agacta**.

The venue occupies part of a former airport complex that was converted into a wrestling arena, company headquarters, training facility, production base, and fan space.

The airport setting is one of the defining features of SLC.

Rather than attempting to disguise the building's original purpose, SLC has gradually embraced it. Former passenger areas, offices, lounges, service corridors, retail spaces, and hangars have all been repurposed while retaining enough of their original appearance to give the venue a distinctive identity.

The result is a wrestling venue that feels part sports arena, part workplace, part underground cultural space.

---

## The Old Arrivals Hall

The main SLC Arena is located in the former **arrivals hall** of the airport.

The space was largely gutted during conversion and rebuilt around the wrestling ring, seating areas, entrance stage, production positions, and spectator facilities.

The arena has a capacity of approximately **1,500 spectators**.

Its relatively small size is intentional.

Fans are positioned close to the ring and entrance area, creating a loud and intimate atmosphere in which individual crowd reactions can be clearly heard on broadcasts.

The venue draws some comparison to smaller cult wrestling arenas such as the old Impact Zone or Full Sail University, but the airport setting gives SLC a visual identity of its own.

---

## Design

The SLC Arena retains a significant amount of the airport's original architecture.

Visible features can include:

- old wall tiling;
- industrial flooring;
- glass partitions;
- airport-style corridors;
- structural columns;
- service doors;
- former baggage and passenger areas;
- older signage;
- exposed infrastructure.

Some areas have been modernised for safety and television production, while others deliberately retain a rougher appearance.

SLC does not attempt to make every corner of the building look pristine.

The slightly uneven mixture of renovation and surviving airport architecture is considered part of the venue's appeal.

---

## Entrance Area

The main wrestling entrance is built into the former passenger terminal space.

Elements of old airport design are occasionally incorporated into SLC presentation.

These can include:

- gate-style numbering;
- departure-board-inspired graphics;
- terminal signage;
- industrial lighting;
- old passenger-route markings.

The references are generally subtle.

SLC avoids turning the venue into an airport-themed gimmick.

The building is an old airport that became a wrestling promotion, not a wrestling promotion pretending to be an airport.

---

## SLC Café

Two former airport cafés were retained during redevelopment and combined into the **SLC Café**.

The café operates before and after SLC events and gives supporters somewhere to eat, drink, and socialise without immediately leaving the complex.

It serves:

- hot and cold food;
- snacks;
- coffee;
- soft drinks;
- alcoholic drinks.

The café retains a licence to serve alcohol.

Because of the venue's relatively small capacity and recurring audience, the café has become part of the social culture surrounding SLC events.

Regular fans, staff, and wrestlers may all pass through the same general environment, reinforcing the promotion's relatively informal and intimate atmosphere.

---

## Merchandise Area

The airport's former **car-rental booths** were converted into merchandise stands.

These sell:

- SLC merchandise;
- wrestler shirts;
- event merchandise;
- posters;
- replica items;
- limited-run merchandise tied to particular storylines or events.

The retained booth structure gives the merchandise area a distinctive appearance compared with conventional arena concourses.

---

## Backstage Areas

SLC uses much of the surviving airport infrastructure as backstage space.

This includes:

- corridors;
- former passenger waiting areas;
- service hallways;
- loading areas;
- stairwells;
- storage rooms;
- old offices;
- production areas.

These spaces frequently appear on SLC programming.

Backstage interviews are not always conducted in front of a dedicated interview wall.

Kiana Martins and Tropicbeck may instead speak with wrestlers in whatever part of the building makes sense at the time.

As a result, the audience gradually becomes familiar with different parts of the complex.

Some areas can develop their own reputations through repeated use in interviews, confrontations, attacks, or storyline moments.

---

## SLC Offices

The main administrative and creative offices are located upstairs in the former airport office area.

During the early period of SLC, much of this area remained under redevelopment.

The offices initially had a deliberately makeshift quality, with basic desks, cubicles, production equipment, and partially renovated rooms.

**Jim Barry** has a private office as SLC president.

Other senior staff originally worked from smaller shared or temporary office spaces while redevelopment continued.

As SLC has grown, the office area has gradually become more complete without losing the feel of a company operating from a repurposed building.

---

## Creative Conference Room

The airport's former **VIP lounge** was converted into SLC's main creative conference room.

This is where the creative team meets to discuss:

- long-term storylines;
- match cards;
- championship programmes;
- character direction;
- television structure;
- roster development;
- major events.

The room has become one of the most important off-camera spaces in the company.

Its atmosphere reflects the different personalities on the creative team.

Lemonbeck may arrive with large storyline outlines or whiteboard ideas.

Limebeck frequently brings notes, statistics, rankings, or tournament structures.

Kiana Martins may bring old wrestling footage, references, or ideas from independent wrestling.

Tropicbeck generally focuses on character, atmosphere, and presentation.

Jim Barry often acts as the final mediator when major disagreements arise.

---

## SLC Dojo

One of the airport's former hangars has been converted into the **SLC Dojo**.

The Dojo is SLC's main training and developmental facility.

It is not a separate wrestling promotion or television brand.

Its purpose is to train and develop wrestlers before they are ready for regular SLC programming.

The facility includes:

- two wrestling rings;
- weights;
- cardio equipment;
- conditioning areas;
- practice space;
- film-study facilities;
- basic recovery and treatment areas.

The size of the former hangar allows SLC to maintain a substantial training space without interfering with the main arena.

---

## Developmental Role

The SLC Dojo is used for:

- training inexperienced wrestlers;
- refining established wrestlers who need additional work;
- developing new characters;
- practising matches;
- improving conditioning;
- teaching television timing;
- preparing wrestlers for Fire or Boom.

Prospects are not automatically rushed onto television.

A wrestler may remain in the Dojo for weeks or months until trainers and creative staff believe they are ready.

Some wrestlers may appear on **Boom** before becoming regulars on **Fire**, but Boom is not considered a developmental show.

It remains SLC's wrestling-focused secondary programme.

---

## Production Facilities

The airport complex also houses much of SLC's production operation.

This includes areas used for:

- television direction;
- audio;
- graphics;
- lighting;
- editing;
- camera storage;
- equipment preparation.

The permanent nature of the venue allows SLC to maintain much of its television equipment on site.

This helps the promotion maintain a consistent visual identity while still allowing the building itself to feel slightly rough and improvised.

---

## Gorilla Position

SLC's main **gorilla position** sits close to the entrance route between backstage and the arena.

This area functions as the final staging point before wrestlers walk through the curtain.

It is also one of the main operational centres during broadcasts.

When he is not required elsewhere, Jim Barry can often be found around gorilla or production during major shows, helping oversee the event.

The area also allows producers and creative staff to speak with wrestlers immediately before or after matches.

---

## Medical and Treatment Areas

SLC maintains medical and treatment facilities within the complex.

These are used for:

- injury assessment;
- first aid;
- post-match checks;
- recovery;
- physiotherapy;
- emergency treatment.

The promotion's sporting presentation means injuries and accumulated damage are treated seriously both on and off television.

---

## Fan Culture

The SLC Arena has developed a strong cult following.

Its limited capacity means regular attendees can become familiar faces.

The audience is close to the action and close to many of the building's shared spaces, which contributes to a sense of community.

Fans often describe attending SLC as feeling less like visiting a conventional arena and more like entering a small wrestling world with its own geography and culture.

That feeling is central to SLC's appeal.

---

## Airport Lore

The airport gradually became part of SLC's wider mythology.

Fans and wrestlers may refer to particular locations informally.

Former airport features can become associated with:

- specific wrestlers;
- interviews;
- confrontations;
- recurring jokes;
- memorable incidents.

Some old signage and architectural features have become recognisable enough that regular viewers immediately know where a backstage segment is taking place.

The building therefore acts almost like a recurring character within SLC programming.

---

## Use in Storytelling

SLC frequently uses the geography of the airport to make storylines feel more immediate.

A wrestler can:

- leave the creative offices;
- be confronted in a corridor;
- walk past the café;
- enter through the old terminal area;
- reach the arena within the same sequence.

This gives SLC programming a stronger sense of physical continuity.

The audience can gradually understand how different parts of the promotion connect to one another.

---

## Presentation Philosophy

The SLC Arena is not intended to look like a smaller version of a major corporate wrestling venue.

Its appeal comes from being visibly different.

The arena is:

- intimate;
- industrial;
- slightly rough;
- recognisable;
- functional;
- full of character.

Renovation is carried out where necessary for safety, comfort, and production.

However, SLC generally avoids removing every trace of the building's history.

The contrast between modern wrestling production and surviving airport architecture is part of the venue's identity.

---

## Importance to SLC

The SLC Arena is considered inseparable from the promotion itself.

SLC may occasionally stage events elsewhere, particularly as the company grows, but the airport complex remains its permanent home and spiritual centre.

It represents the qualities SLC wants to preserve even as the promotion expands:

- closeness to the audience;
- independence;
- experimentation;
- character;
- unpredictability;
- the feeling that fans have discovered something slightly outside mainstream wrestling.

For SLC, the arena is not simply where the wrestling happens.

It is where the promotion lives.
