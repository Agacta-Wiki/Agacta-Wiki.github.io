# Soccer in Agacta

**Soccer** is one of the most popular sports in Agacta alongside [[Agactan Rules Football]]. It is administered by the [[Agactan Sports Commission]] (ASC), which organises professional and amateur competitions throughout the country.

Like all major ASC sports, soccer operates using Agacta's provincial franchise system, with each of the country's nineteen provinces represented by a single professional franchise.

---

# League structure

The professional pyramid consists of three national divisions.

## Division 1

Division 1 is the highest level of Agactan soccer and consists of **12 provincial franchises**.

The league operates on a promotion and relegation system with Division 2.

## Division 2

Division 2 consists of:

- the remaining **7 provincial franchises**
- the **5 highest-ranked minor franchises**

Provincial franchises may be promoted to Division 1, while minor franchises are ineligible for promotion beyond Division 2.

## Division 3

Division 3 consists of the remaining **24 minor franchises** competing on a semi-professional basis.

# Provincial franchises

## The Big Three

Historically Agactan soccer has been dominated by three clubs:

- [[Treslio Thunder]]
- [[Setroburg Sentinels]]
- [[Nleto Knights]]

Together they have won the majority of league championships and are widely regarded as Agacta's traditional "Big Three."

Their position within Agactan football has frequently been compared to:

- Ajax, Feyenoord and PSV Eindhoven in the Netherlands.
- Benfica, Porto and Sporting CP in Portugal.
- Anderlecht, Club Brugge and Standard Liège in Belgium.
- Olympiacos, Panathinaikos and AEK Athens in Greece.

---

## Merino Mariners

The [[Merino Mariners]] are traditionally regarded as the strongest club outside the Big Three.

Although they have not historically matched the sustained success of the traditional powers, they have regularly challenged for league titles and continental qualification.

Many commentators now refer to Agactan football as having a "Big Four" rather than a Big Three due to Merino's consistent competitiveness.

The Mariners have often been compared to clubs such as Braga, AZ Alkmaar, Genk, PAOK and Aberdeen.

---

## Orientoport Owls

The [[Orientoport Owls]] emerged as a major force during the early twenty-first century.

Their breakthrough came with their first league championship in **2001**, followed by a second title later in the century.

Although capable of competing near the top of the table, the Owls have also experienced periods of struggle and are generally regarded as one of Agactan football's most unpredictable clubs.

They are frequently compared to Hearts, FC Twente and Vitória de Guimarães.

---

## Kalisto Falcons

The [[Kalisto Falcons]] were historically regarded as the country's fourth-largest club.

Since the 1980s, however, they have generally fallen behind Merino and have struggled to regain their former status.

Their historical trajectory has been compared to clubs such as Hibernian, Belenenses and Royal Antwerp.

---

## Aquatrip Sunsets

The [[Aquatrip Sunsets]] have been one of the most consistent top-flight clubs outside the traditional elite.

Although rarely considered title contenders, they have regularly competed for qualification to continental competitions.

They are often compared to Motherwell or Gil Vicente.

---

## Stototrip Storm

Despite representing one of Agacta's largest cities, the [[Stototrip Storm]] are widely regarded as one of the country's great underachievers.

The club has experienced numerous promotions and relegations despite its large support base.

Comparisons are frequently drawn with ADO Den Haag and Dundee FC.

---

## Established top-flight clubs

Several clubs have traditionally occupied the lower half of Division 1 while remaining more associated with top-flight football than the second tier.

These include:

- [[Tlelmund Guardians]]
- [[Abeldrip Lions]]
- [[Nlastromund Wolves]]

---

## Yo-yo clubs

Several provincial franchises have alternated regularly between Divisions 1 and 2.

These include:

- [[Montanomund Mountaineers]]
- [[Riala Rapids]]
- [[Savtrio Palms]]
- [[Ulos Blizzard]]

---

## Division 2 specialists

The remaining provincial franchises have historically spent the majority of their history in Division 2, occasionally earning promotion to the top flight but generally being more closely associated with the second tier.

# Culture

Soccer occupies a unique place within Agactan sporting culture.

Unlike most European countries, elite clubs represent entire provinces rather than individual cities. As a result, support is generally organised around provincial identity rather than neighbourhood or municipal rivalries.

Promotion and relegation remain central to the sport, although only provincial franchises are eligible to compete in Division 1.

## Soccer television schedule

The modern top-level [[Soccer in Agacta|Agactan soccer]] weekend consists of six principal fixture slots, with every match in each round occupying its own television window.

| Day      | Kick-off | Selection | Broadcaster                 |     |
| -------- | -------: | --------: | --------------------------- | --- |
| Friday   |    21:00 |    Pick 1 | [[AgaSat Sport]]            |     |
| Saturday |    14:00 |    Pick 5 | [[Invictus Sport]]          |     |
| Saturday |    16:00 |    Pick 3 | [[AgaSat Sport]]            |     |
| Sunday   |    12:00 |    Pick 6 | [[Agasat Sport]]            |     |
| Sunday   |    18:00 |    Pick 4 | [[Agacta Kanalo Diverteso]] |     |
| Sunday   |    20:00 |    Pick 2 | [[Invictus Sport]]          |     |

The **Friday 21:00 fixture** is AgaSat's flagship match and carries **first pick** of the round. AgaSat also holds the two Saturday afternoon packages, giving it three live matches from each round.

**Invictus Sport** holds one Saturday and one Sunday package. Its **Sunday 20:00 fixture** carries second pick, making it one of the most valuable rights packages despite AgaSat's overall dominance of soccer broadcasting. Invictus also broadcasts the Saturday 14:00 fixture, which carries fifth pick.

The **Sunday 18:00 match** is broadcast free-to-air by [[Agacta Kanalo Diverteso|AKD]] and carries fourth pick.

Unlike the traditional Saturday 16:00 schedule which characterised Agactan soccer before the growth of subscription television, the modern fixture list spreads the six matches across the entire weekend. The Friday-night fixture has become particularly established since its introduction during the 1990s, while the two Sunday evening matches give Agactan soccer a Sunday-night television presence similar to that found in Spanish football.
