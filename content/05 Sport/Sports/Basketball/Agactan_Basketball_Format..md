# Agactan Basketball Season

The **Agactan basketball season** is the national competition system for
professional basketball in Agacta. Rather than using a single
conventional league table, the season is built around four historic Cup
competitions --- the **East Cup**, **North Cup**, **West Cup** and
**South Cup** --- followed by the **Agactan Basketball Championship
playoffs**.

Results in each Cup award **Championship points**. At the end of the
four Cups, the eight professional franchises with the most Championship
points qualify for the national Championship playoffs, where the Agactan
champion is decided.

Promotion and relegation between Divisions 1 and 2 can also occur after
each Cup, meaning divisional membership can change several times during
the same season.

## Origins of the four-Cup system

The four Cups pre-date the modern national basketball division.

Before a unified national competition existed, the regional divisions of
**ASC Basketball** each organised an open tournament. Despite their
regional administration, these competitions were open to teams from
throughout Agacta rather than being restricted to clubs from the host
region.

The four competitions were:

1.  **East Cup**
2.  **North Cup**
3.  **West Cup**
4.  **South Cup**

When the national basketball system developed, the existing Cups were
retained rather than replaced. They gradually became the four stages of
the national season.

Each Cup therefore remains a competition in its own right, with its own
champion and history, while simultaneously contributing points toward
qualification for the national Championship.

## Season structure

The competitions are played in the following order:

1.  **East Cup**
2.  **North Cup**
3.  **West Cup**
4.  **South Cup**
5.  **Agactan Basketball Championship playoffs**

The four Cups are not qualifying tournaments in the sense of awarding
automatic Championship places. Winning a Cup earns the largest
Championship-points award, but **does not automatically qualify a
franchise for the Championship playoffs**.

A team must accumulate enough points across the season to finish in the
overall top eight.

## Professional divisions

Agacta has **19 professional basketball franchises**.

At the beginning of each Cup:

-   **Division 1 contains 16 teams**
-   **Division 2 contains eight teams**

Division 2 normally contains the three professional franchises outside
Division 1 together with five leading semi-professional franchises.

A lower semi-professional structure, including Division 3, exists
beneath Division 2.

Only professional franchises accumulate points toward the national
Championship. Semi-professional teams compete within their own
hierarchy.

## Promotion and relegation

Promotion and relegation can take place **after every Cup**.

Consequently, a professional franchise may begin the season in Division
2, earn promotion during one Cup and compete in Division 1 in the next.
Likewise, a Division 1 franchise can be relegated during the season and
attempt to return later.

The exact relegation bracket differs between the East/West and
North/South formats.

### Division 2 eligibility

Semi-professional teams may win Division 2 competitions or reach the
promotion places, but they are not eligible for promotion into the
professional Division 1.

Promotion arrangements therefore adapt when a semi-professional team
occupies a promotion position.

A professional Division 2 champion normally earns automatic promotion.
An eligible professional runner-up may receive a promotion/relegation
playoff against a Division 1 team.

If semi-professional results leave only one eligible professional
promotion candidate, the number of Division 1 relegation places can be
reduced accordingly.

## East and West Cups

The East and West Cups use the same competition structure.

### Division 1 group stage

The 16 Division 1 teams are divided into:

-   **two groups of eight**
-   **single round robin**
-   **seven group games per team**

The top three teams in each group advance to the knockout phase.

The two group winners receive direct places in the semifinals.

The second- and third-place teams qualify for the quarterfinals.

This produces six knockout qualifiers from the 16-team Division 1 field.

### Knockout stage

The Cup knockout phase consists of:

-   two quarterfinals
-   two semifinals
-   the Cup final

The quarterfinals and semifinals are single-game contests.

### Relegation

The bottom two teams from each group enter the relegation process.

-   7th in Group A plays 8th in Group B
-   7th in Group B plays 8th in Group A

The two winners are safe.

The two losers then play each other.

The loser of that game is relegated automatically.

The winner may then face the eligible Division 2 runner-up in a
promotion/relegation playoff.

The professional Division 2 champion receives automatic promotion when
eligible.

## North and South Cups

The North and South Cups use a different group format.

### Division 1 group stage

The 16 Division 1 teams are divided into:

-   **four groups of four**
-   **double round robin**
-   **six group games per team**

The top two teams in each group advance to the quarterfinals.

Eight of the 16 Division 1 teams therefore reach the knockout stage.

### Knockout stage

The Cup knockout phase consists of:

-   four quarterfinals
-   two semifinals
-   the Cup final

### Relegation

The four fourth-place teams enter the relegation bracket.

They are paired into two games.

The two winners are safe.

The two losers then meet.

The result of the Division 2 competition determines whether the losing
Division 1 teams face automatic relegation or a promotion/relegation
playoff against an eligible professional Division 2 team.

## Championship points

Every professional franchise earns Championship points according to its
performance in each Cup.

The four Cup totals are added together to create the season-long
Championship standings.

### East and West Cup points

  Finish                    Championship points
  ----------------------- ---------------------
  Cup champion                           **20**
  Runner-up                              **16**
  Semifinalist                           **13**
  Quarterfinalist                        **11**
  Top 8                                   **8**
  Top 12                                  **6**
  Bottom four, survives                   **4**
  Relegated                               **2**

The **Top 8** category applies to the two fourth-place teams that miss
the East/West knockout phase.

### North and South Cup points

  Finish                    Championship points
  ----------------------- ---------------------
  Cup champion                           **20**
  Runner-up                              **16**
  Semifinalist                           **13**
  Quarterfinalist                         **9**
  Top 12                                  **6**
  Bottom four, survives                   **4**
  Relegated                               **2**

There is no separate Top 8 category in the North and South Cups because
eight teams qualify directly for the quarterfinals.

### Why quarterfinal points differ

Reaching an East or West Cup quarterfinal requires finishing in the top
three of an eight-team group. Only six of the 16 Division 1 teams reach
the knockout stage.

In the North and South Cups, eight of the 16 Division 1 teams qualify
for the quarterfinals.

An East/West quarterfinal appearance is therefore worth **11 points**,
compared with **9 points** in the North/South format.

From the semifinals onward, the four Cups use the same Championship
values:

-   semifinalist --- 13
-   runner-up --- 16
-   champion --- 20

## Division 2 Championship points

Professional franchises competing in Division 2 can still earn
Championship points, but their available totals are deliberately
limited.

  Division 2 result                                    Championship points
  -------------------------------------------------- ---------------------
  Champion and automatically promoted                                **6**
  Runner-up and wins promotion/relegation playoff                    **5**
  Runner-up and loses promotion/relegation playoff                   **2**
  Other non-promoted professional franchise                          **1**

Promotion/relegation playoff victories themselves do not add additional
Championship points.

The system is designed so that dropping into Division 2 cannot become a
shortcut to a better Championship-points return.

A relegated Division 1 team receives two points. Even if it immediately
wins Division 2 and returns to Division 1 at the next opportunity, the
maximum two-Cup return is:

**2 + 6 = 8 points**

Two consecutive bottom-four Division 1 survivals are also worth:

**4 + 4 = 8 points**

There is therefore no Championship-points advantage to being relegated
deliberately.

## South Cup seeding

Unlike the earlier Cups, the South Cup draw is seeded using the
cumulative Championship standings after the East, North and West Cups.

Only teams currently competing in Division 1 are included in the South
Cup seeding.

The 16 Division 1 teams are divided into four pots:

  Pot         Championship position among current D1 teams
  ----------- ----------------------------------------------
  **Pot 1**   1st--4th
  **Pot 2**   5th--8th
  **Pot 3**   9th--12th
  **Pot 4**   13th--16th

Each South Cup group receives one team from each pot.

Teams competing in Division 2 are excluded from the pots even if their
accumulated Championship points would otherwise place them within one of
the four bands.

The teams within each pot are then drawn randomly.

## Championship standings

After the South Cup, every professional franchise's points from all four
Cups are added together.

The **top eight** qualify for the Agactan Basketball Championship
playoffs.

Cup victories do not provide automatic qualification. A Cup champion can
therefore miss the Championship playoffs if its results across the other
Cups leave it outside the overall top eight.

Likewise, a team can qualify without winning a Cup through consistently
strong finishes across the season.

## Championship tiebreakers

If professional franchises finish level on Championship points, the
following tiebreakers are applied in order:

1.  **Most Cup wins**
2.  **Most Cup final appearances**
3.  **Most Cup semifinal appearances**
4.  **Most Cups played in Division 1**
5.  **Neutral-site playoff**

Quarterfinal appearances are not used as a tiebreaker because
qualification for the quarterfinals is structurally different between
the East/West and North/South formats.

Top-12 finishes are also not used because a six-point Top-12 Division 1
result carries the same Championship value as a Division 2 championship
with promotion.

If all four statistical tiebreakers remain equal, the tied teams play a
one-off game at a neutral venue.

## Championship playoffs

The eight qualifiers are seeded according to the final Championship
standings.

The quarterfinal pairings are:

-   **1st vs 8th**
-   **2nd vs 7th**
-   **3rd vs 6th**
-   **4th vs 5th**

### Series lengths

  Round                Format
  -------------------- ---------------
  Quarterfinals        **Best of 5**
  Semifinals           **Best of 7**
  Championship Final   **Best of 7**

The winner of the Championship Final becomes the **Agactan national
basketball champion**.

The Championship is distinct from the individual Cup titles. A franchise
can therefore win one or more Cups during the season without becoming
national champion, while a team that has not won a Cup can still reach
the Championship playoffs through its cumulative results.

## Group-stage ties

Point differential is not the preferred method for settling important
group-stage ties.

Where a tie requires a decisive separation and cannot otherwise be
resolved, a **one-off neutral-site game** may be used.

This avoids creating an incentive for teams to run up large margins
against weaker opponents purely to improve a point-differential
tiebreak.

## Competitive principles

The format is designed around several principles.

### Every Cup matters

Each Cup awards substantial Championship points, so teams have an
incentive to compete throughout the season rather than treating early
competitions as preparation for the playoffs.

### No automatic Championship berth

Winning a Cup does not guarantee a playoff place.

This prevents an already-qualified team from being able to deliberately
concede a later Cup final in order to give another franchise an
automatic Championship berth.

### Poor form hurts, good form can repair a season

A bad Cup can significantly damage a team's position without necessarily
ending its season.

Likewise, a strong later Cup can restore a team to Championship
contention, but does not erase the value of consistency across the
earlier competitions.

### Division 2 is not a shortcut

The reduced Championship-point awards available in Division 2 prevent
relegation from becoming strategically desirable.

### Promotion and relegation remain meaningful throughout the season

Because divisional movement occurs after every Cup, promotion and
relegation are active parts of the national season rather than an
end-of-season appendix.

A franchise can fall out of Division 1, recover later in the same
season, or see a poor stretch materially affect both its divisional
status and Championship prospects.

## Overall pathway

A professional franchise's season can therefore be summarised as:

**East Cup → promotion/relegation → North Cup → promotion/relegation →
West Cup → promotion/relegation → seeded South Cup →
promotion/relegation → final Championship standings → top-eight
Championship playoffs → national champion**

The four Cups determine both trophies and the Championship race, while
the promotion and relegation system continually reshapes the field
around them.
