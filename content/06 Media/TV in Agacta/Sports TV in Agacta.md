# Sports television in Agacta

**Sports television in [[Agacta]]** is dominated by two major subscription broadcasting groups, **[[AgaSat Sport]]** and **[[Invictus Sport]]**, alongside free-to-air coverage provided by the country's traditional terrestrial broadcasters.

The growth of dedicated sports television from the early 1990s substantially changed the scheduling of [[Sport in Agacta|Agactan sport]]. Traditional common kick-off times were gradually replaced by staggered television windows, particularly in [[Soccer in Agacta|soccer]] and [[Agactan Rules]], although regulations continue to guarantee free-to-air coverage and protect broadcasting windows for other sports administered by the [[Agacta Sports Commission]].

## AgaSat Sport

**[[AgaSat Sport]]** is the largest sports broadcaster in Agacta and operates four dedicated services:

- **AgaSat Sport 1**
- **AgaSat Sport 2**
- **AgaSat Sport 3**
- **AgaSat Sport News**

All four channels broadcast **24 hours a day**.

AgaSat Sport has traditionally possessed the greatest financial resources for acquiring sports rights and is generally the dominant broadcaster in the Agactan subscription sports market.

AgaSat Sport 1, 2 and 3 combine live coverage with highlights, classic matches and archive programming. During periods without major domestic events, the channels also regularly carry live sporting events from other countries.

AgaSat Sport News provides continuous sports news and coverage.

## Invictus Sport

**[[Invictus Sport]]** is AgaSat's principal competitor and is backed by [[Platinum TV]]. It operates two channels:

- **Invictus Sport 1**
- **Invictus Sport 2**

Historically operating with fewer financial resources than AgaSat, Invictus has developed a reputation as a smaller but significant challenger in the sports-rights market.

### Invictus Sport 1

**Invictus Sport 1** broadcasts 24 hours a day.

Outside its live broadcasts, the channel carries sports highlights, classic matches, archive programming and sporting events from other countries.

The late-night and early-morning schedules are also used for delayed coverage of less prominent [[Agacta Sports Commission|ASC]] sports, allowing events which do not receive major live television slots to receive national coverage.

### Invictus Sport 2

**Invictus Sport 2** operates a more limited broadcast schedule.

Its normal hours are:

| Day | Broadcasting hours |
|---|---|
| Weekdays | 18:00–00:00 |
| Weekends | 09:00–01:00 |

The channel may remain on air later when covering major live events, particularly **boxing, mixed martial arts and professional wrestling special events**.

Outside its normal broadcasting hours, the channel carries **teleshopping**.

## Development of televised kick-off times

Before the expansion of cable and satellite sports television, Agactan team sports generally had common traditional kick-off times.

The traditional soccer kick-off was **16:00 on Saturday**, while the traditional Agactan Rules kick-off was **14:00 on Sunday**.

This began to change during the early and mid-1990s as [[Cable and Satellite Television in Agacta|cable and satellite television]] expanded and dedicated sports channels competed for exclusive live programming.

One of the most significant innovations was the introduction of regular **Friday-night matches** in both soccer and Agactan Rules. The concept was adopted from neighbouring [[Sensin]] during the 1990s and has since become an established part of the Agactan sporting weekend.

Soccer subsequently developed regular Saturday afternoon television windows at approximately **14:00 and 16:00**, alongside Sunday evening fixtures influenced by the scheduling model used in Spanish football.

Agactan Rules developed televised Saturday and Sunday fixtures, producing the modern staggered schedule.

## Free-to-air requirement

Despite the importance of subscription sports television, major domestic competitions retain a statutory free-to-air presence.

**One match from each round of both the national soccer and Agactan Rules competitions must be shown on free-to-air television.**

The current free-to-air rights are held by:

| Competition          | Free-to-air broadcaster     | Channel   |     |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- | --------- | --- |
| [[Soccer in Agacta]] | [[Agacta Kanalo Diverteso]] | [[AKD]]   |     |
| [[Agactan Rules]]    | [[Grupo Transdono Sutro]]   | [[Sutro]] |     |

The free-to-air match forms part of the wider television rights package rather than operating separately from the selection system used by subscription broadcasters.

## Protected sports windows

The [[Agacta Sports Commission]] maintains a series of **protected broadcasting windows** intended to prevent soccer and Agactan Rules from dominating the entire sporting weekend.

Neither sport may broadcast matches during certain periods, leaving television exposure and spectator attention available for other ASC sports.

The principal protected periods are **Saturday at 18:00** and **Sunday at 12:00**.

Additional restrictions prevent:

- soccer being televised in the **14:00 and 16:00 Sunday windows**
- Agactan Rules being televised in the **16:00 Saturday window**
- Agactan Rules being televised at **18:00 on Sunday**

These restrictions have helped produce the distinctive staggered weekend schedule used by Agactan sport.

## Agactan Rules television schedule

The modern top-level [[Agactan Rules]] weekend consists of six principal fixture slots.

| Day | Kick-off | Selection | Broadcaster |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Friday | 19:00 | Pick 1 | [[AgaSat Sport]] |
| Saturday | 12:00 | Pick 5 | [[Invictus Sport]] |
| Saturday | 20:00 | Pick 4 | Free-to-air |
| Sunday | 14:00 | Pick 3 | [[AgaSat Sport]] |
| Sunday | 16:00 | Pick 2 | [[Invictus Sport]] |
| Sunday | 20:00 | Pick 6 | Rotating |

The **Saturday 20:00 match** is the principal free-to-air fixture.

The **Sunday 20:00 match** has rotating television rights shared between **AgaSat**, **Invictus** and **Sutro**.

The selection system means that broadcasters do not simply receive whichever fixture happens to occupy their broadcast window. Rights packages include different priorities in choosing matches, with the Friday-night AgaSat fixture carrying **first pick**, while other windows receive subsequent selections.

## Soccer television schedule

The modern top-level [[Soccer in Agacta|Agactan soccer]] weekend consists of six principal fixture slots, with every match in each round occupying its own television window.

| Day      | Kick-off | Selection | Broadcaster                 |     |
| -------- | -------: | --------: | --------------------------- | --- |
| Friday   |    21:00 |    Pick 1 | [[AgaSat Sport]]            |     |
| Saturday |    14:00 |    Pick 5 | [[Invictus Sport]]          |     |
| Saturday |    16:00 |    Pick 3 | [[AgaSat Sport]]            |     |
| Sunday   |    12:00 |    Pick 6 | [[AgaSat Sport]]            |     |
| Sunday   |    18:00 |    Pick 4 | [[Agacta Kanalo Diverteso]] |     |
| Sunday   |    20:00 |    Pick 2 | [[Invictus Sport]]          |     |

The **Friday 21:00 fixture** is AgaSat's flagship match and carries **first pick** of the round. AgaSat also holds the one Saturday afternoon package and one Sunday at noon, giving it three live matches from each round.

**Invictus Sport** holds one Saturday and one Sunday package. Its **Sunday 20:00 fixture** carries second pick, making it one of the most valuable rights packages despite AgaSat's overall dominance of soccer broadcasting. Invictus also broadcasts the Saturday 14:00 fixture, which carries fifth pick.

The **Sunday 18:00 match** is broadcast free-to-air by [[Agacta Kanalo Diverteso|AKD]] and carries fourth pick.

Unlike the traditional Saturday 16:00 schedule which characterised Agactan soccer before the growth of subscription television, the modern fixture list spreads the six matches across the entire weekend. The Friday-night fixture has become particularly established since its introduction during the 1990s, while the two Sunday evening matches give Agactan soccer a Sunday-night television presence similar to that found in Spanish football.

## Rugby League television schedule

The top-level [[Rugby League in Agacta|Agactan Rugby League]] schedule consists of five principal fixture slots spread across Saturday, Sunday and Monday.

| Day      | Kick-off | Selection | Broadcaster        |
| -------- | -------: | --------: | ------------------ |
| Saturday |   14:00* |    Pick 4 | [[Agasat Sport]]   |
| Saturday |    16:00 |    Pick 3 | [[Star Network]]   |
| Saturday |    18:00 |    Pick 1 | [[AgaSat Sport]]   |
| Sunday   |    12:00 |    Pick 5 | [[Invictus Sport]] |
| Monday   |    21:00 |    Pick 2 | [[Invictus Sport]] |

**AgaSat Sport** holds first pick of each round, broadcasting its principal match at **18:00 on Saturday**. It also holds the Sunday 12:00 package, carrying fourth pick.

**Invictus Sport** holds second pick, shown in the prominent **Monday 21:00** slot, as well as the Saturday 14:00 package carrying fifth pick.

The **Saturday 16:00 fixture** is shown free-to-air by [[Star Network]] and carries third pick. This ensures that one Rugby League match from each round remains available on free television.

Rugby League scheduling is designed partly around the wider [[Agacta Sports Commission]] broadcasting calendar, particularly to minimise clashes with [[Agactan Rules]] and other major domestic sports.

## Security-related fixture changes

The [[Agacta Sports Commission]] and [[Agactan Police]] retain powers to alter television scheduling where a fixture presents particular security or public-order concerns.

A match may be moved into the **Saturday 12:00** slot where an earlier kick-off is considered desirable.

Authorities may also move the normal **Sunday 20:00** fixture to **Sunday at 10:00** when security considerations make an evening match unsuitable.

These powers take precedence over the normal television schedule and are principally associated with fixtures where policing considerations require greater separation between supporters or reduced opportunities for disorder.

## Impact on Agactan sport

The development of dedicated sports broadcasting transformed the rhythm of the Agactan sporting weekend.

Where soccer and Agactan Rules had once been concentrated overwhelmingly around Saturday at 16:00 and Sunday at 14:00 respectively, television created a schedule stretching from **Friday evening until Sunday night**.

However, unlike systems in which the largest sports can occupy almost any commercially desirable television window, Agacta retained protected periods for other ASC competitions. The result is a deliberately staggered broadcasting system in which television demand, free-to-air access, security considerations and the exposure of smaller sports all influence fixture scheduling.
