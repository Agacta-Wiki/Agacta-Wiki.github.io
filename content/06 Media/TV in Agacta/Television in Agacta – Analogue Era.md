# Television in Agacta – Analogue Era

Television broadcasting in **Agacta** began in 1950 and developed relatively rapidly, with television becoming a normal feature of Agactan households during the early to mid-1950s. The country's analogue terrestrial system eventually consisted of two national public channels, three national commercial broadcasters and a network of regional public channels, alongside separate services in the overseas territories.

## Introduction of television

Experimental television broadcasting began in **1949**, when the state broadcaster **ATV** began test transmissions. Regular television broadcasting officially commenced on **1 February 1950**.

Agacta adopted television relatively early. Unlike much of Europe, the country had not experienced the destruction and economic disruption of the Second World War, and the 1950s were instead a period of considerable economic prosperity, comparable in some respects to the post-war boom in North America.

The Agactan government also regarded television as an important symbol of modernisation and technological progress and actively encouraged households to acquire television sets. A mixture of **grants, subsidies, loans and rental schemes** made sets accessible to a much broader section of the population than their initial purchase price might otherwise have allowed.

As a result, television ownership expanded rapidly and television had become a regular part of domestic life by the early to mid-1950s.

## Original three-channel system

The initial broadcasting settlement established one state television service alongside two licensed commercial broadcasters.

The state service was operated by **ATV**, while licences for the two commercial channels were awarded to:

- **Agacta Kanalo Diverteso (AKD)**, based in **Treslio**
    
- **Grupo Transdono Sutro**, based in **Setroburg**
    

ATV began full broadcasting on **1 February 1950**, followed by AKD in **June 1950**. Sutro began transmissions in **September 1951**.

This created the basic three-broadcaster structure that would define Agactan television for the following two decades.

## Expansion of public television

In **1971**, ATV launched its second television channel, **ATV 2**. The original ATV service subsequently functioned as the broadcaster's primary channel alongside the newer secondary service.

The addition of ATV 2 increased the amount of public-service television available nationally and brought the mainland analogue system to four channels.

## Star Network

During the later 1970s, the government announced that a licence would be made available for a **third commercial television broadcaster**.

The licence was awarded to the **Nleto-based Star Network**, introducing a third major geographical centre to national commercial broadcasting alongside Treslio-based AKD and Setroburg-based Sutro.

**Star Network began broadcasting in 1982**, becoming the fifth national terrestrial television service.

The national analogue television landscape was therefore:

|Channel|Type|Base|Launch|
|---|---|---|---|
|ATV|Public|National|1 February 1950|
|AKD|Commercial|Treslio|June 1950|
|Sutro|Commercial|Setroburg|September 1951|
|ATV 2|Public|National|1971|
|Star Network|Commercial|Nleto|1982|

## Regionala TV

A second public television organisation, **Regionala TV**, was established in **1987**.

Unlike ATV, Regionala TV was not organised primarily as a single national channel. It operated a network of regional television services intended to provide programming tailored to different parts of Agacta.

The principal mainland services were:

- **Regionala North**
    
- **Regionala South**
    
- **Regionala East**
    
- **Regionala Treslio**
    
- **Regionala Stototrip**
    
- **Regionala West**
    

The **Stototrip** service was bilingual.

Regionala West occasionally divided its output further between **West Lake Setro**, serving Setroburg and the surrounding Lake Setro area, and **West Prairies**, serving the more rural prairie provinces of western Agacta.

Regionala TV therefore added a substantial regional component to a television system which had previously been dominated by nationally distributed channels.

## Television in the overseas territories

Terrestrial television reached Agacta's **overseas territories during the 1970s**.

Rather than simply retransmitting the mainland ATV service in its entirety, each territory received its **own version of ATV**. Programming was approximately evenly divided between material produced on the Agactan mainland and locally produced programming, although the exact schedule could vary.

Each overseas territory subsequently received a **second public channel through Regionala TV**, giving the territories their own two-channel public television systems.

From the **mid-1980s**, the other Agactan terrestrial broadcasters also became available in the overseas territories through **cable television**. This allowed viewers to receive the major mainland commercial channels in addition to their locally oriented public services.

## The mature analogue system

By the late 1980s, mainland Agacta had developed a relatively diverse terrestrial television system consisting of:

- two national ATV channels;
    
- three national commercial broadcasters, AKD, Sutro and Star Network; and
    
- Regionala TV's network of regional public channels.
    

The overseas territories operated their own ATV and Regionala services, while mainland commercial television was increasingly available there through cable.

This basic **analogue terrestrial structure remained largely unchanged until the introduction of digital television**, which brought a much larger expansion in the number of available channels and fundamentally changed the Agactan broadcasting landscape.
