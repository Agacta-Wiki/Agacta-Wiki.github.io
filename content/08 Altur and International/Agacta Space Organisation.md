# Agacta Space Organisation

The **Agacta Space Organisation (ASO)** is Agacta's national space organisation. Founded by the government in **1928**, it oversees the country's space activities, including domestic and imported space programmes, and forms an important part of Agactan national identity.

ASO's operational focus is the **Vunter system**, the solar system containing Altur. Its programme centres on scientific research and the development and maintenance of space infrastructure. Most Alturian nations maintain their own space programmes, with international cooperation taking place alongside national activity.

## Technology and scope

Altur's spaceflight capabilities are substantially more advanced than Earth's, although much of its wider technology remains broadly comparable. Crewed spacecraft operate at speeds of approximately **100–125 kilometres per second**. These capabilities support extended expeditions within the Vunter system, but operational interstellar travel remains beyond the programme's scope.

Spacecraft have no warp drives or artificial-gravity generators. Gravity aboard crewed expedition vessels is provided through rotation.

The programme's character is that of long-duration scientific expeditions: missions have defined objectives, require substantial preparation, and involve prolonged periods away from home.

## Centrifugal gravity

**Centrifugal gravity is standard aboard ASO's crewed expedition ships**, providing gravity in living accommodation throughout their missions. Rotating sections allow crews to spend significant portions of their deployment in a gravity environment.

This does not require every compartment to rotate. Other parts of a vessel can remain weightless, including areas where that environment is useful for scientific or technical work.

The standard applies to crewed expedition vessels; it does not imply that every small shuttle, landing craft or uncrewed vehicle must carry rotating accommodation. Detailed spacecraft layouts, rotation rates and gravity levels have not yet been established.

## Typical missions

Most ASO scientific expeditions last approximately **six months to two years**. Their work takes place within the Vunter system and includes research involving planets, moons and asteroids.

The wider programme encompasses stations, telescopes, habitats, fuel depots and scientific bases. Its activities combine exploration with the infrastructure needed to sustain a continuing presence beyond Altur.

Mission duration depends on the destination, the work involved and the journey home. Travel times vary with planetary positions, route selection, acceleration and braking. The six-month-to-two-year range describes typical expeditions rather than a limit applying to every mission.

## Aquatrip launch centre

Agacta benefits from a major space launch centre in its tropical territory of **Aquatrip**. The centre supports Agacta's own programme and is also regularly used by other nations.

It is particularly valuable to allied **Siva nations without tropical territory or a suitable launch centre near the equator**. Access to Aquatrip allows these countries to use Agacta's launch facilities for their own space activities.

**Sensin, Sudo, Skiburg and Avarka Major** have all been known to use the centre. Aquatrip consequently serves both as a national space facility and as a location for international cooperation.

## Specialist outer-system expeditions

Expeditions to distances comparable to Neptune's position in Earth's Solar System are considered **specialist missions**. These involve deployments of approximately **three to four years**, taking personnel to the outer reaches of the Vunter system.

Such missions occur much less frequently than shorter expeditions. Their duration includes the outward journey, operations at the destination and the return to Altur.

### Selection and compensation

Personnel undergo **extensive psychological testing** before selection for these deployments. The lengthy separation from home, isolation and communication delays make them particularly demanding assignments.

These missions offer **exceptionally high salaries and substantial pension arrangements**, reflecting the commitment required of their personnel.

### Communications

At outer-system distances, communications with Agacta are subject to long delays. Crews cannot rely on immediate exchanges with home or real-time conversations with personnel on Altur.

### Multinational participation

Specialist outer-system expeditions are typically undertaken by a **combination of nations**. Agacta generally participates alongside its usual allies.

This cooperation distinguishes these comparatively rare deployments from the more frequent missions carried out by individual national programmes.

## Long-term interstellar planning

Altur's governments also cooperate on the distant prospect of travelling beyond the Vunter system. This includes planning for a future in which their sun no longer allows Altur to remain habitable, on a timescale of billions of years.

This is a rare area of broad international cooperation. The expectation is that future generations will develop capabilities far beyond those currently available.

Interstellar planning remains a long-term undertaking. Present-day ASO missions concentrate on scientific work and space activity within the Vunter system.
